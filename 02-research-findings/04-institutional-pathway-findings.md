# **Institutional Pathway and Political Economy for Integrating Ocean Accounts into the Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI)**

## **1\. Executive Summary and Analytical Objective**

The integration of the Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries’ (MMAF/KKP) Ocean Accounts into the Ministry of National Development Planning’s (BAPPENAS) Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI) represents a critical and highly complex intersection of environmental-economic accounting and national development policy. While the technical feasibility of aligning ocean account data—which measures marine ecosystem assets, flows to the economy, and environmental degradation—with IBEI indicators has been established conceptually, the institutional feasibility remains a profound bureaucratic challenge. This challenge is dictated by strict Indonesian statutory mandates, inter-agency political economy, and rigid fiscal mechanics.  
The primary objective of this exhaustive report is to establish precisely what must occur institutionally for ocean account outputs to enter the IBEI. It delivers an honest assessment of whether the involved agencies possess the mandate, the statutory mechanisms, the available calendar windows, and the underlying political-economic incentives to execute this transfer. The analysis distinguishes sharply between technical capability and bureaucratic reality, acknowledging that in the Indonesian public administration system, superior data is frequently rejected if it bypasses statutory hierarchy.  
The core finding of this research indicates that the structural pathway for integration already exists, rendering the creation of new institutions or high-level Memorandums of Understanding (MoUs) redundant and potentially counterproductive. The integration must occur through the *Satu Data Indonesia* (One Data Indonesia) framework established by Presidential Regulation No. 39/20191. However, severe bureaucratic frictions threaten this pathway. Following the inauguration of the *Kabinet Merah Putih* (Red and White Cabinet) in late 2024, institutional priorities have shifted drastically, placing MMAF under a new coordinating ministry focused on food security3. Concurrently, severe budgetary austerity measures introduced via Presidential Instruction (Inpres) No. 1/2025 constrain any initiative requiring new fiscal allocations5. Consequently, the integration is legally permissible but practically constrained by mandate protection, differing donor dependencies, and shifting cabinet priorities. This report delineates the minimum viable pathway to navigate this specific political-economic environment.

## **2\. Background and Conceptual Divergence**

To understand the institutional friction, one must examine the distinct origins, ownership, and methodologies of the two measurement initiatives. They currently reside in separate institutional homes, governed by divergent immediate objectives and supported by different international donor cycles.  
The Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI) is owned entirely by BAPPENAS. Operating within the broader framework of the Blue Economy Roadmap 2023–2045 and the National Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMN), the IBEI was developed with substantial technical and financial support from the European Union through the ARISE+ Indonesia program7. The index is a composite assessment tool designed to monitor the progress of blue economy development across all Indonesian provinces. It relies on three primary pillars—economic, social, and environmental—and utilizes macro-level indicators to generate a standardized score9. BAPPENAS utilizes the IBEI to set national targets; for example, the baseline IBEI score was recorded at 34.25 in 2023, with a formal RPJMN target set to reach 50.73 by the end of 202911.  
Conversely, the Ocean Accounts initiative is led by the Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (MMAF/KKP). Supported by the Global Ocean Accounts Partnership (GOAP)—which MMAF co-chairs—and heavily funded by Norwegian cooperation agreements (Norad) and the UK Blue Planet Fund, the accounts are built upon the UN System of Environmental-Economic Accounting (SEEA)13. The ocean accounts are highly granular, spatial, and localized. For instance, the final pilot for the Sawu Sea Marine Protected Area (KKP Laut Sawu), completed in May 2025, integrated precise ecological data showing a marine GDP of IDR 5.4 trillion alongside the extraction of 379,588 tons of fish and the generation of 1,436 tons of solid marine waste15.  
The conceptual gap is clear: BAPPENAS requires high-level, standardized provincial composites to feed a national index, while MMAF is producing highly granular, localized accounting structures. A prior concept note hypothesized that the missing element was a coordination mechanism, suggesting joint working groups, data-sharing MoUs, or embedding account outputs into the IBEI refresh cycle. This report tests those hypotheses against the reality of Indonesian administrative law.

## **3\. Mandate Architecture: Jurisdictions, Overlaps, and Constraints**

The Indonesian state apparatus operates strictly within defined legal competencies. Inter-agency data flows are governed by hierarchical legal instruments, meaning that the transfer of ocean account data to the IBEI is not merely a matter of technical agreement between directors, but a question of statutory compliance. The formal mandates of BAPPENAS, BPS (Statistics Indonesia), MMAF, and BIG (Geospatial Information Agency) dictate the boundaries of possibility.

### **3.1 BAPPENAS: National Development Planning Authority**

Under Law No. 25/2004 on the National Development Planning System (Sistem Perencanaan Pembangunan Nasional/SPPN), BAPPENAS holds the absolute mandate to formulate long, medium, and short-term development plans, including the RPJMN and the annual Government Work Plan (RKP)16. The IBEI is legally embedded within these planning documents as a key macroeconomic indicator19.  
However, a fundamental limitation exists within BAPPENAS's mandate: it is a consumer, synthesizer, and evaluator of data, not a primary producer of statistical raw materials. Under Indonesian law, BAPPENAS cannot independently generate the statistical inputs required for the IBEI, nor can it bypass the national statistical system. For BAPPENAS to formally utilize data to construct an official national index, that data must be supplied by the relevant line ministries and, crucially, certified by the national statistical authority.

### **3.2 BPS: Official Statistical Sovereignty**

The Central Bureau of Statistics (BPS) derives its sweeping authority from Law No. 16/1997 on Statistics. This legislation categorizes statistics into three strict domains: *Statistik Dasar* (Basic Statistics, which are the exclusive domain of BPS), *Statistik Sektoral* (Sectoral Statistics, produced by line ministries for specific governmental tasks), and *Statistik Khusus* (Special Statistics, produced by non-governmental entities)20.  
BPS's mandate establishes a rigid constraint on how BAPPENAS may construct an index. Ocean accounts compiled by MMAF fall strictly under the category of *Statistik Sektoral*. For this data to be deemed valid for official national reporting (such as feeding the IBEI), BPS must certify it. BPS enforces its sovereignty over methodology through the *Rekomendasi Kegiatan Statistik* (Statistical Activity Recommendation) system, internally known as Romantik, and evaluates line ministries through the *Evaluasi Penyelenggaraan Statistik Sektoral* (EPSS)23. If MMAF compiles ocean accounts using bespoke methodologies that bypass BPS Romantik clearance, BPS will reject the data's official status, and BAPPENAS will be institutionally prohibited from integrating it into the IBEI.

### **3.3 MMAF (KKP): Marine Sectoral Data Production**

The Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries acts as the sectoral regulator and the primary producer of marine data. MMAF established its internal Ocean Accounts drafting team via Ministerial Decree (Kepmen KP) No. 44/202326. MMAF’s mandate includes managing marine conservation areas, fisheries production, and coastal zones. Under its internal *Satu Data Kelautan dan Perikanan* (One Data for Marine and Fisheries) policy outlined in Permen KP 61/2022, MMAF's Data and Information Center (Pusdatin) is designated as the *Walidata* (Data Custodian), while technical directorates act as *Produsen Data* (Data Producers)28.  
While MMAF possesses the clear statutory right to compile the Ocean Accounts (Neraca Sumber Daya Laut/NSDL) to track marine wealth, these outputs remain classified as internal administrative data until they successfully navigate the BPS certification process.

### **3.4 BIG: Geospatial Information Authority**

Because ocean accounts are inherently spatial—requiring the precise mapping of coral reefs, mangroves, seagrasses, and jurisdictional boundaries—the Geospatial Information Agency (BIG) acts as a secondary gatekeeper. BIG operates under Law No. 4/2011 on Geospatial Information. It is the sole authority for base geospatial information (*Informasi Geospasial Dasar*) and serves as the integrator for thematic geospatial information (*Informasi Geospasial Tematik*)30. MMAF cannot finalize the spatial dimensions of its accounts without BIG’s validation and adherence to the One Map Policy (Kebijakan Satu Peta/KSP) regulated under Perpres 23/202131.

### **3.5 Mandate Conflict and Intersection Analysis**

The institutional architecture ensures that no single agency can unilaterally drive the integration of ocean accounts into the IBEI. The process requires sequential deference to differing forms of statutory authority.

| Agency | Statutory Domain | Legal Basis | Overlaps, Conflicts, and Institutional Constraints |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **BAPPENAS** | National Planning & IBEI formulation | UU 25/2004, Perpres 12/2025 | BAPPENAS is a synthesizer. It holds the mandate for the index but is legally barred from utilizing uncertified line-ministry data. |
| **BPS** | Official Statistics & Methodology | UU 16/1997, Peraturan BPS 3/2022 | Holds absolute veto power over official data validity. Must certify MMAF data via EPSS/Romantik before BAPPENAS can adopt it. |
| **MMAF** | Marine Sectoral Data (Ocean Accounts) | Kepmen KP 44/2023, Permen KP 61/2022 | Must adapt internal data collection to meet rigid BPS statistical standards and BIG geospatial standards to achieve external validity. |
| **BIG** | Geospatial Base & Thematic Maps | UU 4/2011, Perpres 23/2021 | The spatial dimensions of ocean accounts must perfectly align with the One Map Policy, creating a secondary bottleneck alongside BPS. |

## **4\. Existing Coordination Mechanisms**

A foundational rule of Indonesian public administration is to exhaust existing regulatory mechanisms before proposing the creation of new institutions. The hypothesis that a new inter-agency coordination body or bilateral MoU is required ignores the comprehensive framework that currently regulates inter-agency data flows.

### **4.1 Satu Data Indonesia (Perpres 39/2019)**

The most critical and legally binding mechanism available is *Satu Data Indonesia* (SDI), established by Presidential Regulation No. 39/2019. SDI was engineered specifically to eliminate data silos, resolve methodological conflicts, and standardize data sharing across Indonesian government agencies1.  
SDI operates on strict principles: adherence to unified data standards, comprehensive metadata, data interoperability, and the use of reference codes31. More importantly, it establishes a rigid, non-negotiable governance architecture:

* **Dewan Pengarah (Steering Committee):** Chaired by BAPPENAS, providing the planning agency with oversight of the entire ecosystem.  
* **Pembina Data (Data Patrons):** BPS serves as the *Pembina Data Statistik*, while BIG serves as the *Pembina Data Geospasial*. They set the rules and validate submissions.  
* **Walidata (Data Custodians):** The designated data centers of each line ministry (e.g., Pusdatin within MMAF).  
* **Produsen Data (Data Producers):** The technical directorates within the ministries that actually gather the data (e.g., the Directorate General of Marine Space Management at MMAF).

Under Perpres 39/2019, ocean accounts unequivocally qualify as shareable sectoral data. The regulation obliges MMAF (*Walidata*) to submit the NSDL metadata and methodology to BPS (*Pembina Data*). Once BPS verifies that the data meets statistical standards, it is published on the SDI Portal. BAPPENAS, functioning as the *Pengguna Data* (Data User), can then seamlessly and legally pull this certified data to feed the IBEI. This statutory mechanism renders bilateral MoUs obsolete for standard data transfers, provided the SDI protocols are rigorously followed2.

### **4.2 Existing MoUs and Bilateral Agreements**

While BPS has a long history of utilizing Memorandums of Understanding for data sharing—evidenced by a recent 2025 MoU with the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) and a 2025 data-sharing agreement with the Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency (BMKG)—these instruments are often more symbolic than functional within domestic inter-ministry operations37. An MoU that exists on paper does not bypass the statutory requirements of the SDI portal. If a BAPPENAS-MMAF MoU were signed, the actual data transfer would still be legally required to route through BPS validation.

### **4.3 Multi-Ministerial Ocean Accounts Task Force**

MMAF successfully established an Ocean Accounts drafting team via Kepmen KP 44/2023, which functions as a multi-ministerial task force including representatives from BPS, BIG, the Ministry of Finance, and the Rekam Nusantara Foundation15. This task force is highly operational and has delivered concrete outputs, such as the Sawu Sea pilot. However, BAPPENAS is notably absent from the core, day-to-step operational decrees of this specific task force40. Furthermore, while the task force is effective for technical compilation, it is an ad-hoc body that lacks the statutory authority to mandate the inclusion of its outputs into BAPPENAS’s national development indices.

### **4.4 Coordination Mechanism Inventory**

| Candidate Mechanism | Governing Instrument | Current Operating Status | Sufficiency for Ocean Account/IBEI Transfer |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Satu Data Indonesia (SDI)** | Perpres 39/2019 | Fully operational, portal active. | **Sufficient.** It provides the legal, technical, and structural pathway for the data transfer without requiring any new amendments. |
| **Ocean Accounts Task Force** | Kepmen KP 44/2023 | Operating; delivered Sawu pilot. | **Insufficient.** Excellent for compilation, but lacks the authority to mandate BAPPENAS to use the outputs. |
| **Bilateral MoUs** | Discretionary | Various exist; highly dependent on leadership. | **Insufficient.** MoUs do not override the legal requirement for BPS sectoral statistical validation under UU 16/1997. |

## **5\. Policy Calendar and Institutional Decision Windows**

The feasibility of integration is profoundly dictated by the rigid national planning and budgetary cycles. In the Indonesian bureaucracy, proposing an integration at an incorrect point in the calendar guarantees failure, as budgets (DIPA) and Key Performance Indicators (IKU) cannot be retroactively altered to accommodate ad-hoc requests.

### **5.1 RPJMN 2025–2029 and the Annual RKP Process**

The current medium-term development plan, the RPJMN 2025–2029, was promulgated via Presidential Regulation No. 12/2025 in February 2025, cementing the foundation for the "Indonesia Emas 2045" vision41. The IBEI is explicitly codified in the RPJMN matrix as a macroeconomic target. The document sets a baseline score of 34.25 (as of 2023\) and establishes a rigid trajectory aiming for 50.73 by 202910. Because the RPJMN is now locked in law, modifying the core structure of the IBEI at the mid-term level is impossible until the formal mid-term review in 2027\.  
However, the Annual Government Work Plan (Rencana Kerja Pemerintah \- RKP) provides a viable entry point. The RKP is drafted between February and May of the preceding year. To embed ocean account indicators into the IBEI's annual reporting framework for the 2027 fiscal year, the methodological alignment between MMAF and BAPPENAS must be completed, and BPS certification must be secured, no later than **March 2026**.

### **5.2 BPS Statistical Work Programme (Sensus Ekonomi 2026\)**

BPS operates on a strict national calendar that heavily dictates its capacity to process new sectoral statistics. Currently, BPS is executing the highly resource-intensive *Sensus Ekonomi 2026* (Economic Census), which runs intense field operations from June through August 202642. During this period, BPS's capacity to review new sectoral statistics proposals via the Romantik application from line ministries will be severely constrained. Therefore, MMAF must time its submissions meticulously. Proposals for validating new Ocean Account methodologies should ideally be submitted in **Q4 2026** (October–December), aligning with the post-census cooldown and BPS's annual EPSS evaluations.

### **5.3 IBEI Technical Refresh and MMAF Renstra**

Refinements to the IBEI indicators are managed internally by BAPPENAS (Deputy for Maritime Affairs and Natural Resources) during scheduled technical review windows. The integration of ocean accounts—which offer superior spatial resolution and monetary valuation of marine ecosystems compared to current proxy indicators—would likely take the form of an annex or a sub-indicator swap during a Q4 technical review. Meanwhile, MMAF's Strategic Plan (*Renstra*) cycles require that any continuous data collection effort be formally inscribed into the ministry’s performance metrics to secure recurring budget lines.

### **5.4 Calendar Entry Points Summary**

| Policy Cycle | Governing Agency | Nature of Decision Window | Absolute Deadline for Influence |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **RPJMN Mid-Term Review** | BAPPENAS | Revision of national targets | Q2 2027 |
| **Annual RKP (2027)** | BAPPENAS | Setting annual operational targets | March 2026 |
| **IBEI Technical Refresh** | BAPPENAS | Methodological adjustments | November 2026 |
| **BPS Sectoral Validation** | BPS | Romantik/EPSS clearance | Post-Sensus Ekonomi (Oct 2026\) |
| **MMAF Ocean Accounts Publication** | MMAF | Release of pilot data (Sawu Sea) | Completed (July 2025\)15 |

## **6\. Precedents for Domestic Cross-Agency Indicator Sourcing**

International precedents carry very little weight in the Indonesian bureaucracy compared to proven domestic models. Analyzing past domestic successes and failures in cross-agency data sourcing provides the most accurate blueprint for the IBEI-Ocean Accounts integration.

### **6.1 The Success Blueprint: Indeks Kualitas Lingkungan Hidup (IKLH)**

The most direct, persuasive, and structurally identical precedent is the Environmental Quality Index (IKLH), managed by the Ministry of Environment and Forestry (KLHK). The IKLH is a composite index comprising the Water Quality Index, Air Quality Index, Land Cover Quality Index, and the Seawater Quality Index (IKAL)44.  
**The Mechanism:** KLHK acts as the *Produsen Data*, maintaining the physical monitoring stations, conducting the sampling, and compiling the raw data. BPS acts as the *Pembina*, verifying the statistical methodology and sampling validity. Once certified, the resulting index is officially adopted by BAPPENAS as a headline environmental indicator in the RPJMN, driving policy across multiple sectors47.  
**Transferable Lesson:** The integration succeeded because KLHK assumed the full financial burden of data collection, adhered strictly to BPS standards from the outset, and allowed BAPPENAS to consume the final composite figure without demanding BAPPENAS to process the raw data. MMAF must replicate this exact behavior. MMAF must present the Ocean Accounts not as raw spatial spreadsheets requiring interpretation, but as a BPS-certified composite indicator that BAPPENAS can seamlessly ingest.

### **6.2 The Fragile Blueprint: Low Carbon Development Initiative (LCDI)**

The LCDI, spearheaded by BAPPENAS, successfully integrated greenhouse gas emissions and environmental carrying capacity into macroeconomic planning documents. **The Mechanism:** The initiative relied heavily on BAPPENAS's high-level convening power and substantial donor support (UNDP, UKCCU) to fund the complex inter-agency modeling required49. **Transferable Lesson:** While highly successful on paper, the LCDI model suffers from severe sustainability risks. Once external donor funding recedes, line ministries often lack the APBN flexibility to maintain the complex, bespoke data collection required by BAPPENAS. If the IBEI-Ocean Accounts linkage relies entirely on external donor funding (ARISE+ or GOAP) to facilitate the technical transfer, it will face the same institutional fragility.

## **7\. Friction and Incentive Assessment (Political Economy)**

Institutional feasibility is ultimately determined by political economy—the alignment of bureaucratic incentives, budget realities, and institutional self-interest. An honest assessment reveals critical frictions that present formidable barriers to integration.

### **7.1 Cabinet Restructuring and Political Salience**

Following the inauguration of President Prabowo Subianto's *Kabinet Merah Putih* in October 2024, the structure of coordinating ministries was radically altered via Presidential Regulation Nos. 139 and 140/20244. Previously, MMAF and BAPPENAS operated under relatively aligned economic coordinating structures. Post-transition, MMAF (KKP) has been placed under the newly created **Coordinating Ministry for Food Affairs (Kemenko Pangan)**, alongside the Ministry of Agriculture and KLHK4. Concurrently, the Ministry of Finance was elevated to report directly to the President4.  
**Severity: High.** This restructuring fundamentally shifts MMAF's primary political directive toward food security (*swasembada pangan*), fisheries production, and downstreaming, rather than abstract macroeconomic or environmental indices. BAPPENAS and MMAF now answer to different overarching political priorities. This reduces the organic, inter-ministerial incentive for bilateral cooperation on the IBEI. Unless ocean accounts are explicitly framed and marketed to MMAF leadership as a tool for measuring marine food security and aquaculture carrying capacity, the political will to push data to BAPPENAS will evaporate.

### **7.2 Budgetary Austerity (Inpres 1/2025)**

In January 2025, the President issued Instruction (Inpres) No. 1/2025 on Expenditure Efficiency, mandating severe cuts to non-essential spending, travel, honorariums, and cross-agency coordination meetings across all ministries, initially targeting Rp 306 trillion in efficiencies5. While the Ministry of Finance subsequently unblocked Rp 86.6 trillion specifically for priority programs of the new cabinet, the fiscal space for bureaucratic innovation remains extremely tight53.  
**Severity: Critical.** Neither BAPPENAS nor MMAF has the discretionary APBN budget to fund a new joint working group, hire consultants, or expand data collection beyond what is already firmly programmed. Any integration effort must cost near-zero to the APBN, relying entirely on existing statutory meetings (such as the mandatory *Forum Satu Data*) or utilizing continuous donor support to subsidize the technical integration.

### **7.3 Donor Dependency and Cycle Mismatch**

The IBEI was developed with deep financial and technical support from the EU-funded ARISE+ program7. Conversely, the Ocean Accounts are heavily supported by the Global Ocean Accounts Partnership (GOAP), drawing on funding from Norwegian cooperation (Norad) and the UK Blue Planet Fund13.  
**Severity: Medium.** Differing donor requirements, logical frameworks, reporting metrics, and funding cycles naturally pull the two initiatives apart. If ARISE+ funding concludes, BAPPENAS may lack the internal technical capacity to regularly update the complex IBEI methodologies. If GOAP funding ceases, MMAF may halt the expansion of ocean accounts to new provinces. The integration must be institutionalized into the APBN baseline before these respective donor cliffs are reached.

### **7.4 Mandate Protection and Attribution**

Bureaucratic agencies in Indonesia highly value the visibility, branding, and ownership of their flagship products. BAPPENAS has heavily promoted the IBEI as its proprietary, hallmark tool for measuring the blue economy. MMAF views the Ocean Accounts as its signature contribution to sustainable global marine governance.  
**Severity: Medium.** If integrating ocean accounts into the IBEI means that MMAF's branding is entirely subsumed and effectively erased by BAPPENAS, MMAF will subtly resist providing the data, citing technical delays. To succeed, the integration must be structured so that both agencies retain visible credit—for example, by publishing the Ocean Accounts as a distinct, prominently branded technical annex to the main IBEI report.

## **8\. The Multilateral Lever: GOAP and the Bogor Strategic Action Plan**

Multilateral commitments can occasionally force domestic institutional alignment by generating normative pressure. MMAF serves as a co-chair of the Global Ocean Accounts Partnership (GOAP) and has consistently positioned Indonesia as a global leader in ocean accounting39.  
In March and April 2026, Indonesia hosted the South-South Ocean Accounting Exchange, which culminated in the drafting of the **Bogor Strategic Action Plan**55. This document serves as a practical roadmap for scaling ocean accounts across the Global South. Two action areas are highly relevant:

* *Action Area 1 (Closing the Implementation Gap)* specifically demands moving beyond baseline data collection toward structured statistical accounts utilizing national data coordination mechanisms55.  
* *Action Area 3 (Strengthening Governance)* explicitly calls for embedding ocean accounts into national planning frameworks, a directive that perfectly describes the relationship between the accounts, the IBEI, and the RPJMN55.

**Assessment of Leverage:** While the Bogor Strategic Action Plan creates substantial diplomatic visibility and intense normative pressure, it is not a legally binding treaty. Its leverage is entirely reputational. MMAF is strongly incentivized to demonstrate to GOAP and its international peers that its accounts are actively being utilized in top-level national policy, rather than sitting idle as academic exercises. Concurrently, BAPPENAS can leverage this multilateral momentum to access GOAP technical assistance for the IBEI. The Bogor Plan acts as a powerful narrative lever to convince Echelon I and II officials to support the integration, but it does not bypass the strict legal requirements of BPS validation and *Satu Data Indonesia*.

## **9\. Minimum Viable Pathway Options**

Given the restrictive budgetary environment driven by Inpres 1/2025 and the strict legal requirements of the statistical system governed by UU 16/1997, proposing a grand new inter-ministerial council is a guaranteed failure. The solution requires identifying the absolute smallest institutional step that produces a tangible, legally sound result.

### **9.2 Minimum Viable Step Recommendation**

The most feasible, legally compliant, and lowest-cost pathway is **Option 1: SDI Metadata Registration**.  
This is the minimum viable step because it requires no new institutions, necessitates no new budget allocations, and avoids the political theater of drafting new MoUs. It utilizes the exact statutory mechanism engineered for this specific purpose. The execution follows a clear, sequential logic:

> 1. MMAF’s Pusdatin, acting as the legally mandated *Walidata*, submits the finalized methodology of a completed ocean account pilot (such as the Sawu Sea MPA pilot finalized in May 202515) directly to BPS, the *Pembina Data Statistik*.  
> 2. BPS evaluates the methodological submission via the established Romantik system.  
> 3. Upon BPS approval, the ocean account indicators are assigned a standardized reference code and are formally published on the *Satu Data Indonesia* portal (data.go.id)2.  
> 4. BAPPENAS, without needing to negotiate directly with MMAF leadership, legally and seamlessly extracts this certified data from the SDI portal to update the environmental pillars of the IBEI.

This approach respects BPS’s absolute authority over official statistics, fulfills MMAF’s legal obligations under Perpres 39/2019, and provides BAPPENAS with unassailable, legally certified data for national planning.

## **10\. Failure Mode Analysis and Mitigations**

Even the most legally sound and cost-effective pathway is highly vulnerable to bureaucratic entropy and shifting political winds. The following realistic failure modes must be anticipated and actively mitigated:

> 1. **BPS Rejection on Quality Grounds (The Methodological Trap)**  
   * *The Risk:* Ocean accounts utilize complex environmental-economic accounting (SEEA) standards that intricately blend geospatial mapping with monetary economic valuation. BPS may outright reject MMAF's submission if the sampling methodology, or the geospatial delineations (which require prior BIG validation), do not align with strict national statistical conventions.  
   * *The Mitigation:* MMAF must embed BPS statisticians directly into the Ocean Accounts technical drafting teams *before* data collection begins, rather than handing BPS a finished, inflexible product for post-hoc validation. The IKLH precedent proves that this co-creation model prevents downstream rejection.  
> 2. **The Donor Cliff**  
   * *The Risk:* The integration occurs successfully in 2026 while GOAP and ARISE+ funding is active and supporting the heavy technical lifting. By 2028, donor funding concludes. Due to the lingering effects of Inpres 1/2025 austerity, neither MMAF nor BAPPENAS allocates the necessary internal APBN funds to maintain the complex data collection. Consequently, the indicators are quietly dropped from the subsequent IBEI refresh.  
   * *The Mitigation:* The ocean accounts must be translated into standardized *Indikator Kinerja Utama* (Main Performance Indicators \- IKU) for MMAF’s Echelon II officials. In the Indonesian bureaucracy, once an indicator becomes a formalized IKU, it becomes a mandatory APBN line item under the Performance Accountability System (SAKIP)56, effectively immunizing it against donor funding cliffs.  
> 3. **The Orphaned MoU**  
   * *The Risk:* Under pressure to demonstrate multilateral progress following the Bogor Strategic Action Plan, BAPPENAS and MMAF leaders sign a high-level MoU to share data. The ministers secure the diplomatic victory, but no technical directives or standard operating procedures are issued to the Walidata (Pusdatin) levels. The data flow never materializes.  
   * *The Mitigation:* Bureaucrats must abandon the pursuit of a bilateral MoU. Strategic focus must remain entirely on pushing the data through the impersonal, legally binding *Satu Data Indonesia* portal workflow. A system-level integration (SDI) is significantly more resilient to leadership changes and attention deficits than a personality-driven MoU.

## **11\. Assessment of Data Sources and Evidence Reliability**

The analysis within this report relies heavily on the examination of primary Indonesian statutory instruments, official government reporting, and verified outputs from multilateral initiatives. The integration of this evidence highlights the absolute necessity of understanding the domestic legal framework over international theoretical best practices.

* **Statutory Instruments (Tier 1 Reliability):** The foundational arguments regarding institutional constraints are drawn directly from active Indonesian law. This includes Law 16/1997 on Statistics, Law 25/2004 on National Development Planning, and Presidential Regulation 39/2019 on *Satu Data Indonesia*. These documents (accessed in their original Bahasa Indonesia) provide the unyielding boundaries of inter-agency cooperation. The recent cabinet restructuring was analyzed using Perpres 139 and 140/2024, providing highly reliable, current data on the shift of MMAF to the Coordinating Ministry for Food Affairs4. Additionally, the fiscal constraints analyzed stem directly from the text of Inpres 1/20255.  
* **Agency Performance Reports (Tier 1 Reliability):** Evaluations of data capabilities and current integration states rely on MMAF’s SAKIP reports (e.g., Ditjen PKRL performance reports from 2023-2025) and BPS metadata catalogs (*Sirusa*)28. These are official state accountability documents, rendering them highly reliable indicators of bureaucratic reality and actual performance against IKUs.  
* **Multilateral and Donor Documentation (Tier 2 Reliability):** Insights into the IBEI methodologies and Ocean Accounts progress utilize reports from ARISE+ Indonesia, BAPPENAS planning documents, and the Global Ocean Accounts Partnership. The details of the Bogor Strategic Action Plan (March/April 2026\) were derived directly from GOAP publications54. While highly reliable regarding programmatic goals, donor documents often exhibit optimism bias regarding institutional sustainability, requiring cross-verification against domestic APBN realities.

## **12\. Conclusion**

The institutional window for integrating ocean accounts into the Indonesia Blue Economy Index is narrow, fraught with bureaucratic friction, but ultimately navigable. The transition to the *Kabinet Merah Putih* has misaligned the immediate political priorities of MMAF and BAPPENAS, placing the former squarely in the realm of food security while the latter pursues broader macroeconomic transformation. Concurrently, the severe austerity measures dictated by *Inpres 1/2025* guarantee that any integration proposal requiring new inter-ministerial task forces, dedicated coordination budgets, or bespoke data-sharing platforms will fail upon arrival.  
However, the legal architecture—specifically Law 16/1997 on Statistics and Presidential Regulation 39/2019 on *Satu Data Indonesia*—provides a ready-made, highly structured, and frictionless conduit. By abandoning the pursuit of symbolic MoUs and focusing exclusively on pushing MMAF’s completed pilot data (such as the robust ecological and economic data generated by the Sawu Sea accounts) through BPS certification and onto the SDI portal, BAPPENAS can legally and seamlessly harvest this data for the IBEI. This minimum viable step satisfies domestic legal requirements, circumvents inter-agency turf wars, protects institutional mandates, and practically fulfills Indonesia's multilateral commitments under the Bogor Strategic Action Plan.
