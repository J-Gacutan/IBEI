1	# Ocean accounts as compilation infrastructure for a blue economy index
2	
3	## Abstract
4	
5	Indonesia is statutorily obliged to score every province annually on a 44-indicator blue economy
6	composite that, on its own registration, conducts no primary collection and compiles entirely from
7	published sectoral statistics. This study assesses Indonesia's ocean accounts as compilation
8	infrastructure for that index rather than as an alternative indicator source. Two published
9	specifications are read against each other: the index register as printed in its 2025 technical
10	handbook, and the account structures of the SEEA Ecosystem Accounting standard. Each of the register's
11	44 rows is scored against the verbatim constituent data items that define it, with every claim
12	maturity-tagged. Reading data items rather than indicator names corrects prior assessments. Three
13	condition indicators ask for the share of provincial area within a stated cover band, a tabulation of
14	extent by class rather than the index against reference condition earlier work found missing. Five rows
15	are the whole of what an account touches, carrying 7.64% of the composite's published weight, of which
16	about 2.33% is the share an account would itself supply. What separates the account routes into and out
17	of reach is content rather than compilation maturity: routes compiled at pilot scale bear on 12.79% and
18	the one route compiled nowhere on 13.70%, yet no compiled account produces the data item the register
19	asks for at any of them. The figure 12.79% is not independent of 7.64% but wholly contains it. Every
20	share measures what these account types bear on rather than what an account would supply, and every
21	share is nominal published weight, which under principal-component weighting need not track effective
22	importance — a test that cannot be run because the provincial variable matrix is unpublished. What accounting offers such an index is a production process — a standing
23	classification, a balancing identity, a re-aggregable spatial basis and a documented revision rule —
24	rather than a stock of substitutable values. What an accounting
25	system can carry for a statutory composite is now a general question.
26	
27	
28	# 1. Introduction
29	
30	Composite indices are a standard instrument of ocean governance. They convert heterogeneous marine
31	statistics into a single number that ministries can target, budget against and report. Their
32	credibility rests less on indicator design than on the production system beneath them, because an
33	index can only be as traceable as the statistics it consumes. That dependency is rarely examined.
34	
35	The form is well established. The Ocean Health Index scores every coastal country against ten goals
36	for a coupled human–ocean system (Halpern et al., 2012). The methodological difficulties are
37	documented rather than novel: the standard reference treats imputation, weighting and aggregation as
38	the three choices that most move a published ranking (Nardo et al., 2008). The Indonesian case
39	therefore belongs to a recognised class of measurement problem.
40	
41	Ocean accounting has developed as the candidate infrastructure for that problem. The System of
42	Environmental-Economic Accounting Ecosystem Accounting framework was adopted by the UN Statistical
43	Commission in 2021 as an international statistical standard. It supplies the extent, condition,
44	service and asset structures on which such accounts are built (United Nations et al., 2024), and a
45	complete national sequence has been specified in detail (IDEEA Group, 2021). Ocean accounts have been
46	argued to belong in the national statistical apparatus rather than in analysis, on the grounds that
47	recurrent policy reporting requires a production system and not a study (Fenichel et al., 2020).
48	Sub-national compilation across extent, service flows and ocean-economy production has been
49	demonstrated operationally (Gacutan et al., 2022). In Indonesia, the spatial basis such accounts
50	require has been tested empirically: grid size and designation rule change the extent estimate
51	materially (Rahayu et al., 2024). None of that work establishes whether an account compiled to those
52	specifications can carry the reporting load of a statutory index. That is a question about production
53	capacity, not about accounting method.
54	
55	Indonesia is where the policy demand is most explicit. Its ocean policy lineage runs from the 2017
56	Indonesian Ocean Policy through a development framework (BAPPENAS, 2021) to a blue economy roadmap
57	(BAPPENAS, 2024). A recent review of that lineage names limited data support among the obstacles to
58	implementation (Wuwung et al., 2024). Quantitative work on Indonesian blue economy indicators remains
59	thin. Rinaldi et al. (2025) construct their own sub-national index for Sumatera on a human development
60	index template and regress it on regional product. That is the closest existing treatment, and it is
61	not an analysis of the official index. The production system beneath the official index has not been
62	examined.
63	
64	The Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI) is an official national development indicator under
65	presidential regulation Perpres 12/2025. Provincial targets for 2045 are assigned by joint circular of
66	the Ministry of Home Affairs and the Ministry of National Development Planning (BAPPENAS) for carriage
67	in long-term development plans. A province cannot decline to be scored, and BAPPENAS cannot decline to
68	score it. Against that recurring obligation, the compilation activity is registered in Indonesia's
69	statistical activity system as sectoral statistics gathered by secondary collection from the
70	publications of the Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (MMAF, Indonesian KKP) and of Statistics
71	Indonesia (BPS) (SIRUSA kegiatan 94936). The index conducts no primary collection of its own. It
72	inherits whatever those agencies publish, at whatever resolution they publish it. Section 2 documents
73	the traceability defects that follow.
74	
75	This study assesses Indonesia's ocean accounts as compilation infrastructure for an index the state is
76	already obliged to produce, rather than as an alternative source of indicator values. The assessment
77	is built on the IBEI's own published documentation, on the primary reports of the Indonesian account
78	pilots, and on the international accounting standard, with every claim tagged for maturity. It
79	contributes four things: a documented account of what makes provincial compilation hard, a three-tier
80	indicator mapping scored against the register's verbatim constituent data items rather than its
81	indicator names, a quantification of where in the index the account-relevant weight sits, and a
82	register of the conditions precedent for continued supply.
83	
84	Two boundaries hold throughout. Account compilation has its own statutory and analytical purposes
85	under MMAF's mandate, and supplying an index is one downstream use among several. Nothing here
86	proposes replacing the IBEI or subordinating either framework to the other; the relationship examined
87	is statistical infrastructure feeding a policy instrument. The object of study is bounded to the
88	44-indicator edition of the index, and every Indonesian ocean account output discussed carries maturity
89	tag (b), experimental or pilot compilation, with no statistical work-programme entry and no revision
90	policy.
91	
92	Three findings follow. First, read against verbatim constituent data items rather than indicator
93	names, the three ecosystem condition indicators are definitionally matched to account output rather
94	than category-mismatched. That corrects a judgement in earlier work, including a provisional mapping by
95	the present authors that scored account-derivable shares at figures moving from 61% to 39% to 2.8% as
96	the assumed indicator set changed, the last over a 36-indicator denominator the register does not have
97	(Section 4.2). Second, what separates the account routes into and out of reach is not compilation
98	maturity but content. The routes Indonesia has compiled at pilot scale bear on 12.79% of the
99	composite's published weight and the one route compiled nowhere bears on 13.70%, a difference of
100	1.07 times; yet no compiled account produces the constituent data item the register asks for at
101	any of these rows (Section 4.3). Third, the share an account would itself supply is about 2.33%, and
102	what the mature account types offer at those rows is a production process rather than a stock of
103	substitutable values (Section 5).

106	# 2. The compilation problem
107	
108	A recurring statutory obligation to score every province annually sits on top of a compilation
109	activity that conducts no primary collection of its own. The obligation is assigned rather than
110	volunteered. Perpres 12/2025 carries the IBEI as an official national development indicator, and
111	joint circular SEB Mendagri/Bappenas No. 2/2024 distributes a 2045 target to each province for
112	carriage in its long-term development plan (Juknis, Tabel 2, p. 69 [pdf 79]). That the compilation
113	beneath it collects nothing directly is a design decision recorded in Indonesia's own statistical
114	metadata system, and its consequences are legible as specific strains in the published output. This
115	section establishes both from the IBEI's own documentation, and closes by declaring the pillar
116	structure the rest of the article computes on, since the handbook prints two.
117	
118	## 2.1 Compilation without collection
119	
120	SIRUSA registers the IBEI compilation as *Kompilasi Produk Administrasi* under *Statistik Sektoral*,
121	implemented by BAPPENAS's Directorate of Marine Affairs and Fisheries, with an empty *Identitas
122	Rekomendasi* (kegiatan 94936, retrieved 2026-08-13). The design is secondary compilation throughout.
123	Collection mode is *Pengumpulan data sekunder*, the instrument is *Lainnya: Publikasi KKP dan BPS*,
124	and the unit is the province. The register records no pilot survey, no non-response adjustment, no
125	staff training, zero supervisors and zero enumerators. The index's supply is therefore fixed to
126	whatever KKP and BPS have already published, at the resolution and vintage they published it.
127	
128	The collection capability the index draws on is real, and it sits with those two producers. That is
129	the statutory division this article holds to: BAPPENAS plans, whilst MMAF and BPS produce
130	statistics. What the register establishes is not an absence of capability in the system. It is the
131	absence of any collection instrument inside the compilation itself.
132	
133	The reporting window is narrow and is published from two directions. SIRUSA schedules collection
134	from 1 April to 1 May 2025 and dissemination from 1 August to 1 September 2025. The Juknis puts the
135	first phase of its monitoring and evaluation cycle in October to December of year *n* (p. 71 [pdf
136	81]). Read together, these imply that an upstream figure not public by roughly April of year *n+1*
137	cannot enter that cycle's index. Neither document reconciles them, and only the first phase was
138	extracted into the corpus, so the implication is stated conditionally `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Figure 1
139	draws the two calendars against the thirteen-step production chain they feed, and marks the single
140	step at which account-derived data could enter.

142	## 2.2 What the published output records
143	
144	Two defects carry the argument, and both are failures of provenance rather than of arithmetic.
145	
146	Gaps are filled by prediction, and the prediction is not recorded. Annex 1 of the Report specifies a
147	machine-learning framework predicting missing values from socio-economic proxies under two scenarios,
148	with SUSENAS named as the proxy source (pp. 64–66 [pdf 76–78]). The framework itself is specified in
149	detail. What is missing sits downstream of it: the annex names no imputed indicator, no count of
150	imputed cells, no selected model, no achieved error, and no proxy beyond SUSENAS. Under the first
151	scenario an indicator absent in every province is predicted for every province. No reader of any
152	published table can therefore distinguish a measured provincial value from a predicted one
153	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Which indicators were imputed must not be inferred from which look hard to collect.
154	
155	Three provinces record no value across three sub-pillars. Papua Barat Daya, Papua Selatan and Papua
156	Tengah each record 0.00 for marine-based industry, technology and governance, and Papua Tengah's
157	headline value of 8.51 is the lowest in the annex (Report p. 70 [pdf 82]). All three were created in
158	the 2022 provincial split. Juknis Tabel 2 carries the same three provinces at 2023 baselines of 49.25,
159	33.50 and 21.48 against 2025 achievements of 13.16, 12.25 and 8.51 (p. 69 [pdf 79]), and the two
160	documents head those columns differently. Whether the zeros reflect unavailability or a measured zero
161	is not stated in either document. They are reported as printed, and read as consistent with a
162	compilation discontinuity rather than as proof of one `[ASSUMPTION]`. This is where a new
163	administrative unit needs a spatially re-aggregable source rather than a republished provincial total.
164	
165	Table 1 records four further symptoms without arguing them here. Each is uninterrogable from the
166	published record.
167	
168	The denominator of the obligation is itself unstable inside the guidance that creates it. Indonesia
169	has 38 provinces. The Report's Annex 2 tabulates 37, Juknis Tabel 2 lists 38 rows plus a national
170	row with Papua Pegunungan carrying a dash in all three columns, the monitoring chapter asserts
171	comparability over *37 provinsi* (p. 71 [pdf 81]), and the sensitivity chapter thirty pages later
172	recomputes the index *untuk 38 provinsi* across 59 scenarios (p. 101 [pdf 111]). Two of the four are
173	in the same handbook. The number used in any sentence here is the number that sentence is about.

175	## 2.3 The pillar conflict, and the locus this article adopts
176	
177	The handbook prints two pillar structures. Tabel 1 prints four, with an *Enabler (teknologi & tata
178	kelola)* pillar holding indicators 15 to 18. Tabel 4, Tabel 5 and Gambar 11 print three, placing
179	tourism, technology and governance as economy sub-pillars. This article does not resolve the conflict.
180	All weight arithmetic here follows the three-pillar structure, for the stated reason that weights are
181	published only there, whilst register rows keep their Tabel 1 labels.
182	
183	The choice is material in one place and immaterial in another. Every indicator in the Tier A mapping
184	sits in the environment pillar under both structures, so Section 4.2's arithmetic is unaffected
185	`[ASSUMPTION]`. Section 4.3's figures are computed over precisely the pillars across which indicators

216	# 3. Materials and methods
217	
218	## 3.1 Design
219	
220	The study is a documentary analysis of two published specifications read against each other: the
221	IBEI register as printed by its custodian, and the account structures of the SEEA Ecosystem
222	Accounting standard together with the specified sequence of national ocean accounts. No sampling,
223	statistical inference or hypothesis test is involved, and no result reported here is an estimate
224	with a sampling distribution. The equivalent burden is reproducibility of a scoring rule and of an
225	arithmetic chain, which the deposited materials discharge.
226	
227	## 3.2 What an account has to be, for this test
228	
229	The membership rule below tests register rows against account output, so the account side needs
230	specifying first. Compilation infrastructure, as used here, means a production system that turns
231	primary observation into a statistical output on a repeating cycle, carrying its own spatial unit,
232	provenance chain and revision rule. Four properties do that work. They are this study's synthesis
233	rather than a construct the standard names as such, assembled from provisions it states separately.
234	
235	*A standing unit and classification definition* is fixed by the account types themselves. *A
236	balancing identity* renders an unexplained change a visible residual: closing stock equals opening
237	stock plus additions less reductions, with additions and reductions each split into managed and
238	unmanaged, so that the nature of a change is recorded rather than inferred (United Nations et al.,
239	2024, paras 4.14–4.15, p. 86). *A re-aggregable spatial basis* follows from basic spatial units
240	being "a means of implementing the ecosystem accounting approach rather than … elements in a nested
241	hierarchy" (App. A1.1, para. A1.3), with the reporting geography a compiler-delineated accounting
242	area; provincial and district figures are then two aggregations of one production run rather than
243	two data requests. *Documented vintage and revision* follows from the requirement that an account be
244	consistent over time and across classifications (para. 1.60, p. 18), and that previous estimates be
245	revised where updated imagery or methods permit a reassessment, with the size of the revision
246	recoverable as a difference (para. 4.17, p. 88).
247	
248	Two of the four are properties of a compiled account rather than of a well-run register. Section 5
249	concedes the other two.
250

252	
253	A citation-anchored corpus of 199 chunks was built over the index's own published documentation and
254	is released with this article under the Data availability statement, so every quotation resolves to a
255	numbered record rather than a page

287	## 3.4 Unit of analysis and the membership rule
288	
289	The unit is the register row, of which there are 44. The object tested is the row's constituent data
290	items rather than its title, on the Indonesian wording. Each row was graded on one of three grounds.
291	A row is *matched* where a constituent data item names a quantity that a defined SEEA Ecosystem
292	Accounting output contains. It is *partial* where account output supplies a component of the quantity
293	but not the quantity itself. It is a *provenance question* where account output bears on the row
294	without supplying it. Tier A is the union of the three grades and has five members: indicators 19, 20
295	and 21 matched, 24 partial, and 27 a provenance question. The remaining 39 rows are graded none.
296	
297	The rule was applied item by item, so a row carrying several items takes the highest grade any one
298	attains, which makes the 62 items rather than the 44 rows the tested population. Two decisions were
299	fixed before scoring: that indicator titles are not evidence of what a row requires, titles having
300	produced the predecessor figures this analysis rejects; and that a definitional match carries no
301	claim about whether anything has been compiled. Grading was by the authors against those criteria,
302	not blind, with no second coder and no adjudication protocol. Every row's grade and ground is
303	deposited as Supplementary Table S1.
304	
305	Membership in Tier A is a definitional property. The separate operative test applied to the account
306	side is repeatability into a dated window rather than demonstrated capability, since the obligation
307	is annual and can receive only what a producer has already released at the required vintage.
308	Maturity is tagged on every claim that something exists, as (a) published official statistics, (b)
309	experimental or pilot compilation, (c) methodology under development, or (d) announced but not
310	started. The tag describes the datum rather than the document reporting it.
311	
312	## 3.5 Weight arithmetic and its one ambiguity
313	
314	Weights are taken from the Juknis as printed: pillar weights from Tabel 4, p. 94 [pdf 104];
315	sub-pillar splits from Tabel 5, p. 94 [pdf 104]; variable weights from Tabel 6, pp. 95–96 [pdf
316	105–106]; and the sub-pillar shares of Gambar 11, p. 93 [pdf 103], used as an independent integer
317	cross-check. Shares of the composite are products of those published percentages, carried to two
318	decimal places from one-decimal inputs and reported in prose to one decimal with 'about'. Every
319	weight used here is printed; none is reconstructed. Where a total is built from component shares it
320	is the sum of the rounded components rather than the product of the unrounded inputs, which moves
321	three totals by 0.01 and is stated so either route is reproducible. All weight arithmetic follows
322	the three-pillar structure (Section 2.3).
323	
324	The handbook does not state whether the percentages in Tabel 4 to Tabel 6 are raw
325	first-principal-component loadings or normalised contributions, noting only that *besaran pembobot
326	dihasilkan secara otomatis melalui proses PCA* (p. 93 [pdf 103]). The distinction matters because
327	raw loadings need not sum to anything in particular, so shares built by multiplying them would have
328	no defined denominator. The test applied is to renormalise each printed group to sum to 100 and
329	recompute. It changes almost nothing, for a reason that is a property of the tables rather than a
330	stipulation of this study: all fifteen printed weight groups already sum to 100.0 within one-decimal
331	rounding, none departing by more than a tenth of a point. The largest effect anywhere is 0.03
332	percentage points, moving the four Tier B sub-pillars from 26.15% to 26.12%; 18.45%, 7.64%, 3.58%,
333	2.33% and governance at 5.15% are unchanged, and Gambar 11's printed integers of 26 and 5 agree with
334	both. Fifteen independent groups of raw loadings would not sum to 100 by accident, so the
335	percentages are read here as normalised contributions and the derived shares are carried without a
336	denominator caveat. That inference is this study's reading of the tables and is stated nowhere in
337	the source `[ASSUMPTION]`. The census is deposited as Supplementary Data S2.
338	
339	## 3.6 Reporting conventions
340	
341	Indonesian decimal commas are preserved inside quotation and rendered as points in prose. Quotations
342	are from the Indonesian source text. Three inline flags mark evidential status: `[EVIDENCE GAP]`
343	where a required source was not found, `[ASSUMPTION]` where the argument depends on something
344	unconfirmed, and `[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]` on every share of the composite, carrying one meaning
345	throughout and defined at the head of Section 4.2. It is not a flag about the weights, which are
346	printed. Conflicting sources are reported as conflicts and are not averaged or
347	adjudicated.

349	# 4. Results
350	
351	## 4.1 The compiled record
352	
353	Indonesian ocean accounting has produced one pilot compilation, in one marine protected area, in one
354	province, on one vintage. Every output carries maturity tag (b).
355	
356	The compiled record comprises the Gili Matra Marine Recreational Park pilot off north Lombok, together
357	with one peer-reviewed grid-optimisation study at Saleh Bay, Sumbawa (Rahayu et al., 2024). Both sit in
358	West Nusa Tenggara, so NTB is the single province carrying a documented published compilation located
359	by this study. A national Ocean Accounts Dashboard launched in July 2025 is described by GOAP as publicly available
360	and interactive on a harmonised spatial frame (GOAP country page, retrieved 2026-08-13) — a programme
361	self-statement about a platform, evidencing no compilation beyond the pilot record.
362	
363	The pilot's footprint belongs in its own units, because the smallness is part of the finding: 2,980.84
364	ha on a 25 m by 25 m marine basic spatial unit (MBSU) grid of 48,211 cells, opening in 2015 and
365	closing in 2021, those years chosen on data availability (MMAF, 2022a, pp. 30, 39 [pdf 44, 53]). The two
366	figures do not reconcile. At 625 m² each, 48,211 cells give 3,013.19 ha against the 2,980.84 ha
367	printed, a shortfall of 32.35 ha or 1.1%. That would be consistent with cells clipped at the park
368	boundary, but is nowhere explained, and is recorded as observed rather than resolved `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
369	Against a statutory obligation to score every province annually, this is an area smaller than many
370	single districts, compiled once.
371	
372	Maturity is the programme's own self-description rather than an external verdict. GOAP labels the three
373	2022 MMAF outputs 'preliminary consultation drafts', and BPS lists scaling up to the total Indonesian
374	marine area among its outstanding challenges (Tasriah et al., 2022, slide 9). There is no BPS ocean
375	account release, no entry in the national statistical work programme, no published revision policy, and
376	no published compilation cost per site or per unit. The ecosystem scope is confined to mangrove,
377	seagrass and coral reef.
378	
379	The three account types differ in completeness of the account form, not in the standing of the
380	compilation producing them. Extent is the only type with a verified opening, change and closing series:
381	the pilot publishes a complete asset account, with additions and reductions itemised and an
382	ecosystem-conversion matrix alongside (MMAF, 2022a, tab. 5–6, pp. 52–53 [pdf 66–67]). Condition is
383	compiled in a limited form, as cover-based measurement at Tier 1 to Tier 2 read against documented
384	reference bands rather than as a normalised multi-indicator composite (pp. 31–33 [pdf 45–47]). Valuation
385	is the least developed and may not be described as an ecosystem services account in physical units.
386	What exists is benefit-transferred monetary valuation, rising from IDR 45.24 bn per year in 2015 to IDR
387	64.17 bn in 2021 (pp. 36, 60 [pdf 50, 74]). Benefit transfer is not the exchange-value basis the
388	standard requires, a limit BPS states itself (Tasriah et al., 2022, slide 9). No ecosystem services
389	account in physical units has been compiled in Indonesia. Supplementary S5 reproduces all three.
390	
391	Extent is also where two official series diverge, and the divergence is this article's strongest

394	Geospatial Information Agency's 2020 mapping validated by 2021 field survey. Neither document states
395	its relationship to the other and no reconciliation is published `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. The divergence is
396	not a constant offset: the BPS-to-MMAF ratio moves across ecosystems and across the accounting window

398	coral loss of 12.00 ha against a BPS loss of 18.26 ha. Change is the quantity a balancing identity
399	exists to report, and Section 5.2 interrogates the consequence.

401	## 4.2 Indicators the existing accounts can serve
402	
403	Five of the register's 44 rows are the whole of what an ocean account touches. Indicators 19, 20 and 21
404	name quantities an account's output structure already contains; indicators 24 and 27 sit adjacent to
405	account output and are disposed of rather than claimed (Table 2). Figure 2 gives the assignment for all
406	44 rows with the constituent data items in full.
407	
408	Four qualifications govern every share in this section and the two that follow. Each carries `[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]`: it measures what these account types bear on, never
409	what an account would supply and never an achievable score improvement. Each is a share of nominal
410	published weight, which under this estimator need not track effective importance (Section 5.5). Every economy and
411	social figure — 26.1%, 5.15%, 4.11%, 1.34%, 0.31%, 7.20%, 6.29%, 13.7% — is structure-dependent on the
412	three-pillar locus of Section 2.3, with no four-pillar equivalent in the published weights. And the social pillar's indicators remain BPS outputs throughout, the
413	account contribution being a classification standard rather than a substitute source, so the wider
414	30.5% reading may be used only where it is named as the wider reading.
415	
416	On the compilation axis the concession is complete. None of the five indicator values has been
417	published by any Indonesian account, for any province or any site `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Every capability
418	claim here is a claim about definitional correspondence and a demonstrated input layer, never about an
419	existing output. All five rows sit in the environment pillar under both pillar structures, so the
420	unresolved conflict does not reach this arithmetic.
421	
422	The three condition indicators share one grammatical form. Indicator 19 asks for the *Persentase
423	kawasan terumbu karang dengan tutupan karang hidup antara 50-100% per provinsi*, indicator 20 for the
424	seagrass equivalent, and indicator 21 for *kerapatan tutupan* in mangrove (Juknis, Tabel 1, p. 12 [pdf
425	22]). Each names a percentage of area falling within a stated cover or density band, per province. That
426	is a tabulation of extent by class, not a normalised index against a reference condition. Earlier
427	assessments judged that Indonesia lacks the condition account these indicators require. They do not
428	require one, so the judgement answers a question the index does not pose.
429	
430	What the pilot sets beside that specification is the input layer, not the tabulation. The MBSU extent
431	surface exists, and cover is measured at survey stations: live coral cover of 18.11% in 2016 and live
432	hard coral cover of 16.99% in 2021, which the report does not state to be the same measure and which
433	are therefore not read here as a series, and seagrass cover averaging 32.28% with a station maximum of
434	82.01% (MMAF, 2022a, pp. 40, 45 [pdf 54, 59]). The 2021 coral figure is unreconciled in the source,
435	where the narrative reports 16.99% whilst the report's own tab. 3 on the same page prints 18.36%. The
436	cross-tabulation of extent by cover class is not published. The step from the input layer to indicator
437	19 is one tabulation that has not been performed.
438	
439	The match frays at four edges. Two are definitional. The 50% threshold is the index's own and matches
440	no Indonesian condition class boundary in use, the pilot classifying reef condition on the bands of
441	Giyanto et al. (2017), where moderate runs from 19% to below 35%. Reclassifying a continuous per-cell
442	cover surface at 50% would be trivial, but only where condition is mapped wall to wall rather than
443	surveyed at stations, and no obtained document evidences a wall-to-wall condition surface
444	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Where national data exist only as classes pre-binned on the Giyanto boundaries, the
445	50% cut cannot be recovered, and the coral row becomes a definition gap rather than a coverage gap
446	`[ASSUMPTION]`. The mangrove unit differs outright: indicator 21 asks for canopy density as a
447	percentage, whilst the pilot reports m² per ha and individuals per ha, with no documented conversion
448	(p. 47 [pdf 61]) `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
449	
450	The other two edges are unfixed choices. Neither the minimum mapping unit nor the denominator is fixed
451	by the register or by the SIRUSA definitions `[ASSUMPTION]`. And the extent estimate is itself a
452	function of the grid rule: Rahayu et al. (2024) report mangrove error of 44.06% under a 25 m hierarchy
453	scheme against 1.59% under 25 m dominance, so until a grid rule is adopted the account's figure is a
454	family of figures. A vintage gap sits alongside these, the only verified series closing in 2021 against
455	the index's T-1 requirement.
456	
457	Indicator 24 asks for the *Total luas kawasan mangrove, rawa, dan lahan gambut yang direstorasi per
458	provinsi*, glossed in SIRUSA as degraded area subsequently replanted. That is an activity input measured
459	in hectares planted. The pilot's asset account carries a managed-expansion line that is unpopulated at
460	Gili Matra, where every addition is natural expansion and the only managed entry is 11.19 ha of managed
461	mangrove regression. The correspondence is structural rather than demonstrated: an account of this form
462	records realised managed change, whilst the indicator counts hectares planted. A scope mismatch
463	compounds it, the indicator's three land covers being mangrove, swamp and peatland against the marine
464	account's mangrove, seagrass and coral reef.
465	
466	Indicator 27 asks for the *Total luas kawasan konservasi perairan yang ditetapkan oleh KKP, KLHK, dan
467	Pemerintah Daerah per provinsi*, and is in the tier for a different reason. At 22.0% it is the largest
468	single variable in the resource-quality sub-pillar, about 4.06% of the composite, and it needs no
469	account to be produced. Designated area is an administrative datum already held by its custodians. The
470	account's near-term contribution here is reconciliation of mapped extent to the designated polygon, not
471	supply.
472	
473	The weight chain is shown openly as a multiplication. The environment pillar carries 36.9% of the
474	composite and the resource-quality sub-pillar 50.0% of the pillar (Juknis, Tabel 4 and Tabel 5, p. 94
475	[pdf 104]), giving 18.45%, of which the five Tier A variables sum to about 7.64%. Figure 3 places that
476	band inside the published weight structure. Most of the 7.64% sits at rows the mapping has itself
477	disqualified, so the decomposition is reported in three parts: about 2.33% at
478	the three matched condition rows, about 1.25% at indicator 24 as a partial, and about 4.06% at
479	indicator 27, which the account does not supply. **The share at which an account is the prospective
480	supply route is about 2.33%**, rising to about 3.58% only if indicator 24's structural correspondence
481	is credited in full, which the activity-against-outcome mismatch and the scope gap indicate it should
482	not be. The lower figure is operative and the upper an outer bound.

484	The register is silent on a point worth recording: no producing agency is stated for any of the 44 rows
485	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`.

615	## 4.4 Conditions precedent for supply
616	
617	A definitional match is not a supply arrangement. Five conditions must hold before pilot account output
618	could serve the IBEI as a repeating input, none is satisfied in the obtained record, and they are not
619	equally binding (Table 4). Three are one-off acts of codification that a single decision by MMAF, BPS or
620	BAPPENAS could discharge. Two are discharged only by sustaining a production cycle, and those two
621	decide whether the mapping in Section 4.2 is a recommendation or a wish.
622	
623	The register would be refuted by producing an adopted national marine spatial standard, a BPS
624	statistical work-programme entry for a marine ecosystem account, or an extent compilation covering more
625	than the pilot sites and closing after 2021.
626	
627	The two recurring conditions are the binding ones. SIRUSA specifies reference period T-1 for all five
628	mapped variables. The single verified compilation closes at 2021 and was published the following year,
629	a lag T-1 can accommodate; what is absent is the second vintage, and no repeat compilation of the pilot
630	sites was found `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Figure 5 sets the required cycle against the vintages the accounts
631	have produced: the gap between the 2021 closing year and the reference year the 2025 cycle requires is
632	three reference years. The second is a revision policy, for which the standard supplies the substance
633	(paras 4.17–4.18, p. 88) and Indonesia supplies no instrument `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. An account silently
634	improved between vintages would move a province's index value with no ecosystem change behind it, and
635	nothing in the published record would flag it.
636	
637	One condition follows from the standard rather than from Indonesian practice. Condition is not additive
638	across areas, its variable account being an area-weighted arithmetic mean over the assets of an
639	ecosystem type (para. 5.54, p. 105), so a provincial ratio cannot be recovered by averaging district
640	values and a supplier would need to publish the denominator alongside the percentage.
641	
642	The institutional condition is unmet against an instrument whose own term has run. Kepmen KP 44/2023
643	constituted the Tim Penyusun Neraca Sumber Daya Laut in February 2023 and set its term to expire on
644	31 December 2023, and no renewal instrument was located `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. The July 2025 dashboard launch
645	indicates continued activity under some instrument not obtained, so the defensible statement is that no
646	renewal was located rather than that the mandate lapsed. Two sectoral products feeding one another do
647	not by themselves constitute an accredited statistical input, and no microdata are released, so even a
648	compliant supplier could not verify that its value survived into the published provincial score.
649	
650	One design recommendation follows. Where a province has no account, an account-derived indicator would
651	itself be predicted from socio-economic proxies under the framework the Report publishes (pp. 64–66
652	[pdf 76–78]), so an environmental value would become partly a function of economic and social
653	covariates. The framework's first scenario trains on the previous cycle's historical data, which a newly
654	introduced indicator does not have. **Insertion should be phased behind coverage: compile first, insert
655	second.** That is a sequencing recommendation, not a change to the index's method. Supplementary S3
656	carries the derivation for each condition.

674	# 5. Discussion
675	
676	## 5.1 What compilation changes, and what it does not
677	
678	What an account offers this index at the rows it touches is a production process, not a stock of
679	substitutable values. That is a narrower claim than an efficiency claim, and the evidence assembled
680	here would not support the wider one.
681	
682	The register decomposes into 62 constituent data items across its 44 rows. Three of them —
683	indicators 19, 20 and 21 — are cross-classifications of one extent-and-condition compilation, so the
684	consolidation is three items into one production run, out of 62. The argument is not that the count
685	is large. It is that those three are among the items the index presently has no way to show it
686	measured rather than predicted.
687	
688	A cover-class percentage compiled from a gridded extent surface and a classified cover layer carries
689	its own spatial unit, its own opening and closing stocks, and a provenance chain back to the imagery
690	and field survey behind it. It is reproducible in the next cycle by repeating the compilation. A
691	value taken from a secondary publication, or predicted where no publication exists, carries none of
692	those properties, and the published record does not distinguish the two (Section 2.2).
693	
694	That the distinction is worth publishing is established outside Indonesia. The Ocean Health Index, a
695	composite of comparable construction in the same domain (Halpern et al., 2012), reports that 18.5%
696	of its global score rests on gapfilled rather than observed data, ranging from 1% to 43% across its
697	ten goals and exceeding 60% for ten regions that are predominantly small island territories. Its
698	authors recommend that gapfilling be tracked from the outset and presented in the same framework as
699	the scores themselves (Frazier et al., 2016). The IBEI publishes an imputation framework and no
700	per-indicator record, so the comparison identifies a reporting reform rather than a data programme —
701	and one the index could adopt without any account being compiled.
702	
703	Everything downstream of the input is untouched. Account supply changes the value entering a slot,
704	not the slot, its weight or the aggregation rule. The three-stage principal component procedure, the
705	loading-factor weights, the pillar shares and every limitation the index publishes about itself
706	stand exactly as they are.
707	
708	## 5.2 The counter-instance, and what survives it
709	
710	The strongest evidence against this article's own claim is in its own account-side record. The one
711	compiled Indonesian account has produced two closing stocks and two deltas for the same site, the
712	same three ecosystems and the same two years, disagreeing not by a constant offset but by a ratio
713	that moves across ecosystems and across the accounting window (Section 4.1). The pilot's own
714	reporting compounds it, the narrative giving 16.99% live hard coral cover for 2021 against 18.36% in
715	the report's table on the same page. Change is the quantity a balancing identity exists to report,
716	and provenance is what this article claims accounts buy.
717	
718	This is a disconfirming instance and is retained as one. It is not disposed of by observing that the two
719	series are parallel releases of one mapping layer and that neither is an account of the other: that
720	observation is true, and it is also unfalsifiable in the direction this article needs, since the only
721	compiled Indonesian instance would be reclassified out of the evidence base at the point where it tests
722	the claim. The narrower property claimed
723	here is not that compilation prevents divergence, which nothing in the standard promises, but that a
724	balancing identity gives divergence somewhere to appear — opening stock, additions, reductions and
725	closing stock are four entries that must reconcile, and a second compilation must restate rather than
726	replace. Gili Matra cannot confirm that property, because neither document was compiled under such an
727	identity. It cannot refute it either, for the same reason. What it does establish is the cost of the
728	absence: a 6.26 ha disagreement about coral change was published twice and reconciled nowhere. The
729	provenance property is therefore asserted from account form and is untested on the Indonesian record,
730	and it will remain untested until a compilation is revised under such a rule `[ASSUMPTION]`.
731	
732	A second objection bears on the same argument, and holds outright on two of the four properties. A
733	standing unit and classification definition is an act of codification — a published definition of the
734	mapping unit, the ecosystem classes and the landward and seaward limits — and nothing about compiling
735	an account is needed to write one down. Documented vintage and provenance is likewise a metadata
736	requirement any well-run register discharges. The spatial property is intermediate: a register can
737	mandate a grid and a minimum mapping unit but cannot produce the re-aggregable cell-level tabulation
738	the mandate presupposes. What does not reduce to codification is the residual — the balancing identity,
739	and the revision rule that alters the opening and closing entries themselves — both properties of
740	double-entry over a stock. The residual is thin, and saying so is more useful than defending a wider
741	claim. A reader who concludes that Indonesia should codify definitions before compiling anything has
742	read the evidence correctly, and that ordering is what Section 4.4 recommends.
743	
744	## 5.3 What account supply does not fix
745	
746	The index's custodian has printed its own methodological limits, and account supply reaches none of
747	them. Four are conceded in the Report's own methods statement (Section 3.1.1, p. 34 [pdf 46]): an
748	assumption of linear and monotonic relationships, sensitivity to outliers, weights set on 2024 data
749	that *mungkin saja menjadi tidak relevan untuk tahun-tahun mendatang*, and a single weight structure
750	applied across a heterogeneous archipelago. Every one is a property of the estimator, whilst an
751	account changes the input. The first is worth dwelling on. The threshold relationship BAPPENAS
752	describes — the benefit of fishing intensity holding below a threshold whilst damage rises
753	exponentially above it — is exactly what an ecosystem account measures well, and better inputs
754	cannot reach a relationship the estimator represents linearly by design.
755	
756	Aggregation discards what the account was built to show. The constituent data items for indicators
757	19, 20 and 21 each end *per provinsi*, so an account compiled on a fine spatial grid loses its
758	within-province distribution at the moment it becomes an indicator. The account can tell a
759	provincial planner which reef complex is driving a change; the index can report only that a
760	percentage moved. That is a consequence of publishing the indicator without the grid `[ASSUMPTION]`,
761	so the remedy is an architecture in which both are released. The provincial boundary is
762	administrative and not ecological, and the standard leaves the accounting area to the compiler
763	(para. 2.12), so a reef complex straddling that line is divided by legal artefact. Accounts make the
764	division explicit and auditable; they do not make it ecologically meaningful.
765	
766	Net extent change cannot simply be inserted. The index states its own weighting rule: an indicator
767	with greater variability and strong correlation to others in its domain *akan secara alami
768	mendapatkan bobot yang lebih tinggi* (p. 29 [pdf 41]). A signed annual difference of a large, slowly
769	changing stock is low-variance by construction and can change sign between cycles, so it would attract
770	a small and unstable weight, and might meet the printed rule *Reversi nilai indikator (atau drop
771	indikator) jika ditemukan relasi negatif* (p. 30 [pdf 42]) — whether a sign-changing series triggers it
772	is not specified and is read here as an inference. The mechanism is general: under principal
773	components, weighting corrects for overlapping information rather than measuring importance (Nardo et
774	al., 2008, §6.1). Net extent change belongs beside the composite as a companion series, not inside it
775	as a forty-fifth indicator.
776	
777	Half the environment pillar measures something no ocean account should be built to measure. The
778	renewable-energy sub-pillar carries 50.0% of the pillar and contains no marine variable, being solar
779	and hydropower throughout (S4.3). No existing account can supply what it asks for, and building one
780	to do so would be an instrument error. The coverage claim stops at that boundary, and stopping is
781	what makes it credible.
782	
783	## 5.4 Loose coupling, and three readings that would lead elsewhere
784	
785	Where substitution is not warranted, and on the published maturity evidence it is not, the
786	arrangement that delivers the gain is loose coupling. It has three requirements: shared indicator
787	definitions at the cover-class thresholds the Juknis already prints, with the unit and denominator
788	rules the register leaves open settled with its custodian; a shared spatial unit that aggregates to
789	the province; and parallel publication of the account alongside the index. It is defined as much by
790	what it does not require — no institutional transfer, no change to the index's data pipeline, and no
791	dependency of a statutory index on an experimental compilation. Parallel publication is a combined
792	presentation (paras 14.56–14.57) and is the nearer term of the two, whilst sub-national compilation
793	follows from the accounting area being definable at a subnational administrative boundary (para.
794	2.12) and has been demonstrated at that scale elsewhere (Gacutan et al., 2022). Loose coupling has
795	the property that a compilation cycle missing the window fails without the index failing with it.
796	
797	The institutional division holds at the point of recommendation, where it is most at risk. BAPPENAS is
798	*pengampu* of the calculation whilst the constituent data are provincial (Juknis, p. 68 [pdf 78]), so
799	nothing here asks either institution to take on the other's mandate. The arrangement would have to be
800	settled by whichever body now holds the coordinating function, the team constituted under Kepmen KP
801	44/2023 having had its term expire with no renewal instrument located.
802	
803	Three readings of the same record would lead elsewhere. Rinaldi et al. (2025) construct an
804	alternative index on a human development index template, treating the problem as one of index design
805	rather than of the production system beneath it; on that reading the effort recommended here is
806	misdirected. Wuwung et al. (2024) locate the constraint on Indonesian ocean policy in institutional
807	fragmentation across mandates, which would make an inter-agency supply arrangement the hardest
808	rather than the cheapest of the conditions precedent. Ravallion (2012) defines a mashup index as a
809	composite for which theory and practice provide little guidance on design, leaving the producer free
810	to set an unusually large number of moving parts, and doubts the value added of such indices
811	relative to monitoring the components separately. The answer this record supports is statutory
812	rather than methodological: the IBEI is scored for every province annually under Perpres 12/2025, so
813	the dashboard alternative is not in BAPPENAS's gift, and what supplies the composite is a live
814	question whether or not an aggregate is the instrument a statistician would have chosen. No source
815	located by this study argues that accounts cannot serve as statistical infrastructure for a
816	composite index.
817	
818	## 5.5 Limitations
819	
820	Every share reported here is a nominal share of printed weight, not a measure of effective
821	influence. Nardo et al. (2008, §6.1) hold that under principal components, weighting is not a
822	measure of the theoretical importance of the associated indicator. Paruolo et al. (2013) measure
823	what each variable actually contributes to five composites, the human development index among them,
824	and find that because socio-economic variables are heteroscedastic and correlated, relative nominal
825	weights hardly ever match relative effective importance. The IBEI is a composite over correlated
826	provincial socio-economic variables with weights taken from first-component loadings, which is the
827	configuration in which that divergence was found. Becker et al. (2017) supply the test that would
828	settle it, a correlation-ratio measure of each variable's importance with confidence intervals. It
829	cannot be run here, because it requires the provincial variable matrix beneath the index, which is
830	unpublished `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. What does not survive should be named as plainly as what does.
831	What does not survive is the reading of 7.64%, 2.33%, 12.79% and 13.70% as measures of consequence:
832	they are shares of nominal published weight, and nominal weight is not importance under this estimator.
833	They remain exact statements of what the handbook prints and of what an account bears on, which is the
834	use made of them here. What survives is the production-process case, which never rested on the
835	shares.
836	
837	Six further limitations bear on specific figures. Whether Tabel 4 to Tabel 6's percentages are raw
838	loadings or normalised contributions is not stated anywhere, and the reading adopted here — that
839	they are normalised, because all fifteen printed groups sum to 100.0 within rounding — is an
840	inference from the tables rather than a statement in the source (Section 3.5). Were it wrong, the
841	derived shares would lose their denominator rather than move. The printed aggregation formulae are
842	illegible in the PDF text layer at Juknis pp. 64–65 and Report pp. 31–32, so what is reconstructed
843	is the notation, at medium-high confidence, rather than the method. The per-indicator imputation
844	record is unpublished, which is the largest single unknown, because it determines how much of any
845	published provincial value is measurement and how much is prediction. The mapping is a single-coder assessment against
846	stated criteria, graded by the authors and not blind, with no second coder and no adjudication
847	protocol. Deposit secures auditability, not reliability, and the two are not substitutes: the same
848	team's earlier mapping moved from 61% to 39% to 2.8% as the assumed indicator set changed, which is
849	direct evidence that a grading of this kind is unstable under a change of premise. An
850	author-independent re-grade of all five Tier A rows and a random fifteen of the thirty-nine, reporting
851	raw disagreements, is the check this study has not run. It is why every row's
852	score and exclusion ground is deposited. Compilation cost is absent from the public record at every
853	site. And this study engaged no Indonesian agency, by project constraint, so the absence claims
854	above are conditions to live with rather than future work.
855	
856	# 6. Conclusion
857	
858	Ocean accounts are worth building for this index because of how the handful of indicators they touch
859	would be produced, and not because of how many indicators they could fill. The share an account would
860	itself supply is about 2.33% of the composite on the published weights, which the handbook prints at
861	every level but which are nominal, and nominal weight need not track effective importance under this
862	estimator. What separates the account routes into and out of reach is content rather than compilation
863	maturity: the routes compiled at pilot scale and the route compiled nowhere bear on comparable shares
864	of the index, 12.79% against 13.70% — one containing the other — yet no compiled account produces the
865	constituent data item the register asks for at any of those rows.
866	
867	The binding constraint on acting on this is not methodological. No compilation cost appears anywhere in
868	the record obtained for this study — no staff-day figure, no budget line, no imagery cost and no
869	field-survey cost, at any site `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. A province-by-province annual obligation is a scope
870	decision before it is a method decision. This study declines to estimate a cost, since an
871	order-of-magnitude range would be read as a figure.
872	
873	Two follow-on studies suggest themselves. The claim that indicators 19, 20 and 21 are definitionally
874	matched to account output is empirically testable today at exactly one site. Re-tabulating the Gili
875	Matra compilation to the index's own 50% to 100% cut would settle whether an account's extent and cover
876	layers yield the register's class-share form at all, and would generate the first real compilation-cost
877	observation. Its limits are set by the record itself: the coral and seagrass cover behind that
878	compilation was surveyed at stations rather than mapped per cell, the mangrove row is blocked on an
879	undocumented unit conversion, and a footprint of 2,980.84 ha supports no comparison against a published
880	provincial value. Extending the tabulation to NTB as a whole would test provincial resolution, and
881	requires the province-wide compilation that does not exist. The second output is practitioner-facing
882	rather than research: no published guidance sets out how a sub-national marine ecosystem account should
883	be compiled to serve a provincial indicator in an Indonesian setting. Both assume continued external
884	technical assistance to the Indonesian programme, which is an assumption rather than an established
885	condition `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
886	
887	What would falsify the argument is worth stating, because a paper of this shape is otherwise
888	structurally unfalsifiable. If a single-province compilation reproduces the published provincial value
889	no better than the current secondary-publication route, or does so at a cost per province that an annual
890	national cycle cannot bear, then the production-process case fails on its own terms.
891	
892	Within the statutory boundary held throughout, what this study describes is a capacity gap under a
893	statutory obligation, in a country that has demonstrated, at one experimental pilot site, one instance
894	of the production process the obligation would require at scale.

1426	## S3.1 A national spatial standard
1427	
1428	Provincial account output is not comparable across provinces or across vintages until one minimum
1429	mapping unit and one basic spatial unit are adopted nationally. This is an act of codification,
1430	discharged once, requiring no new compilation.
1431	
1432	The standard requires an account to be consistent over time and with respect to concepts and
1433	classifications (United Nations et al., 2024, para. 1.60), and requires previous estimates to be
1434	revised for continuity of the time series wherever updated data, methods or classifications permit a
1435	reassessment of area (para. 4.17). Grid-based spatial units are the mechanism by which an account
1436	aggregates to any administrative boundary, conditional on the grid existing and being national.
1437	
1438	No adopted national marine grid or minimum mapping unit was found in any obtained Indonesian document
1439	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Rahayu et al. (2024), the only peer-reviewed Indonesian account source, state that a
1440	standardised grid has yet to be established, and the pilot literature records different resolutions at
1441	different sites — the case the rule exists to prevent. Grid choice changes the extent tabulation
1442	itself: mangrove error of 44.06% under a 25 m hierarchy scheme against 1.59% under 25 m dominance. Until
1443	a grid rule is adopted, the account's figure is a family of figures, and the index would be importing a
1444	methodological choice.
1445	
1446	## S3.2 A codified clipping rule
1447	
1448	Provincial marine authority extends to 12 nautical miles under Law 23/2014, cited here at second hand
1449	and not read at source `[EVIDENCE GAP]`, so an account reaching further must be clipped before it can
1450	yield a provincial number.
1451	
1452	The standard leaves the accounting area to the compiler (para. 2.12) and supplies no sub-national
1453	allocation rule for an asset spanning a boundary. The convention this study takes as necessary —
1454	allocation by physical extent within each side, with condition compiled per spatial unit rather than
1455	inherited whole — is a specification rather than a citation `[ASSUMPTION]`. Neither it nor the
1456	statutory line is codified in any account obtained.
1457	
1458	One consequence follows from the standard directly. Condition is not additive across areas, its
1459	variable account being an area-weighted arithmetic mean over the assets of an ecosystem type (para.
1460	5.54, p. 105). Because indicators 19, 20 and 21 ask for a ratio, a provincial value cannot be recovered
1461	by averaging district values, and a supplier would need to publish the denominator alongside the
1462	percentage for a provincial value to be recoverable.
1463	
1464	Clean nesting of cell boundaries across administrative, fisheries-management and marine-spatial-planning
1465	frames is asserted rather than evidenced `[ASSUMPTION]`, and forms part of this condition rather than a
1466	property that may be assumed.
1467	
1468	## S3.3 A repeated compilation inside the reporting window
1469	
1470	SIRUSA specifies reference period T-1 for all five mapped variables (19, 20, 21, 24, 27). The single
1471	verified Indonesian compilation closes at 2021 and was published the following year, a lag T-1 can
1472	accommodate. What is absent is the second vintage. One compilation, however timely, cannot meet a T-1
1473	requirement in any subsequent cycle, and no repeat compilation of the pilot sites was found
1474	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
1475	
1476	This condition is recurring rather than one-off. It is discharged by running the cycle again, not by a
1477	decision, and it survives the index's own inconsistent rendering of its reference periods
1478	`[ASSUMPTION]`. Sustaining a cycle is a resourcing question, and no compilation cost was located in the
1479	public record, so no condition in this register can be priced.
1480	
1481	## S3.4 A revision policy
1482	
1483	The standard already supplies the substance: revision of previous estimates for continuity of the
1484	series, recording no distinct revision entry but altering the opening, closing, addition and reduction
1485	entries themselves, with the size of a revision shown as the difference between the historical and the
1486	revised account for the same period (paras 4.17–4.18, p. 88).
1487	
1488	What is unwritten is the Indonesian instrument that would designate a baseline year and disclose where
1489	the latest extent data refer to an earlier period. The index's own principle of controlled adaptability
1490	presupposes that discipline on the supply side. No revision policy was found for any Indonesian ocean
1491	account output in the searched set `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
1492	
1493	The consequence is specific. An account silently improved between vintages would move a province's
1494	index value with no ecosystem change behind it, and nothing in the published record would flag it. The
1495	instrument requires no new compilation: it is a statement of when a series is restated and how a
1496	provisional estimate is marked.
1497

1500	Account output cannot become a durable input to an official index whilst it remains an unregistered
