# Cut list — which questions are carrying their weight, and which are not

**Date:** 2026-08-14. Assessed against [SOT-journal-article.md](SOT-journal-article.md) revision 1 and
[PRD-journal-article.md](PRD-journal-article.md) v2.
**Occasion:** the director gate found the sections' own asks already exceed the 10,000-word target, and
§§6, 7 and 8 each filed an allocation note saying they cannot carry their obligations. Something has to
go. This is the recommendation for what, and why.

**Evidence used.** Flag density per section (§§4, 7, 8 carry ten flags each; §10 carries six), the three
filed allocation notes, and whether each question is load-bearing for the thesis as revised.

---

## 1. Cut outright

### 1.1 The village-sanitation ratio — [PRD claim 6, second half]

Two coastal-village sanitation counts (~7.3% of the composite) outweigh all three ecosystem-condition
variables (~2.3%) by roughly three to one.

**Why cut.** True, published, and rhetorically satisfying — which is the problem. It does no work in the
mapping. In a paper whose framing constraint is "not an attack on either institution", a three-to-one
comparison inside a sub-pillar named for marine resource quality reads as one however neutrally worded.
This was flagged in the v2 hostile review (F6) and only half-applied. **Keep the first half of claim 6**
— sub-pillar H is solar and hydropower, therefore not marine — because that explains an exclusion in the
mapping and is doing structural work. ~100 words.

### 1.2 Internal conflicts 3, 4 and 5 as analytical content

Targets above the stated maximum; 58 printed rows against 59 stated scenarios; a 50.0/50.0 tie labelled
High and Low.

**Why cut.** These are bookkeeping. They belong to the archived v1 paper, where defects in the index's
specification were the subject. Here the subject is the production chain, and each of these earns at
most one clause in §2's symptom list — not the subsection treatment §8.4 currently gives conflict 5.
Preserve them in the tier-1 corpus record, which is where preservation actually matters. ~200 words.

### 1.3 The asset-depreciation line in Tier C, as prose — [§7.2]

**Why cut to a table row.** The candidate depends on monetary valuation; Indonesian monetary values are
benefit-transferred; TG-1.9 *Safe Usage of Monetary Valuation* is the constraint rather than the
enabler. So the honest finding is "cannot yet be done credibly", which is a Table 3 obstacle cell, not a
paragraph. Prose here invites a reviewer to ask for a valuation discussion the paper cannot support.
~120 words.

### 1.4 Two of the seven conditions precedent — [§6]

Drop **Satu Data Indonesia metadata compliance** (generic to any Indonesian statistic; carries no
marine- or account-specific content) and **the documented per-indicator imputation record** (it is a
transparency ask of BAPPENAS, not a condition on account supply — it belongs in §8.5 limitations, where
it now sits anyway).

**Why cut.** §6 filed the sharpest allocation note in the set: seven conditions at 1,200 words is ~170
words each, "not enough without dropping maturity tags or compressing the phasing argument into an
assertion". Five conditions at ~240 words each is workable. The five that bind are the spatial standard,
the clipping rule, the T-1 window, the revision policy, and the work-programme entry — every one of
which is specific to account supply. ~250 words.

## 2. Demote to table, no prose

### 2.1 Tier B — [§7.1]

Five sub-pillars, ~26% of the composite, and §7 filed an allocation note saying it cannot carry both
this and §7.3.

**Why demote.** Tier B is a list of "the Technical Guidance has a method for this" with no Indonesian
evidence behind any row. That is exactly what a table conveys and prose does not improve. It also has
the highest fabrication risk in the paper — the first figure pass invented an "Applied" badge precisely
here. Table 3 with verified badge and status columns carries it; the text needs two sentences naming
the pattern (economy sub-pillars are reachable, social and governance are not). ~250 words.

### 2.2 The pillar-structure conflict — [§2.3]

**Why demote.** It bites only on indicators 15–18, none of which appears in any tier of the mapping.
The article needs one sentence declaring its locus and one clause noting the conflict is confined to
four indicators. It currently has a subsection, plus recurrences in §5, §7.3 and Figure 5. Declare once,
cross-refer thereafter. ~250 words.

## 3. Reduce sharply

### 3.1 §8.3 — the signed net-extent-change argument

**Why reduce.** This is the last structural remnant of the archived loss-typology thesis: a
near-zero-variance signed variable attracts an unstable PCA weight, so net extent change cannot simply
be inserted. §8's own note calls it "the only genuinely novel argument" and asks for ~280 words.

Novelty is not value here. This paper's contribution is the mapping and the production argument; §8.3's
job is to supply one obstacle cell for one Tier C candidate. At full length it pulls the paper back
toward the critique it deliberately stopped being, and it is the weakest-evidenced passage in the
article — analysis rather than sourced fact, which is the easiest thing for a methodological referee to
reject. **Two sentences plus the Table 3 obstacle cell.** ~250 words.

### 3.2 §3.0 — what ocean accounts are

Already first in the pre-committed compression order (1,000 → 800). **Go further, to ~600.** The four
account types are a table; the stock–flow identity is where the argument lives; a *Marine Policy*
readership does not need the SEEA-EA primer. ~200 words beyond the committed cut.

### 3.3 §9.2 — "what it does not change"

Overlaps §8.4 on sub-pillar H with the same locators. Merge into §9.1's closing. ~150 words.

## 4. Narrow rather than cut — the one I added and would now scope down

### 4.1 "A good statistical register could do the same" — [§3.4 → §4.3]

I added this obligation at SOT revision 1 to close chain break C2. Reassessed: answering it *fully*
means establishing a negative across Indonesian statistical products — an unbounded search, for a
defensive point, in a paper that already carries 82 flags.

**Narrow it.** Scope the claim to *marine ecosystem extent and condition products*, answer it from the
sources already searched, and state it as a bounded search result naming those sources. ~80 words rather
than an open evidence task. If a reviewer produces a counterexample it damages a sentence, not the
argument — which is the same standard the project applies to the precedent claim.

## 6. What must not be cut, and why the temptation will arise

- **The maturity qualifications in §4.** They are the longest, least interesting passages in the paper
  and the first thing a word-count pass reaches for. Overstating account maturity is the one error that
  would make this article irresponsible rather than merely wrong.
- **The 34/1 pairing and the loadings-versus-normalised caveat.** §8 explicitly warns against solving an
  overrun by dropping either. Both are now additionally implicated in contested formulations.
- **Any flagged concession.** The paper's credibility rests on conceding early — that the mature route is
  worth 7.64%, that the definitional match is unverified on one side, that social and governance accounts
  exist nowhere in Indonesia.
- **§8.5 limitations.** Newly added, and the only home for the four permanently unavailable items.

## 7. Two questions the paper is not asking, and should not start

Recorded so they are not rediscovered as gaps.

- **Whether the IBEI is a good index.** Answered in the archived v1 PRD, deliberately abandoned. Every
  residue of it — compensability, sign inversion, band compression — is either withdrawn or reduced to a
  Tier C obstacle.
- **Whether ocean accounts should be compiled at all.** The framing boundary settles it: accounts have
  independent statutory purposes and index supply is one downstream use. The paper's marginal-cost
  argument depends on not reopening this.
