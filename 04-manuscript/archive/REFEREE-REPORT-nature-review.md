## Executive summary

The paper asks whether Indonesia's ocean accounts can serve as *compilation infrastructure* — a
production system with a standing classification, a balancing identity, a re-aggregable spatial
basis and a documented revision rule — for a statutory 44-indicator provincial composite that
conducts no primary collection of its own. Its outstanding feature is methodological honesty of a
kind this literature has lacked: it scores the register against verbatim Indonesian constituent
data items rather than indicator names, and in doing so kills three predecessor derivability
figures (61% → 39% → 2.8%) including its own authors', diagnosing each as an artefact of an assumed
indicator set. It preserves source conflicts rather than resolving them, tags every inference, and
declines to price what the record does not price. Every published share re-derives exactly from the
printed weights.

The decisive weakness is that the paper's headline result — an inversion between a mature route at
7.64% and an aspirational route at 18.9% — is anchored to four display items that do not exist,
rests on a denominator the manuscript itself tags as contested nine times, over-counts one of its
two arms by half again, and compares two subsets while excluding by argument the largest tier
(26.1%). Separately, the single positive claim of the Discussion is that accounts buy provenance;
the manuscript's own Results show the one compiled Indonesian account producing two irreconcilable
official series, and that counter-instance appears nowhere in its seven limitations.

**Recommendation: major revision.** The evidence base appears sound and almost every fix is
bookkeeping rather than new research — none is blocked by the no-agency-engagement constraint. But
the manuscript is not currently in submittable form: no title, no Methods section, no reference
list, no availability statements, no figures, and seven drafting-note blocks still inside the files.

## Consolidated issues

### Critical

**C1 — The two headline results cite display items that do not exist.**
§5.4 L681 `"giving 18.45% (Figure 2)"`; §7.3 L1130 `"by roughly two and a half times on the adopted
denominator (Supplementary Fig. S1)"`. Figures 1, 2, 3 and Supplementary Fig. S1 are cited at L109,
L681, L856 and L1130 and have no legend, no caption and no file anywhere in the drafts. Both of the
paper's carrying quantities — the Tier A weight decomposition and the inversion ratio the abstract
leads with — are therefore unverifiable at the point a referee would check them.
*Sections: Results, Methods, Conclusion.*
**Fix:** commission the four figures with legends stating that shares are this study's products of
published percentages and are indicative; or strike all four citations and route the reader to
Table 3 and Table 6, which already carry every number. Do not submit with cited-but-absent items.

**C2 — A self-tagged contested figure carries load-bearing conclusions, and the abstract states it
as settled.**
`[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]` appears nine times on 7.64% (L90, L682, L912, L1122, L1321, L1425, L1462,
and elsewhere), and §8.5 L1319–1321 concedes that whether Tabel 4–6's percentages are raw loadings
or normalised contributions `"is not stated anywhere, and every derived figure in this article
inherits that"`. Yet §9.1 L1423–1426 reasons *from* that figure to a directional conclusion
(`"inserting account-derived values would perturb the index very little, which cuts against the fear
of destabilisation"`), and the abstract L15–16 states `"about 7.6%"` and `"roughly 19%"` with no
hedge and no tag. The paper cannot both disclaim the denominator and reason from the numerator.
*Sections: Abstract, Results, Discussion, Conclusion, Methods.*
**Fix:** restate the §9.1 inference as conditional and symmetric under both readings; carry the
qualification into the abstract; and define what `[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]` means at first use in
§5.4 — it currently sits in apparent contradiction with L586 (`"the unresolved conflict does not
reach this section's arithmetic"`), so a referee cannot tell whether the tag names a problem the
manuscript has already answered.

**C3 — The 3.58% supply-route figure over-counts by half again.**
§5.4 L685: `"The share at which the account is the prospective supply route is therefore about
3.58%"`. That is 2.33 + 1.25, crediting indicator 24's **full** weight — but §5.3 L649–653 has just
established that the account records realised managed change whilst `"the indicator counts hectares
planted"`, and that `"even complete national coverage would leave indicator 24 partly unserved"`. A
row the text has disqualified cannot contribute its whole weight to the share the account would
supply. 3.58% overstates 2.33% by 54%.
*Sections: Results, Introduction, Abstract.*
**Fix:** report as a range — 2.33% supplied, rising to about 3.58% only if indicator 24's structural
correspondence is credited in full, which §5.3 shows it should not be — and carry the two-figure
form into §7.3 L1124, §6.5 L913, §1 L91 and the abstract.

**C4 — The membership rule is stated two incompatible ways, so the mapping is not reproducible.**
§5.1 L571–579 defines matching and says `"Two further rows enter under a weaker rule"`, implying
five matched plus two more. §5.4 L682–684 then attributes 2.33% to `"the three definitionally
matched condition rows"`, classes 24 as a partial and says of 27 that `"the account does not
supply"` it. So Tier A is 3 matched + 2 weak-rule rows, and the "two further rows" are those same
two, not additional ones. A reader cannot tell whether the population is 5 or 7. The 7.64% is a sum
over a set whose membership rule is given twice, differently.
*Sections: Methods, Results.*
**Fix:** replace §5.1's rule with an explicit three-grade scheme (*matched* n=3; *partial* n=1;
*provenance question* n=1; Tier A = 5), state the 44-row denominator in the same passage, and repair
L580 and L682 to use those terms consistently.

**C5 — There is no Methods section, no scoring sheet for the 39 excluded rows, and no availability
statements.**
The analysis *is* the mapping, yet the 44-row scoring record is never published: Table 3 shows only
the five included rows, and the 39 excluded rows carry no score and no exclusion reason. The
underlying corpus, its construction and its query protocol are never described in the manuscript.
Data Availability, Code Availability, ethics, competing interests, funding and the reference list
are all absent. §8.5 L1331–1332 warns that predecessor figures `"moved from 61% to 39% to 2.8%, each
an artefact of a different assumed indicator set"` — without the row-level sheet, this paper's 7.64%
is equally unfalsifiable.
*Sections: Methods, Results.*
**Fix:** a ~1,200-word Methods section carrying the corpus description with retrieval dates, the
membership rule from C4, the search protocol from M7, and the arithmetic convention; deposit the
join table, the 44-row scoring sheet and the weight workbook; add the standard back matter.

**C6 — The Discussion's one positive claim is contradicted by the manuscript's own Results, and the
contradiction is absent from the limitations.**
§9.1 L1411–1413 claims accounts add `"provenance of a specific kind"` and let a provincial figure
`"be revised on improved information and defended afterwards"`. But §4.2 L444–451 reports two
official extent series for the same pilot, ecosystems and years that are never reconciled. My own
check shows this is worse than a level offset: the BPS:MMAF ratios are not constant — coral 0.707
(2015) → 0.667 (2021), seagrass 0.736 → 0.633, mangrove 0.629 → 0.575 — so the two series disagree
on the **change** (MMAF coral −12.00 ha against BPS −18.26 ha over the same window), which is
precisely the quantity the balancing identity exists to report. §5.2 L606–608's unreconciled 16.99%
vs 18.36% on a single page compounds it. §8.5's seven limitations mention neither.
*Sections: Discussion, Results.*
**Fix:** add this as the first limitation in §8.5, then interrogate it in §9.1 — does the balancing
identity make the divergence *visible* (which would support the claim, and is what §10.1 already
argues) or merely reproduce it? The honest answer probably strengthens the paper, but it must be
argued, not omitted.

**C7 — §7.1 asserts score-invariance, contradicting a concession established in §5.4.**
§7.1 L1010: `"What is established is that no route in Table 5 supplies a quantity of a kind the
index does not already specify, so none would raise or lower a provincial score."` §5.4 L692–694
concedes the opposite mechanism: `"weights derived by principal components are re-estimated from
the data, so a substitution is not weight-invariant."` Recompiling a quantity on a different industry
boundary changes its value; under a data-re-estimated PCA, changed values change weights and
therefore scores. Nothing in Table 5 *establishes* invariance — at most the register's specification
is unchanged.
*Sections: Results.*
**Fix:** restate as *"No route in Table 5 supplies a quantity of a kind the register does not already
specify. Whether recompiling those quantities on an accounting industry boundary would change their
values, and hence provincial scores, cannot be determined here, and under a data-re-estimated PCA it
cannot be assumed not to (Section 5.4)."*

**C8 — The final sentence of the paper inflates a single pilot into a national capability.**
§10.3 L1523–1524: `"a country that has already built, at one site, the form of production the
obligation requires"`. This is the only unhedged clause in a section that spends seventy lines
qualifying, and it converts one experimental-maturity pilot — one MPA, one province, one vintage,
tag (b) throughout — into the form of production a 38-province annual statutory obligation requires.
It sits against §10.1 L1459–1461's own opening promise and against §1 L88's `"stated at the outset so
that nothing later reads as an overclaim"`.
*Sections: Conclusion.*
**Fix:** *"…has demonstrated, at one experimental pilot site, one instance of the production process
the obligation would require at scale."*

### Major

**M1 — The inversion excludes the largest tier by argument, and the exclusion rule is not stated
independently of the result.** §7.3 L1120–1122: `"Tier B stands outside the comparison on both
sides"`. Tier B is 26.1% of the composite, larger than either arm. The two reasons given are
asymmetric: "no Indonesian ocean-economy boundary exists" is precisely the immature-account-type
condition that *qualifies* social and governance for the aspirational arm. State the exclusion
criterion in §7.1 before the tiers are priced, and report in one sentence what including Tier B does
to the ratio. *(Results M4.)*

**M2 — The numerical spine is pre-announced twice and derived once.** The abstract (L15–18), the
Introduction (L88–95) and §5.4 L688–690 by forward reference all state 7.64 / 4.06 / 1.25 / 3.58 /
18.9 before §7.3 derives them 400 lines later. Under sequential-build no statement may depend on a
result not yet established. Consequence: if a referee rejects the 3.58% (C3) or the Tier B exclusion
(M1), the correction propagates to four sections. Cut the forward-referenced figures at L688; reduce
the Introduction's preview to one figure or none. *(Results M2, Introduction M4.)*

**M3 — The loadings-vs-contributions ambiguity is disclosed but never quantified.** §8.5 L1319–1321
discloses it and every derived figure inherits it, but no sensitivity recomputation is offered. The
test is trivial: renormalise the variable weights to sum to 100 within the sub-pillar and report the
resulting range for 7.64%, 3.58%, 26.1% and 5.15%. Without it a reader cannot tell whether the
ambiguity moves the headline by 0.1 points or by a factor. *(Methods M1.)*

**M4 — The alternative explanation the authors themselves raise is abandoned.** §3 L330 poses the
decisive objection: `"a well-documented statistical register could supply all four properties
without any account being compiled."` §9.1's provenance argument is exactly what that objection
targets, and §9.2's first requirement is shared *definitions* — codification, not accounting; §6.5
concedes three of five conditions are `"acts of codification"`. The Discussion must isolate the
residual that genuinely requires an account (the balancing identity; the revision rule) and say why
metadata reform alone cannot supply it. If the residual is thin, that is itself a finding.
*(Discussion M2.)*

**M5 — The 62-item register decomposition appears only in the Discussion.** §9.1 L1397–1402 states
`"The register decomposes into 62 distinct constituent data items across its 44 indicators"` with no
folio and no display item, yet §5.1's whole membership test operates on constituent data items and
is the natural place for that denominator. *(Note: the derivation exists in
`briefs/section-9-brief.md` as arithmetic over `05-rag/rag-parts/join-table.json`, Juknis Tabel 1
pp. 11–13 [pdf 21–23] — it simply never reached the manuscript.)* Derive it in §5.1 with its folio
and let §9.1 cite back. *(Results M6, Discussion M4.)*

**M6 — The pillar-locus crosswalk is missing for the §7 figures.** §2.3 L181–186 adopts a hybrid —
three-pillar arithmetic, Tabel 1 row labels — and §5.1 L585 correctly notes the conflict does not
reach §5's arithmetic because all five Tier A rows are environment-pillar under both loci. But §7's
26.1% and 5.15% are computed over exactly the pillars where rows 15–18 move, and §7.3 L1101 concedes
the governance variable becomes indicator 18 in the Enabler pillar under the four-pillar locus. State
at L1004 and L1099 which figures are structure-dependent and that no equivalent four-pillar figure
can be given. *(Methods M4.)*

**M7 — Absence claims rest on a search whose protocol is not recorded.** §4.3 L502–508 and Table 4's
caption name the searched set honestly and concede `"No dedicated search of the BPS statistical
catalogue or of Satu Data Indonesia was undertaken"` — but give no retrieval dates (only SIRUSA, at
L305), no queries, no bilingual search record, no date range. §3 L331–333 then leans on this bounded
search to answer the M4 objection, which is the weakest available foundation for a load-bearing
rebuttal. Add dates, platforms and the Indonesian query terms actually used. *(Methods M3.)*

**M8 — Loose coupling is generalised nationally from one pilot, against a lapsed instrument.**
§9.2 L1432 presents a three-requirement national architecture. The evidence ceiling is one MPA in
one province at one vintage, all tag (b) — which §8.4 L1308 concedes but §9.2 does not carry
forward. Compounding it, §9.2 L1449–1451's feasibility claim is made without noting that Kepmen KP
44/2023's team term expired 31 December 2023 with no renewal instrument located (§4.3 L493–496). An
architecture requiring inter-agency coordination should engage the fact that the coordinating
instrument has lapsed. *(Discussion M5.)*

**M9 — No contradicting literature is engaged anywhere.** Across §8 and §9 the only external sources
are United Nations et al. 2024 and Gacutan et al. 2022, both supportive. No source in the manuscript
is cited as disagreeing with its conclusion; the only objection handled is one the authors raise
themselves (§3 L330). With no reference list this cannot be fully adjudicated. Bring at least two
sources into §9 that could be read against the conclusion, or state explicitly that the position is
uncontested and cite the nearest adjacent work. *(Discussion M3.)*

**M10 — The Introduction's ¶4 does §2's job, and the section ends on caveats rather than findings.**
L52–66 delivers the Perpres mechanism, the SIRUSA registration, the imputation framework and the
undocumented rescaling factor, then hands off with `"they are documented in Section 2"` (L65–66).
Separately the section's last paragraph (L97–103) is scope boundaries, so the reader's final
impression of the Introduction is a disclaimer. Cut ¶4 to two or three sentences; move the boundary
paragraph into the approach paragraph; end on a compact findings statement. *(Introduction C1, C2.)*

**M11 — The Discussion opens on drafting history rather than restating the advance.** §9.1 L1394:
`"An earlier form of this argument claimed that account supply would streamline the index's
compilation… it is withdrawn here."` A reader who skipped the Results opens the Discussion and is
told what a previous draft got wrong. The withdrawal is creditable but belongs as one sentence in
the Introduction's prior-work positioning. *(Discussion M1.)*

**M12 — The Conclusion is a fourth copy, not a synthesis, and carries four competing take-homes.**
§10.1 L1459–1475 repeats the percentages and the four account properties already given at §1 L88–95,
§3 L281–291, §6.5 L910–916 and §9.1 L1421–1428; L1520–1524 repeats the Introduction's boundary
paragraph near-verbatim. The section then offers the production-process case, the unpublished-cost
argument, two follow-on studies and the statutory boundary with no signal of which is primary.
*(Conclusion M1, M2, M3.)*

**M13 — The abstract fails three template requirements.** No citations anywhere (L4–21); no
broadening close — it ends on an evidence gap (L20–21), so the hourglass never re-widens; 267 words
against the ~200 ceiling that applies when the broader-perspective section is absent. The "Here we
show" moment (L6) also carries no signal phrase. *(Abstract C1, C2, M1, M2.)*

**M14 — The abstract's headline framing contradicts the paper's own Conclusion.** The abstract leads
with the percentage inversion (L14–18); §10.1 L1459–1461 says `"the contribution is not that they can
supply a meaningful fraction of it"`, and the Discussion's actual advance is conceptual. Re-balance
the inversion sentence to lead with the production-process point and cite the percentages as
supporting. *(Abstract M4.)*

### Minor

**m1 — Cell count and reported area do not reconcile.** §4.1 L418–419: `"2,980.84 ha on a 25 m by
25 m … grid of 48,211 cells"`. At 625 m²/cell, 48,211 cells is 3,013.19 ha — a 32.35 ha (1.1%)
shortfall; 2,980.84 ha implies 47,693 cells. This may simply be boundary cells clipped at the MPA
edge, so it is **plausible rather than confirmed** — but §5 argues the grid rule determines the
extent tabulation, and the section's own standard elsewhere (L466, L606) is to record such tensions
as observed and unexplained. Add one clause and tag it.

**m2 — Three derived figures lack folios and shown arithmetic.** 2.75% and 0.77% (L1023–1024) and
1.34% (L1102) are given without derivation, and sub-pillar A's pillar weight — needed to reproduce
the 0.31% and 4.11% at L1020–1021 — is never stated. Conspicuous against Table 3's caption, which
exposes its multiplication in full.

**m3 — The economy pillar's decomposition is never assembled in one place.** 15.2+23.6+19.3+22.1+15.8
= 96.0 reads as a 4% hole. It is not: the residual is the technology sub-pillar, stated at L1102 as
~1.34% of composite = 4.11% of the pillar, closing to 100.11. Nothing is wrong with the arithmetic —
but the reader must reconstruct it across two subsections. State it once.

**m4 — Inline drafting apparatus and drafting-note blocks remain in the manuscript.**
`[ASSUMPTION]` ×18, `[EVIDENCE GAP]` ×~20, `[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]` ×9 in running prose, including
in the Conclusion (L1462, L1481, L1512); seven HTML-comment `DRAFTING NOTE` blocks sit inside the
draft files (L214–276, L349–393, L517–557, L723–793, L934–986, L1189–1242, L1339–1387). The
apparatus is admirable internally but produces the hedging asymmetry at C2. Convert to prose
qualifiers with a documented legend, or footnote them.

**m5 — Unglossed institutional jargon.** BAPPENAS (L44), Perpres 12/2025 (L52), KKP (L58) vs MMAF
(L98) for what appears to be the same ministry, SIRUSA, Juknis — none expanded at first use.

**m6 — Sensitivity figures given without units or convention.** §7.3 L1113: `"+2.45 against
good-condition coral reef at +0.41"`. Two bare signed numbers cannot be interpreted.

**m7 — Subheading promises a distinction the text withdraws.** §4.2 L433 `"three account types,
three maturities"`, but L427–428 assigns tag (b) to all three. What varies is completeness.

**m8 — Two different coral measures juxtaposed across years.** §5.2 L604–605: `"average live coral
cover of 18.11% in 2016 and 16.99% live hard coral in 2021"`. No trend is claimed; the juxtaposition
invites one.

**m9 — No stated rounding or precision convention.** Shares are quoted to two decimals from
one-decimal inputs; "about" is present at L681 but absent from Table 3's tabulated values.

**m10 — Section-length and register issues.** §8+§9 ≈ 2,589 words, the whole of a Nature main-text
budget spent on the Discussion alone; §10 runs three subsections against the one-paragraph
convention; the subheading "The number nobody has published" (L1477) is a register shift.

## Arguments for and against publication

**For.** The core contribution is conceptual and it is executed: a four-property definition of
compilation infrastructure, and the observation that extent and condition accounts produce
quantities the register already demands whilst services and asset accounts produce quantities
appearing nowhere in it. The paper withdraws its own earlier and more attractive claim, kills three
predecessor figures including its authors' own, and states the resulting claim narrowly enough that
it survives its own numbers. The epistemic hygiene — refusing to resolve the two-pillar conflict,
tagging every inference, conceding that no Indonesian account has published any of the five
indicator values — is the discipline this literature has lacked.

**Against.** The headline inversion is damaged on both arms: one cites a figure that does not exist
and over-counts a row the text disqualifies, the other rests on a self-conceded assumption and is
compared against a field from which the largest tier has been removed by argument. The Discussion's
one positive claim is contradicted by the manuscript's own Results and the contradiction is omitted
from the limitations. §7.1 contradicts §5.4. And the manuscript is a set of draft files with process
metadata still inside them, missing title, Methods, figures, references and every declaration.

The remedy is substantial but tractable, requires no new research, and is not blocked by the
no-agency-engagement constraint.
