## 1. Structure and logic — Acceptable

The order follows the reader, not the author. Every section carries a *Logical connection* block that
names the reader's question and hands it forward, and the chain test at the end is honest rather than
decorative — it declares five breaks (C1–C5) rather than claiming a clean chain. That is the right
posture and it is why this criterion is not "Needs work". Three specific defects.

1. **§6 is scoped to Tier A but sits where it governs the whole paper.** §6.0's core claim, condition 3
   ("all five Tier A variables"), and §6.3 are entirely about the five environmental indicators. But
   §7.3 then makes the paper's largest recommendation — an ocean-sector classification standard bearing
   on ~13.7% (or ~30.5%) of the composite — and it receives no conditions-precedent treatment anywhere.
   A reader who accepted §6's discipline for a 7.6% route will ask what the conditions are for the 30%
   route and find none. Fix one of three ways: (i) move §6 after §7 so the register covers both mapping
   sections; (ii) keep the position and add a two-sentence conditions paragraph to §7.3 (custodian for
   the classification standard, BPS concordance, adoption instrument); or (iii) declare §6's scope as
   Tier-A-only in its first sentence and say why. (iii) is cheapest, (i) is best, and (i) also fixes the
   heading-parallelism defect at 6 below.
2. **C2 is a real missing bridge and must not be carried into drafting.** §3.4 pre-empts the reviewer's
   strongest objection — a well-documented statistical register could supply all four production
   properties without any account — and explicitly defers the answer to §4 as an empirical question.
   §4 nowhere establishes that no equivalent register exists for Indonesia's marine ecosystem
   variables. The objection is raised and abandoned across the §3/§4 boundary. Either §4.1 adds that
   absence finding with its search boundary named (per its own absence-claim rule), or §3.4 must answer
   on the narrower ground it can actually hold — that the four properties are produced as one artefact
   in one cycle — and stop promising an empirical reply. Do not draft §3 until this is decided.
3. **One argument is owned by three sections (C3/D5).** The imputation-phasing point appears at §6 kp
   12 and §6.5, §8 kp 7, and §9 kp 7 and §9.1. §8's word-allocation note assumes §6 carries it in full;
   §9 restates it as a counter-move. Assign it to §6.5 exclusively and reduce §8 and §9 to a
   cross-reference of one clause each. Fix this in the standing rules, not at reconciliation, or all
   three drafting agents will write it.

Not a defect: §6 sitting between the two mapping sections is defensible on the reader's question at the
end of §5 ("then why is this not already happening?"), which §6 answers. The problem is scope, not
position.

## 2. Completeness — Needs work

Four things essential to the deliverable are absent, and two things present add length without argument.

1. **There is no abstract in the skeleton.** No section 0, no allocation, no purpose, no core claim. Yet
   four flags and both recorded disagreements (D1, D2) instruct that "the abstract" must adopt a
   convention, PRD §7.3 says the inversion must be stated in the abstract, and hostile review F9 asked
   for the Emerging-badge sentence to go there. Marine Policy also requires highlights. Add §0 with its
   own core claim, because the abstract is where D1 and D2 bind hardest and where the inversion is most
   easily misquoted.
2. **Figure 1 is unassigned.** PRD §9 calls the compilation-chain schematic "the paper's most
   load-bearing graphic" and specifies its content (sources → imputation → standardisation → three-stage
   PCA → rescale → provincial score → RPJPD target, with the MEP calendar along the top and Tier A entry
   points marked). It appears nowhere in the skeleton: no section claims it, no evidence line specifies
   its inputs, no word budget accounts for its caption. Tables 1–3 and the recommended Table 4 are all
   placed. Assign it to §2 or §3, or record a decision to drop it.
3. **The article has no limitations-of-this-study statement, and 82 flags therefore have no destination
   in the manuscript.** PRD §6 names four items as "§8's limitations paragraph": the illegible printed
   formulae at Juknis pp. 64–65 and Report p. 31 (notation reconstructed, substance not); the
   loadings-versus-normalised-contributions question; which indicators were imputed; and the

   §8's skeleton carries the second (as the loadings caveat's second required appearance) and touches
   the third by cross-reference. The **illegible-formulae item appears nowhere in the SOT at all**, and

   not fix" — is a different object from "what this study could not verify", and conflating them will
   leave the paper with no place to state its own evidence limits. Add an explicit limitations passage,
   either as §8.5 or as a closing paragraph of §8.1, carrying all four items.
4. **The aggregate word budget is unreconciled and F9's pre-committed compression order was not
   absorbed.** The sections' own notes ask for: §1 at 1,050–1,080, §3 at 1,150–1,200, §4 at ~1,400, §5

   kept, §10 at 800. Those asks alone exceed 10,000 before Table 1, Table 4 and Figure 1 captions.
   F9 predicted 11,000 and specified the compression order; the SOT contains no compression order at
   all, only nine independent pleas for the upper bound. Pre-commit it now.

Unnecessary material, both nominated by the SOT itself and both of which should simply be cut rather

## 3. Clarity — Acceptable

An expert reader can follow it. The Purpose / Core claim / Key points / Evidence / Logical connection
discipline holds across all ten sections and all thirty-one subsections, and six sections state an
explicit falsification condition (§6.0, §7.0, §7.1, §7.2, §7.3, §9.0, and §10 kp 10). Falsifiability
stated at section level is unusual and it is the skeleton's best feature. Three defects.

1. **Two core claims are too long to function as claims.** §5.0's runs to roughly 100 words across three
   clauses and a semicolon-joined concession; §6.0's to roughly 110 across a four-part division plus a
   refutation condition. A core claim a drafter cannot hold in mind will not survive into the prose.
   Split each into a claim sentence and a qualification sentence.
2. **§7.3's job is ambiguous because it carries four jobs.** Its title names social and governance
   accounting; its contents are the social decomposition, the governance sub-pillar, indicator 27's
   designation-versus-management point (an *environment*-pillar variable), the inversion statement and
   three guard rails — at ~620 words, the tightest budget-to-load ratio in the paper. Indicator 27
   belongs there on the argument's logic (it is a governance-account contribution to the environment
   pillar) but the heading conceals it. Retitle to signal the cross-pillar content, or promote indicator
   27 to a short §7.4.
3. **The 12-nm clip is introduced three times with three different jobs** — §3.3 defers it, §6.2
   conditions it, §8.2 concedes it — and a reader meeting it in §3.3 cannot tell which treatment is
   authoritative. Either drop the §3.3 mention entirely (its own flag already recommends deferring it)
   or state in §3.3 that it returns twice, as a condition and as a residual limit.

## 4. Precision — Needs work

This is the criterion where the skeleton is weakest, and one of the defects is at the level of the
paper's title term.

1. **"Compilation infrastructure" is never defined.** §3.0's purpose says the section exists "so that
   'compilation infrastructure' in the thesis has a concrete referent before any mapping is attempted",
   and then no key point in §3.0–§3.4 supplies the definition. Four *properties* are named (standing
   unit-and-classification definition, balancing identity, re-aggregable spatial basis, documented
   vintage and revision), but the noun is never defined as a noun. The article's title, thesis and
   §9's whole argument turn on it. Add a one-sentence definition as §3.0's first key point and use it
   verbatim in §1.3 and §9.1.
2. **"Services" is used incompatibly across three sections, and this is a maturity-discipline breach,
   not a style point.** Standing rules §3.0 and §1 kp 9 / §1.3 state Indonesian coverage extends to
   "extent, condition and services". §4's evidence gap, working from the primary documents, finds that
   what exists is benefit-transferred monetary valuation of ecosystem functions, **not** SEEA-EA
   services supply-and-use in physical units, and instructs that the one must not be upgraded into the
   other. §7.2 then states that no services account is compiled anywhere in Indonesia, and §7.2's key
   point gives services "tag (b) at best and (c) for most". Three positions, one of them mandated by the
   standing rules. Fix by definition: reserve "services account" for SEEA-EA physical supply-and-use,
   use "benefit-transferred valuation of ecosystem functions" for what Indonesia has, and amend
   standing rules §3.0 accordingly. Until that is done §1 overstates account maturity, which PRD §6
   calls the fatal error for this paper.
3. **"Condition" carries four senses, one of which is a homonym.** (i) the SEEA-EA condition account
   (three-stage reference-condition procedure, A1.5); (ii) Indonesian cover-based Tier 1–2 measurement
   against documented reference bands; (iii) the IBEI's percentage-of-area-in-a-50–100%-cover-class.
   Senses (i)–(iii) are distinguished carefully at §3.1, §4.2 and §5.1 and that work is good. But (iv)
   "conditions precedent" occupies the whole of §6, whose subsections are titled "Spatial conditions",
   "Temporal conditions", "Institutional conditions" and "The traceability condition" — while §5.2 is
   about condition indicators and §6's own condition 3 governs the five condition variables. Reserve
   "condition" for the ecosystem sense and rename §6's items *requirements* or *preconditions*, keeping
   "conditions precedent" only as the register's proper name.
4. **"Coverage" carries five senses.** Provincial/geographic completeness of accounts (§4, §5, §6);
   "cover class" and "live coral cover" as the measured variable (§5.2, §4.2); "coverage gap" as one of
   the four gap types (§5.4); TG-3.7's "governance coverage index" (§7.3); and social-protection
   coverage in the register's own wording. §5.2 uses "coverage" as a gap type and "cover" as a
   measurement inside the same key point. Fix a rule: **coverage** = spatial completeness only;
   **cover** / **cover class** = the ecological variable; quote TG-3.7's index by its full name.
5. **"Compilation" carries four senses in adjacent sentences.** The IBEI's own registered activity
   (*Kompilasi Produk Administrasi*, §2.1, §6.4); account compilation by MMAF (§4); "compilation
   infrastructure" as the thesis term (§3); and "documented published compilation" as the counting unit
   of the 34/1 pair (§4.1). §6.4 uses three of the four in one core claim. At minimum, never use the
   IBEI sense and the account sense in one sentence without a qualifier.
6. **"Streamline" is barred from a results sentence and retained in a section heading.** §9.0 kp 1
   concedes F3 in text and reclassifies the claim as structural, which is correct — but §9's title is
   still "Discussion: streamlining, not substituting". Either retitle (§9 "What Tier A compilation
   changes, and what it does not" is available from §9.1/§9.2) or state in §9.0 that the heading uses
   the word architecturally and the results sentences do not.
7. **Three tier vocabularies coexist.** The PRD's ("can serve now" / "reachable with existing TG
   method" / "what accounts could let the index measure"), hostile review F4's proposed
   demonstrated / compilable / additive, and §5.1's new "definitionally matched; compilation
   demonstrated at pilot scale". §5's heading uses the first, §5.1 rejects it, §7's subsection headings
   use the third form for B and C. Fix one vocabulary and apply it in both mapping headings.

## 5. Concision — Acceptable

Section-level discipline is real: every section has a word-allocation note that names what it will
protect and what it will surrender, and several nominate their own least costly cut. That is why this
is not "Needs work". Six specific tightenings, plus one aggregate action.

1. **§1.2 duplicates §2.2.** The same four symptoms appear in both, with the same chunk locators. §1.2's
   five bullets and §2.2's six bullets carry `chunk_107`, `chunk_109`, `chunk_016` and
   `cross-slice-conflict_chunk_001` twice each. §1.2 should name the symptoms without locators and defer
   all citation to §2, or drop to two symptoms. This alone buys §1 the 30–40 words its allocation note
   says it needs for the governance-locus caveat.
2. **The 0.6819 factor appears five times** — §1.2, §2.0, §2.2, §3.2, §9.1. Two are load-bearing (§2.2
   as evidence, §9.1 as the provenance contrast). Cut it from §3.2, which can make the same point on the
   imputation record alone, and reduce §1.2 to a clause.
3. **Coral +0.41 from Tabel 7 appears in five prose positions** — §5.0 kp 9, §5.2, §7.3 kp 10, §8 kp 5,
   §8.3 — against the PRD's instruction to make the sensitivity point in two sentences and not build a
   section on it. Keep §5.2 and §8.3; cut from §7.3.
4. **Indicator 27's weight (22.0% / ~4.06%) is restated four times** — §5.0 kp 7, §5.3, §7.3 kp 10, §8's
   evidence table. The DoD requirement that the designation-versus-management *argument* be made once
   holds; the *weight* should be stated once in §5.3 and referred back to thereafter.
5. **§8.4 and §9.2 both carry sub-pillar H and the village-sanitation counts.** Consolidate into §8.4
   and have §9.2 cross-refer in a clause.
6. **The 34/1 pair appears in eight passages** (§1's evidence table, §4.1, §5.1, §6.4, §8.2, §8 kp 8,
   §9 kp 12, §10.3). The repetition is mandated by standing rules §3.0, but at eight appearances in a
   9,500-word paper it reads as a tic, and check 4 below shows the numeral is unverified anyway. State
   the pair in full once in §4.1, then use a short fixed formula thereafter, and amend the standing rule
   to say so.

**Aggregate:** commit the compression order before drafting, per F9 — §3 to 800 by assuming SEEA
literacy, §8 to 700, §2's symptom enumeration moved into Table 1's rows — and note that Table 1 and
the recommended Table 4 are both unbudgeted in PRD §5.

## 6. Polish — Needs work

1. **§5's heading contradicts its own first subsection.** "Mapping: indicators the existing accounts can
   serve now" against §5.1's conclusion that "'can serve now' must be dropped because no Indonesian
   account has published any of these five indicator values anywhere". This is the one place where a

2. **Numbered key points do not map onto subsections.** §2 has ten numbered key points and three

3. **Table numbers do not follow reading order.** Table 1 (§2), Table 2 (§5), Table 4 recommended (§6),

5. **Heading parallelism.** Two "Mapping:" sections (§5, §7) are separated by §6, and two labelled

## Project-specific checks

**1. Does any section rest on finding 02 without a primary re-check flag? — No, but the SOT is

**4. Is every account-maturity claim tagged, and is the 34-selectable / 1-documented pair always paired?
— Tagged and paired in form; unsound in substance.**
Tag (b) is carried at every account-side claim, the pair is stated together in all eight of its

**5. Does anything rest on an unpublished compilation, explicitly or implicitly? — Explicitly no;
implicitly, one exposure that must be closed.**

write the pair as "a national dashboard covering most provinces, against one province with a documented

the step from pilot input layer to indicator 19 "is one additional tabulation" implies a wall-to-wall
per-grid condition surface exists; §5's second flag catches it and the wording constraint must survive
drafting.

## Summary verdict

This is a strong skeleton carrying a genuine defect at the level of its headline number. The
architecture follows the reader's questions, every section states a purpose, a single arguable claim and
a forward hand-off, six sections state their own falsification conditions, the flag register is complete
and internally consistent at 82 flags with no orphans, and — most unusually — the chain test declares
five breaks rather than claiming a clean chain. The hostile reviews have been absorbed substantively
rather than cosmetically: F2's concession is deeper than the review asked, F3's surrogate quantification
is exactly what was specified, and F1's primary re-check has been performed far enough to *contradict
the standing rules it was meant to confirm*, which is the mark of a re-check done honestly. Against
that: the paper's inversion — the finding everything else is arranged around — is currently carried in
two incompatible arithmetics (D1: 7.64% or ~11.2%; D2: ~13.7% or ~30.5%), the abstract that must state
it does not exist in the skeleton, Figure 1 is unassigned, the article has nowhere to state its own
evidence limitations so PRD §6's four unavailable items have no home, the term "compilation
infrastructure" is never defined, and the standing rules still mandate three account-maturity
formulations that §4's own primary reading could not confirm. None of these is a reason to rebuild; all
of them are reasons not to dispatch ten drafting agents against briefs that would each propagate the
same unresolved figures.
