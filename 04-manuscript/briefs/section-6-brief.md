## 1. The section's plan

### 6.0 Conditions precedent for continued supply

**Purpose.** Convert §5's "these five indicators are definitionally matched" into an operational
answer to the reader's obvious next question — can the index actually use them next cycle? — by
setting out the checkable conditions that must hold before pilot account output can enter an
annually reported official statistic, and stating which are unmet.

**Core claim.** Seven conditions must hold for account output to enter the IBEI as a *repeating*
supply, none is currently satisfied in the obtained record, and they are not equally binding: the
spatial and institutional conditions are one-off acts of codification that a single ministerial or
BPS decision could discharge, whereas the two temporal conditions — compilation inside the index's
T-1 reference window, and a revision policy that survives a vintage change — can only be discharged
by sustaining a production cycle, and are therefore the conditions that decide whether §5's mapping
is a recommendation or a wish. Refutable by producing an adopted national marine spatial standard, a
BPS statistical work-programme entry for a marine ecosystem account, or a national extent vintage
with a closing year after 2021.

**Subsections.** 6.1 the supply test and the annual calendar · 6.2 spatial standard and the 12-nm
clipping rule · 6.3 the T-1 window, vintage lag and revision policy · 6.4 institutional entry: BPS
work programme, Satu Data metadata, custodianship · 6.5 the traceability condition, and why coverage
must lead insertion.

**Logical connection.** §5 ends with five indicators whose constituent data items an account already
matches. The reader asks: so use them. §6 answers that a one-off pilot is not a supply, lists what
would make it one, and ends by asking what is worth compiling next — which §7 answers with Tier B,
Tier C and the leverage inversion.
