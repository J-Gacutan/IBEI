## 1. The section plan

### 2.0 Why provincial IBEI compilation is hard

**Purpose.** Establish, from the IBEI's own published documentation and nothing else, that the index
carries a perpetual annual provincial reporting obligation without a compilation infrastructure
underneath it — and that the resulting strain is visible as specific, locatable defects in the
published output, not as commentary.

**Core claim.** The IBEI's published documentation itself records a compilation activity that conducts
no primary collection of its own, no field staff, a one-month secondary-collection window and a
machine-learning gap-filling routine whose per-indicator application is unpublished; and the
published provincial output carries at least five traceability defects — 0.00 sub-pillar scores for
newly created Papuan provinces, a technology sub-pillar of 483.43 on a nominal 1–100 scale, an
underived 0.6819 factor, twelve provincial 2045 targets above the national 2045 target of 100.00,
and a four-versus-three-pillar conflict inside one handbook — each of which is a defect of
provenance rather than of arithmetic, and therefore the kind of defect an accounting production
system is built to prevent.

**Testable against:** anyone who can show these are commentary rather than printed statements, or
that the published record does state which indicators were imputed, or that the two pillar loci are
reconcilable within the Juknis, defeats the section.

### 2.1 A compilation activity without primary collection of its own

**Purpose.** Establish the supply-side constraint from the registered activity design, so the later
defects read as consequences of a design rather than as failures of care.

**Core claim.** SIRUSA registers the IBEI as an administrative-product compilation with zero field
staff and a one-month collection window, which fixes the index's data supply to whatever KKP and
BPS have already published at the resolution and vintage they published it — a constraint of design,
not of effort.

**Key points.**
- Registered as `Statistik Sektoral` with an empty `Identitas Rekomendasi`; implementing agency is
  BAPPENAS's Directorate of Marine Affairs and Fisheries, not BPS. The index is therefore a planning
  ministry's sectoral statistic, not a BPS basic statistic — which is a statement about statutory
  division, not about quality.
- Collection instrument is verbatim *"Lainnya : Publikasi KKP dan BPS"*; mode is
  `Pengumpulan data sekunder`; collection unit is *"Lainnya : Provinsi"*. No pilot survey, no
  non-response adjustment, no staff training, 0 supervisors and 0 enumerators.
- The compilation calendar is narrow and published twice, from two directions: SIRUSA schedules
  collection 1 Apr – 1 May 2025 with dissemination 1 Aug – 1 Sep 2025; the Juknis MEP cycle puts
  Phase 1 (coordination, circular, standard reporting format, contact points) in October–December of
  year *n*. Together these define the window any prospective supplier must hit: an upstream figure
  not published by roughly April of year *n+1* cannot enter that cycle's index. Make this the
  operative statement, because §6 turns it into a condition precedent.
- Reference periods are `T-1` throughout the variable register but `T-2` for variables 12, 13 and 14,
  and are rendered `2024` in a second SIRUSA table for the same variables. A two-year lag sits inside
  a single index vintage.
- The activity records its analysis method as `DESKRIPTIF` and releases no microdata (`Data Mikro:
  Tidak`), while the sub-pillar metadata pages state PCA weighting on first-principal-component
  loading factors. State the tension; do not resolve it.

### 2.2 What the published provincial output shows

**Purpose.** Convert the design constraint into observed, locatable symptoms in BAPPENAS's own
published numbers.

**Core claim.** Four symptoms in the published output — imputation without a per-indicator record,
0.00 sub-pillar scores and collapsing headline values for the new Papuan provinces, a 483.43 reading
on a nominal 1–100 scale, and an underived 0.6819 factor — cannot be interrogated from the published
record, and each is a provenance failure rather than a computational one.

**Key points.**
- The published rule is **machine-learning prediction from socio-economic proxies** (SUSENAS named),
  under two scenarios, with ensemble models and k-fold cross-validation. Under Scenario 1 an
  indicator missing in *all* provinces is trained on the previous cycle and predicted for *every*
  province. Annex 1 names no imputed indicator, no count, no model, no achieved RMSE/MAE/R², no value
  of *k* and no proxy beyond SUSENAS — so a reader cannot tell a measured provincial value from a
  predicted one anywhere in the published tables. That is the load-bearing point; the imputation
  framework itself is competently specified and must not be attacked.
- Papua Barat Daya, Papua Tengah and Papua Selatan each return **0.00** on the marine-based industry,
  technology and governance sub-pillars. Papua Tengah returns the lowest headline value in the annex,
  **8.51**. Against the SEB 2023 baselines the new provinces' 2025 values fall steeply — Papua Barat
  Daya 49.25 → 13.16, Papua Selatan 33.50 → 12.25, Papua Tengah 21.48 → 8.51 — which is a compilation
  signal, not a development signal.
- **Papua Pegunungan** carries a dash in all three Tabel 2 columns: no baseline, no 2025 achievement,
  no 2045 target. Lampiran 2 tabulates 37 provinces; Tabel 2 lists 38 rows plus a national row; the
  MEP chapter states the standardisation principle over *"37 provinsi"*. Say which count is in play
  every time one is used.
- Jakarta's technology sub-pillar reads **483.43**; Aceh 190.37; Bali 147.45; Kepulauan Riau's
  trade/transport/logistics sub-pillar 111.50. The tables are headed *Normalized*.
- The note *"normalized factor sebesar 0,6819"* is printed identically beneath all four regional
  tables with no formula, derivation or point of application.
- Twelve provinces carry a 2045 target above the national 2045 target of **100.00**, the highest
  being Sulawesi Selatan at **275.29**. Report the count as the author's own tally of printed rows.

### 2.3 The specification's own inconsistencies, and this article's pillar locus

**Purpose.** Show that the strain reaches the guidance itself, and discharge the PRD's requirement to
declare a pillar locus before any weight arithmetic is used.

**Core claim.** Two structural contradictions sit inside the published specification — four pillars
against three, and a bounded 1–100 rescale against an unbounded principal-component score on one
printed page — and because the second locus is the only one in which weights are published, this
article computes all weight arithmetic on the three-pillar Tabel 4/5 structure while keeping the
Tabel 1 four-pillar labels for the register, a choice that leaves every Tier A indicator unaffected
and moves only indicator 18.

**Key points.**
- Tabel 1 (pp. 11–13) prints a fourth pillar *Enabler (teknologi & tata kelola)* holding indicators
  15–18, with an unnamed sub-pillar printed as a dash for 17–18. Tabel 4/5 and Gambar 11 (pp. 93–94)
  print three pillars and place tourism (22.1%), technology (4.1%) and governance (15.8%) as
  sub-pillars of Economy. Both are in the same handbook. Neither is adopted by the document.
- **Declared locus:** the three-pillar Tabel 4/5 structure, for the stated reason that weights exist
  only there; register rows retain Tabel 1 labels. **Consequence, stated plainly:** indicators
  19–21, 24 and 27 sit in Environment under both loci, so §4's arithmetic is locus-independent;
  indicator 18 (governance) does move, so every governance figure in §7 carries the locus caveat.
- Report p. 33 asserts, on one page, both `Standardize(index) = (index / Max(index)) * 100` into a
  1–100 range and a principal-component score that is unbounded, may be negative and has a
  cross-province mean near zero. The document does not state which scale the published figures are
  on. This is what makes 483.43 uninterrogable rather than merely surprising.
- Two of the 44 registered variables (17, vessels > 30 GT; 18, ports with TPI) appear in no
  sub-pillar's `Variabel Pembangun` list on any retrieved metadata page; the nine sub-pillar pages
  account for 42. Report as an open record, with the retrieval limitation attached.
- Close on the framing: none of this is incompetence. It is what an annually reported, 44-indicator,
  38-province composite looks like when assembled from secondary publications without a compilation
  infrastructure underneath it.
