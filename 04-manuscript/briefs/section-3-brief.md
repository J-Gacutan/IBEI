## Skeleton

**Core claim.** What makes ocean accounts compilation infrastructure is not the data they hold but
four production properties — a standing unit-and-classification definition, a balancing stock–flow
identity that makes an unexplained change *detectable*, a spatial basis re-aggregable to any
reporting boundary, and documented vintage and revision — none of which the IBEI's own registered
production process has, and each of which is a property of accounting rather than of any dataset.

- **3.1** The four account types and what each produces as a table (extent, condition, services,
  monetary asset). Claim: only extent and condition produce anything the current 44-indicator set
  asks for; services and monetary asset accounts produce quantities the IBEI does not contain at all
  (forward, §7.2).
- **3.2** The stock–flow identity as an error-detection device. Claim: closing = opening + additions −
  reductions makes an unexplained change a *visible residual*; the IBEI's chain has no equivalent
  check, which is why the 0.6819 factor and the imputation record cannot be interrogated.
- **3.3** The spatial basis. Claim: the account's reporting geography is a compiler choice
  (EAA delineation over BSUs), so provincial and district figures come from one compilation — but the
  claim holds only under a documented grid, MMU and clipping rule, which is a condition precedent
  (§6), not an inherent property.
- **3.4** Provenance, vintage and revision. Claim: an account is defined by its metadata as much as
  its numbers; measured-versus-modelled is recoverable from an account and is not recoverable from
  the published IBEI.

that production is repeatable. It does not argue that accounts should supply the index — §§5 and 7 do
that — and it must not imply that account compilation exists for the index's benefit.
