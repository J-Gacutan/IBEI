# Section 4 brief — "What Indonesia has already compiled"

> **Faithful extract.** This brief reproduces the §4 plan and only those cross-cutting rules that bear
> on it. The [SOT skeleton](../PRD-journal-article.md) and the
> [PRD](../PRD-journal-article.md) remain authoritative; where this brief and either differ, they govern.

**Allocation:** 1,200 words ±20% (960–1,440). Expect to draft at the top of the band.

---

## 1. What the section must accomplish

Establish, from primary MMAF and BPS documents in [00-background/](../../00-background/) rather than
from finding 02, exactly what Indonesian ocean accounting has produced: which account types, at what
spatial and temporal coverage, on what methods, with what monetary basis, under what institutional
standing — every claim maturity-tagged. This is the supply side of the mapping in §5, and its honesty
is what licenses §5 to make a definitional-match claim without overstating what exists.

## 2. Core claim

Indonesian ocean accounting has, at pilot scale and tag (b) throughout, produced the one account form
IBEI indicators 19–21 actually require — a cover-class tabulation of ecosystem area measured against
documented reference levels — while every property an annually reported official index needs
(national coverage, a settled periodicity, an exchange-value monetary basis, a standing institutional
custodian, an adopted spatial standard) is absent, and two published government documents give
irreconcilable extent figures for the same pilot.

## 3. Verified from primary documents (safe to assert with these locators)

**Gili Matra pilot (MMAF 2022, *Ocean Accounts of Indonesia — Gili Matra Pilot Study*):**

- MBSU basis, 2,980.84 ha total; 2015 opening / 2021 closing (p. 39 [pdf 53]).
- Full extent account, Table 5 (p. 52 [pdf 66]): coral 259.50 → 247.50 ha; seagrass 76.75 → 102.50;
  mangrove 21.50 → 10.69; additions and reductions itemised (natural expansion, natural regression,
  managed regression). Ecosystem-conversion matrix, Table 6 (p. 53 [pdf 67]).
- Condition methods (pp. 40, 47 [pdf 54, 61]): coral by underwater photo transect (Muttaqin et al.
  2020), CPCe analysis, 50 m line transects; seagrass by Seagrass Watch; mangrove by plot sampling;
  biophysics by Landsat-8 plus in-situ pH/DO and laboratory BOD.
- Condition read against **documented reference bands, not a normalised composite**: coral 16.99%
  live hard cover in 2021, classified against Giyanto et al. (2017) bands ("moderate", 19–35%);
  mangrove classified "good" on density ≥1,500 ind/ha per MoE Decree 201/2004. *This is the Tier 1–2,
  cover-based condition claim, verified.*
- Monetary values **benefit-transferred** (p. 36 [pdf 50] and p. 33 [pdf 47]): coral from Bohol
  (Samonte-Tan et al. 2007) and the Mariana Islands (Beukering et al. 2006); seagrass from Mexico;
  mangrove from Gazi Bay, Kenya (Hoberg 2011) and Bintuni Bay, Indonesia (Ruitenbeek 1992). Totals
  IDR 45.24 bn/yr (2015) → IDR 64.17 bn/yr (2021) (p. 60 [pdf 74]).

**BPS 2023 (Tasriah, Agung, Minarmi & Dulatif, Manila SEEA workshop; and Wulandari & Jannah):**

- Same pilot, **different extent figures**: coral 183.34 → 165.08 ha; seagrass 56.50 → 64.93;
  mangrove 13.53 → 6.15, sourced "BIG (2020), validated 2021" (Gili Matra deck, slide 3).
- 25 m × 25 m MBSU, 48,211 grids; opening/closing chosen "based on data availability"
  (Wulandari & Jannah, slides 11, 14).
- Own stated challenges include "Ocean ecosystem valuation — standard valuation methods not yet
  implemented by relevant stakeholder" and "Scale up — implementing ocean account for the total
  Indonesia marine area" (Gili Matra deck, slide 9).

**Rahayu et al. 2024 (*One Ecosystem* 9: e125578), the only peer-reviewed Indonesian account source:**

- Saleh Bay, West Nusa Tenggara; grid-size optimisation 10 m vs 25 m, hierarchy vs dominance methods;
  states plainly that **"a standardised grid has yet to be established."**

**Kepmen KP 44/2023 (*Tim Penyusun Neraca Sumber Daya Laut*, 16 Feb 2023):**

- Five working groups; environmental-accounts implementation chaired by **BPS** (Direktur Neraca
  Produksi); five named accounts (extent/condition + monetary value; flows to the economy; waste
  flows; ocean economy; governance); BAPPENAS a single member, not a co-lead.
- **Term expired 31 December 2023** (diktum KEEMPAT); no renewal instrument located.

**GOAP country page (snapshot 2026-08-13):** the three 2022 MMAF reports are labelled
**"preliminary consultation drafts"**; national Ocean Accounts Dashboard launched **July 2025**.

## 5. Mandatory formulations (from the standing rules)

- **Every Indonesian ocean account output is tag (b).** There is no BPS ocean account release, no
  statistical work-programme entry, no revision policy.
- **"34 dashboard-selectable provinces and one province with a documented published compilation"** —
  stated together at every point of use. Selectability is not compilation. The one province is **NTB**
  (Gili Matra, North Lombok; Saleh Bay, Sumbawa).
- **Extent is the only account type with a verified opening/change/closing series.**
- **Condition is cover-based Tier 1–2 against documented reference levels**, not a multi-indicator
  composite. Say so precisely: it is both a limit of the account and the reason claim 3 works.
- **Monetary values are benefit-transferred**, therefore not the exchange-value basis SEEA-EA requires.
- **Ecosystems: mangrove, seagrass, coral reef only.**
- **National series 2018/2021; only Gili Matra is 2015/2021.** No implied national 2015 baseline.
- **Maturity tags describe the datum, not the document.**
- Overstating account maturity is the fatal error for this paper.

## 9. Framing boundary

Statistical infrastructure feeding a policy instrument. BAPPENAS plans; MMAF and BPS produce
statistics. Neither institution is under attack — §4 is a capability inventory, not an audit finding.

## 10. Position in the argument

§3 has said what accounts are and why the account *form* is compilation infrastructure. §4 answers
"does Indonesia have any of it?" — and must leave the reader asking "then which indicators can that
actually serve today?", which §5 answers as Tier A.
