## The article in one paragraph

Compiling the IBEI is hard, hardest at provincial scale. Ocean accounts are **compilation
infrastructure** for the index that already exists — a spatially explicit, provenance-documented,
repeatable production system that can supply several IBEI indicators from one compilation cycle at
the spatial unit the index needs. The near-term gain is in *how* the indicators it touches are
produced, not how many it replaces. The headline finding is an **inversion**: the mature account
types address **≈7.64%** of the composite, while the account types bearing on the larger share —
social-classification **≈13.7%** and governance **≈5.15%**, together **≈18.9%** — are the least
developed. That is an inversion of roughly **2.5×**.

## Terms that must be defined at first appearance

The gate found four collisions, each a precision failure rather than a style point.

- **Compilation infrastructure** — the title term, previously never defined. **Definition to use, in
  §3.0:** *a production system that turns primary observation into a statistical output on a repeating
  cycle, carrying its own spatial unit, provenance chain and revision rule.* The claim of the article
  is that ocean accounts are one, and that the IBEI presently has none.
- **Services** — three incompatible uses were found. Fix: **"ecosystem services in physical units"**
  (SEEA-EA supply and use) versus **"benefit-transferred monetary valuation"**. Never the bare word.
  Conflating them is a maturity breach; see standing rules §3.0 item 3.
- **Condition** — reserve for the ecosystem account sense. §6's requirements are **"conditions
  precedent"**, always the full phrase, never shortened to "conditions" in a passage that also
  discusses ecosystem condition.
- **Coverage** — five senses were found. Use **"geographic coverage"** (how many provinces),
  **"register coverage"** (how many of the 44 indicators), and **"cover class"** only for the
  50–100% live-cover threshold in indicators 19–21. Never bare "coverage" beside "cover class".
- **Compilation** — reserve for the act of producing an account or the index. Where the meaning is the
  activity BAPPENAS registers in SIRUSA, say **"the IBEI compilation activity"**.
- **Streamline** — barred from every results sentence and from §9's heading. It appears in framing
  only. The operative claim is **structural**, not an efficiency measurement.

## 1.1 A statutory index without a compilation system

  made responsible for the indicator values themselves — the statutory version of the institutional

# 3.0 What ocean accounts are, and what makes them compilation infrastructure

**Allocation:** 1,000 words (800–1,200).

**Purpose.** Give the reader the minimum account structure needed to follow §§5–7, framed throughout
as an argument about production properties rather than as a SEEA primer, so that "compilation
infrastructure" in the thesis has a concrete referent before any mapping is attempted.

**Core claim.** What makes ocean accounts compilation infrastructure is not the data they contain but
four properties of the production process — a standing unit-and-classification definition, a
balancing stock–flow identity that renders an unexplained change a visible residual, a spatial basis
re-aggregable to any reporting boundary from a single compilation, and documented vintage and
revision — and each is a property of accounting rather than of any particular dataset, which is
precisely why the IBEI's own registered production process, a descriptive annual compilation of
secondary publications with no released microdata, has none of them.

**Key points.**

1. The four SEEA-EA account types are not equally relevant and the section must say which is which:
   extent and condition accounts produce quantities the current 44-indicator register already asks for
   (area, and area by cover class); ecosystem services and monetary ecosystem asset accounts produce
   quantities that appear nowhere in the 44 indicators, which is what makes them §7.2's forward
   register rather than §5's mapping.
2. The stock–flow identity — closing = opening + additions − reductions, with every change attributed
   to a managed or a natural cause — is an error-detection device before it is a reporting convention.
   TG-3.1 states this in its own terms: "This accounting identity is your quality control mechanism:
   it forces you to account for every change" (TG-3.1, Applied/Draft, v6.0, §3). The arguable contrast
   is that the IBEI's published chain has no analogous residual: an unexplained 0.6819 rescaling
   factor and an imputation annex naming no indicator can both sit in a published output without any
   internal check failing.
3. An account measures a stock in physical units against a fixed reference; the IBEI score is
   explicitly relative and has no fixed reference point. SIRUSA's own interpretation field states that
   a lower value, "including negative values", indicates performance lagging *relative to other
   provinces* (`doc_4_sirusa-metadata_chunk_017`). A hectare figure is comparable across years without
   recomputing every other province; a PCA-weighted Z-score is not. This is a production argument, not
   a criticism of PCA, and must be written as such.
4. The spatial claim must be stated more carefully than "accounts aggregate to any boundary by
   design". SEEA-EA 2021 demotes the basic spatial unit to "a means of implementing the ecosystem
   accounting approach rather than as elements in a nested hierarchy" (A1.3); the statistical unit is
   the ecosystem asset and the reporting geography is the compiler-delineated ecosystem accounting
   area. The correct claim is that re-aggregation to provinces and districts is available from one
   compilation *once the grid, the minimum mapping unit and the landward and seaward boundaries are
   documented and held constant* — a condition precedent (§6), not a free property.
5. Grid choice is a measured, not a notional, source of error, and Indonesia's only peer-reviewed
   account paper measures it: Rahayu et al. (2024) test 10 m against 25 m MBSU cells and dominance
   against hierarchy designation in Saleh Bay, and find larger cells produce higher error in both
   total area per ecosystem and spatial configuration, with dominance more accurate but hierarchy far
   cheaper computationally. That is the concrete content of "a documented spatial standard", and also
   why no such standard can be assumed to exist nationally.
6. Provenance is what distinguishes a measured value from a predicted one, and only the account side
   currently carries it. TG-2.12 (Applied/Draft, v8.0) requires the coordinate reference system, the
   minimum mapping unit, temporal consistency across vintages and a stated accuracy tier in the
   metadata record, with the rule that "when accounts compiled at different accuracy tiers are
   aggregated… the lowest tier applies to the aggregate". The IBEI publishes no microdata
   (`doc_4_sirusa-metadata_chunk_009`) and no per-indicator imputation record.
7. The section must pre-empt the strongest objection rather than leave it for a reviewer: every
   property named here could in principle be obtained from a well-documented statistical register
   without compiling any account. The reply is that the properties are bundled — the identity, the
   spatial unit and the metadata are the *same* artefact in an account, produced in one cycle — and
   that no such register exists for Indonesia's marine ecosystem variables, whereas a pilot account
   compilation does (§4). State the objection, give the reply in two sentences, and do not overclaim.
8. One explicit forward disclaimer, so §3 cannot be misread as a capability claim: nothing here
   asserts that Indonesia has compiled any of these accounts. Every account type described here is
   described from SEEA-EA 2021 and the GOAP Technical Guidance; what exists in Indonesia, and at what
   maturity, is §4's subject and is tag (b) throughout.

## 3.3 The spatial basis, and what it does and does not give for free

## 5.1 What Tier A means, and what it is called

# 6.0 Conditions precedent for continued supply

**Allocation:** 1,200 words (960–1,440). Recommends a fifth exhibit, **Table 4 — the
conditions-precedent register** (see word-allocation note).

**Purpose.** Convert §5's definitional match into an operational answer to the reader's next question
— *can the index actually use these five indicators next cycle?* — by setting out the checkable
conditions that must hold before pilot account output can enter an annually reported official
statistic, and stating plainly which are unmet and who would discharge each.

**Core claim.** Seven conditions must hold before account output can serve the IBEI as a repeating
supply rather than a one-off demonstration; none is satisfied in the obtained record; and they are not
equally binding — the spatial, institutional and traceability conditions are one-off acts of
codification that a single MMAF, BPS or BAPPENAS decision could discharge, whereas the two temporal
conditions (compilation inside the index's T-1 reference window, and a revision policy that survives a
vintage change) can only be discharged by sustaining a production cycle, and are therefore what
decides whether §5's mapping is a recommendation or a wish. Refutable by producing an adopted national
marine spatial standard, a BPS statistical work-programme entry for a marine ecosystem account, or a
national extent vintage with a closing year after 2021.

**Key points.**

1. A supply test, not a capability test. The IBEI's obligation is annual and perpetual (Perpres
   12/2025; SEB 2/2024), and its monitoring architecture fixes Phase 1 at October–December of year *n*,
   when BAPPENAS issues a circular carrying the collection timeline, any methodology or definition
   change, and a standardised reporting format. A pilot compilation that cannot be repeated into that
   window is not a candidate data source however well it matches indicator 19–21's constituent data
   item. This is the sentence §5 could not say.
2. The conditions divide into one-off and recurring, and the recommendation turns on the division. A
   spatial standard, a clipping rule, a work-programme entry and a metadata registration are each
   discharged once. Hitting T-1 every year, and revising a published provincial figure when a vintage
   changes, are discharged only by running the cycle.
3. **Condition 1 — an adopted national spatial standard.** TG-2.12 (Applied/Draft) requires one MMU
   applied consistently across ecosystem types within a vintage, across vintages, and across the whole
   accounting area, with any MMU change carrying either a retrospective correction or a quantified
   break-in-series estimate; TG-3.11 (Emerging/Draft) makes grid-based BSUs the route to aggregating
   to any administrative boundary. No adopted national marine grid or MMU is found in any obtained
   Indonesian document, and the published pilot literature records different resolutions at different
   sites. Without it, a provincial figure compiled in year *n* and *n+1* is not comparable, which
   breaches the MEP system's own first principle.
4. **Condition 2 — a codified clipping rule at the provincial limit.** Provincial marine authority
   under Law 23/2014 runs to 12 nm, so an account compiled over an accounting area extending to the
   EEZ must be clipped before it can produce a provincial number. TG-2.12 supplies the UNCLOS zone
   definitions (territorial sea 12 nm, Part II Art. 3) but no sub-national rule; TG-3.11 §3.1 supplies
   the convention — an ecosystem asset spanning a boundary is allocated by physical extent within each
   boundary, and condition indicators are compiled per BSU and aggregated, never inherited whole.
   Neither the rule nor the convention is codified in any Indonesian account. This is the same
   evidence v1 read as an objection, read here as a requirement.
5. **Condition 2a**, stated once and not laboured — the aggregation asymmetry the clipping rule
   exposes. TG-3.11 §3.4 states that extent is additive across sub-national areas but condition is
   not, and prescribes an area-weighted mean. The IBEI asks for a percentage of area in a cover class
   (a ratio), so a provincial value cannot be recovered by averaging district values. Any supplier must
   publish the denominator, not only the percentage.
6. **Condition 3 — compilation inside the T-1 window.** SIRUSA records reference period T-1 for all
   five Tier A variables (19, 20, 21, 24, 27). The published Indonesian national extent series closes
   at 2021 and the observed lag from compilation to public platform is on the order of four years — a
   T-4 supply against a T-1 requirement. TG-4.7 (Applied/Draft) benchmarks a workable cycle at
   preliminary data within 6–12 months and final within 12–24 months of reference-period close. The gap
   is roughly two to three years of cycle time, and it is the single largest distance between what
   exists and what the index needs.
7. The window is not even uniform inside the index. SIRUSA renders variables 12, 13 and 14 at T-2 while
   the rest sit at T-1, and renders the same variables' reference period as T-1 in one table and 2024
   in another. A supplier cannot design to a window the specification states three ways. A
   documentation defect worth one sentence, not a critique.
8. **Condition 4 — a revision policy.** TG-2.12 §3.4.3 requires a designated baseline year, consistent
   methods across vintages, a parallel reclassification of the baseline where a method change is
   unavoidable, and explicit disclosure where the latest extent data refer to *t-1*. TG-0.7 §3.2.2
   makes timeliness a named quality dimension. No revision policy exists for any Indonesian ocean
   account output, and the index's own MEP principle of *adaptabilitas terkendali* presupposes one on
   the supply side. An account silently improved between vintages will move a province's IBEI without
   any real ecosystem change, and no rule would presently flag that.
9. **Condition 5 — a BPS statistical work-programme entry, and the custodianship it settles.** The
   IBEI is registered in SIRUSA as *Statistik Sektoral* with an empty *Identitas Rekomendasi*, compiled
   from administrative products, with BAPPENAS as *pengampu*. Ocean account output is an MMAF ministry
   product with no BPS registration found. Two sectoral products feeding each other is not the same as
   an accredited statistical input, and the article should say which of the three bodies would have to
   move: BPS to register, MMAF to submit, BAPPENAS to accept. Do not propose reassigning any mandate.
10. **Condition 6 — Satu Data Indonesia metadata compliance and a documented handover.** TG-4.7 §3.2's
    six-element checklist — scope and classification, delivery frequency and timeliness, quality
    standards and revision notification, confidentiality regime, exchange format and metadata standard,
    dispute resolution and review cycle — is the minimum content of an arrangement that would make
    supply institutional rather than relational. Note what works against this: SIRUSA releases no
    microdata, so an external party cannot check whether a supplied account value survived into the
    published score.
11. **Condition 7 — a documented per-indicator imputation record**, so measured and predicted values
    can be told apart. Annex 1 publishes the framework but names no indicator as imputed, no count, no
    selected model per indicator and no fitted error. TG-0.7 §3.6's flag set already provides the
    instrument (E = derived from models or benefit transfer, with an aggregate carrying the flag of its
    lowest-quality input), and TG-4.11 §3.5.2 (Applied/Draft) lists the minimum metadata fields for a
    model-derived cell. Low-cost, high-value, and the condition that most directly serves the index's
    own published transparency principle.
12. The coverage-phasing consequence, made as a design recommendation and not a critique. Where a
    province has no account, an account-derived indicator would itself be imputed from socio-economic
    proxies, so an environmental value becomes partly a function of economic and social covariates.
    Under Scenario 1 the model trains on the previous cycle's complete historical data, which a newly
    introduced indicator does not have. Insertion should therefore be phased behind coverage.
13. One line the section must not overreach on. The whole account-addressable share is ~7.6% of the
    composite (ecosystem condition ~2.3%), against a social pillar at 30.5% and a governance sub-pillar **[superseded — use the adopted D2 convention: ≈13.7% classification-dependent, ≈18.9% combined, 2.5×]**
    at ~5% bearing on account types not compiled anywhere. Conditions precedent are cheap relative to
    the index's total exposure but are not bought by the leverage they unlock in Tier A alone — which is
    why §7's forward register matters more than §5's.

## 6.1 What continued supply requires: the annual window

**Purpose.** Establish the test the rest of the section applies, so the conditions read as derivations
from a stated obligation rather than an author's checklist.

**Core claim.** A one-off pilot compilation is not a data source for the IBEI, because the obligation
is annual and perpetual and the monitoring architecture fixes a preparation and coordination phase in
October–December of year *n* against a T-1 reference period; the operative test for any supplier is
repeatability into a dated window, not demonstrated capability.

**Key points.**

- The obligation is statutory and recurring: Perpres 12/2025 and SEB 2/2024, so provinces must be
  scored annually in perpetuity.
- The window is printed, not inferred: MEP Phase 1 runs Oktober–Desember tahun *n*.
- The index has no primary collection of its own, so it can receive only what an upstream producer has
  already released, at the resolution and vintage of that release.
- Two of the index's own five MEP principles are supply-side conditions in disguise: temporal
  continuity, and methodological standardisation guaranteeing 37 provinces aggregate apple-to-apple.
- One sentence on the pillar conflict and no more: a standardised reporting format cannot be issued
  without stating which pillar structure it follows. Recorded, not adjudicated.

## 6.2 Spatial conditions: a national standard and a codified clip

**Purpose.** Convert v1's spatial-aggregation and boundary analysis from an objection into two
specific, dischargeable requirements, and name who would discharge them.

**Core claim.** Provincial account output is not comparable across provinces or across vintages until
a single MMU and BSU are adopted nationally and a clipping rule at the 12-nm provincial limit is
codified; both are one-off acts of codification available to MMAF and the national mapping agency
without any new compilation, and neither is found in any obtained Indonesian document.

**Key points.**

- TG-2.12 §3.3.3 makes MMU consistency binding in three directions and requires a retrospective
  correction or a quantified break-in-series estimate where the MMU changes. Published Indonesian
  pilot work records different resolutions at different sites — the case the rule exists to prevent.
- TG-3.11 makes grid-based BSUs the mechanism by which an account aggregates to any administrative
  boundary — the property §3 claims — but conditional on the grid existing and being national.
- The clipping rule has a statutory line and a methodological convention, and Indonesia has codified
  neither: Law 23/2014 sets provincial marine authority at 12 nm; TG-2.12 §3.2.2 gives UNCLOS zone
  definitions but no sub-national rule; TG-3.11 §3.1 gives the allocation convention.
- Stated once: TG-3.11 §3.4 holds extent additive across sub-national areas and condition not,
  prescribing an area-weighted mean. Indicators 19–21 ask for a ratio, so any supplier must publish
  the denominator alongside the percentage.
- Clean nesting across administrative, WPP and PKKPRL frames is asserted by GOAP and evidenced
  nowhere; it is listed as part of the condition, not assumed as a property.

## 6.3 Temporal conditions: the T-1 window and a revision policy

**Purpose.** Quantify the largest single distance between what Indonesian accounts produce and what
the index requires, and show why it cannot be closed by a methodological decision.

**Core claim.** The T-1 reference period is the binding constraint on Tier A: SIRUSA specifies T-1 for
all five Tier A variables while the published national extent series closes at 2021 with an observed
lag on the order of four years, a gap of roughly two to three years against TG-4.7's 6–12 month
preliminary benchmark — and because no revision policy exists, a silent methodological improvement
between vintages would move a province's IBEI score with no ecosystem change behind it.

**Key points.**

- The requirement is quotable: variables 19, 20, 21, 24 and 27 each carry T-1 in the SIRUSA register —
  the same five §5 matched definitionally.
- The supply is not merely late but a different order of lag. A T-4 supply against a T-1 requirement
  cannot be fixed by scheduling; the compilation cycle itself must shorten, which is a resourcing and
  production question the article cannot cost.
- The index's own window is stated inconsistently — T-2 for variables 12–14, and the same variables
  rendered T-1 in one table and 2024 in another. One sentence, noted because the argument survives
  either rendering.
- A revision policy is not optional under either framework: TG-2.12 §3.4.3's requirements, and the
  IBEI's own MEP principle of *adaptabilitas terkendali*, presuppose exactly this on the supply side.
- The instrument already exists and costs nothing to adopt: TG-0.7 §3.6's flags, where P marks a
  provisional estimate and an aggregate inherits its lowest-quality input's flag.

## 6.4 Institutional conditions: work-programme entry, metadata and custodianship

**Purpose.** Move the register from method to institution, naming which of the three bodies would have
to act on each condition without proposing that any mandate be reassigned.

**Core claim.** Account output cannot enter an official index as a durable input while it remains an
unregistered ministry product feeding an activity itself registered only as sectoral statistics with
an empty recommendation identity; the condition is a documented, metadata-compliant supply arrangement
between MMAF as producer, BPS as statistical authority and BAPPENAS as compiler — and its absence, not
the absence of data, is what makes present supply relational rather than institutional.

**Key points.**

- The published registration facts are the argument: the IBEI is *Statistik Sektoral*, *Kompilasi
  Produk Administrasi*, *Identitas Rekomendasi: -*, BAPPENAS as *pengampu*; Indonesian ocean account
  output is an MMAF product under Kepmen KP 44/2023, with no BPS work-programme entry found.
- Maturity discipline, not softened: every output is tag (b); 34 provinces dashboard-selectable and one
  with a documented published compilation, the pair stated together. A dashboard is a ministry
  product, not a statistical release.
- TG-4.7 §3.2 supplies the minimum content of an arrangement that would make supply institutional.
  Written as a checklist it is a short document, not a programme.
- Satu Data Indonesia metadata compliance is the registration route; if the SDI standard cannot be
  retrieved, state the condition conditionally through TG-4.6 and TG-4.7 rather than asserting its
  content.
- The reproducibility gap runs both ways: SIRUSA releases no microdata, so even a fully compliant
  supplier could not verify that its value survived into the published provincial score.
- The framing line held explicitly: accounts are compiled under MMAF's own statutory mandate and index
  supply is one downstream use. These conditions are what the index would need, not obligations the
  accounts programme owes it.

# 7.0 Mapping: what future compilation could serve

**Allocation:** 1,900 words (1,520–2,280). Carries **Table 3**.

**Purpose.** Extend the indicator mapping beyond the five indicators existing Indonesian accounts
already touch, by showing — against the Juknis's verbatim constituent data items and published
weights, and against named GOAP Technical Guidance circulars with their badges and statuses — which
further parts of the IBEI a future compilation could serve, what accounts would let the index measure
that it currently cannot, and where the index's largest weights and the account programme's least
developed methods coincide.

**Core claim.** Outside the five ecosystem-extent indicators of Tier A, what ocean accounting offers
the IBEI is not substitute data but a classification, boundary and stock–flow standard for data BPS and
MMAF already publish — and the share of the composite whose production depends on such a standard
(~26% across the four economy sub-pillars, ~13.7% of the social pillar's fisheries-domain variables,
~5% governance) is three to five times the ~7.6% that mature, already-compiled account types address,
while being served by the least mature guidance on both sides. The inversion is testable: it fails if
any Tier B or Tier C route can be shown to supply a currently unavailable *number* rather than a
currently unavailable *definition*, or if the mature-route share is shown to exceed the
aspirational-route share once waste flows are correctly assigned.

**Key points.**

1. **Declare the pillar locus in the first paragraph.** §7's arithmetic follows the three-pillar Tabel
   4/5/Gambar 11 structure because that is the only locus in which weights are published; under Tabel
   1's four-pillar structure indicators 15–18 sit in an *Enabler* pillar instead. Every tourism and
   governance figure therefore carries its locus, and the conflict is stated, not resolved
   (`doc_1_cross-slice-conflict_chunk_001`).
2. **Tier B is a boundary problem, not a data problem, and the weights prove it.** The four economy
   sub-pillars TG-3.3 / TG-2.5 / TG-6.7 / TG-6.10 / TG-6.14 bear on carry (15.2+23.6+19.3+22.1)% ×
   32.6% ≈ 26.1% of the composite (Gambar 11 integers 5+8+6+7 = 26). Every constituent data item in
   them — production tonnages, export tonnages, passenger counts, vessel counts, site counts — is
   already published by KKP and BPS. Nothing an account adds is a new observation; what it adds is an
   ISIC/KBLI-consistent industry boundary (TG-3.3 Applied/Draft, §3.4 and Step 1, with ocean ratios of
   1.0 or <1.0) under which those quantities become comparable across provinces and cycles.
3. **A specific and checkable correction to the naive fisheries mapping.** Tabel 6 shows sub-pillar A
   is 83.0% aquaculture (akuakultur 27.3%, tambak 26.3%, rumput laut 29.4%) and only 6.3%
   capture-fisheries production volume. So TG-6.7 *Fisheries Accounting: Integrating Stock Assessment*

   (both Applied/Draft) bear on ≈4.12%. The stock-assessment route is the intellectually interesting
   one and the numerically negligible one; the aquaculture route is the reverse.
4. **The single sharpest Tier C mechanism, stated as an identity rather than an aspiration.**

   Extraction − Sustainable yield)`. Not a better number, a signed one.
5. **Tier C rows each carry their obstacle, and two obstacles are Core-badged guidance rather than
   missing data.** Ecosystem services in physical units (TG-2.4, TG-3.2, both Applied/Draft) — no

   not the enabler. Net extent change as a stock–flow identity (TG-3.1, TG-6.1/6.2/6.3) — recommend as
   a reported companion series, not a 45th indicator, because a signed near-zero-variance variable
   attracts an unstable PCA weight (§8's material; one clause here).
6. **Two Tier C rows the published weights make almost self-evident.** Sub-pillar H is 50.0% of the

   Indonesia's installed marine renewables are minimal, so this is a forward recommendation about
   indicator design. And TG-3.8 *Combined Presentations* (Applied/Draft) is the realistic near-term win

7. **§7.3, first claim — the social pillar is already a social account in all but name, but the share

10. **§7.3 — indicator 27, made once here and not repeated in §5.** Conservation area in hectares is

# 8.0 What account supply does not fix

**Allocation:** 900 words (720–1,080).

**Purpose.** Bound the article's own recommendation by naming, before a reviewer does, the four things
ocean account supply does not fix — and to do it as an extension of the limitations BAPPENAS has
already published rather than as an exposure of them.

**Core claim.** Ocean account supply improves the provenance and repeatability of a small, identified
set of IBEI inputs; it does not repair the index's spatial resolution, its statutory marine boundary,
its variance-driven weighting, or the half of the environment pillar that measures things no ocean
account should be built to measure — and two of those four constraints (cross-sectional variance
weighting, regional heterogeneity) are limitations the IBEI Report itself prints at §3.1.1, so the
article extends BAPPENAS's caveats rather than discovering them.

**Key points.**

1. Report §3.1.1 already concedes four methodological limits — linearity/monotonicity, outlier
   sensitivity, cross-sectional weights that "mungkin saja menjadi tidak relevan untuk tahun-tahun
   mendatang", and a single weight structure applied across a geographically and socio-economically
   heterogeneous archipelago. Account supply touches none of them; it changes the input, not the
   estimator. Saying so first is what makes §8 an extension rather than an attack.
2. The IBEI wants exactly one number per province: the register's own wording for indicators 19–21 ends
   *per provinsi*. Aggregating an MBSU grid to a provincial total therefore discards the within-province
   distribution the account was built to show — an account can tell a province which reef complex is
   degrading, the index can only tell it that its coral percentage fell. The account's diagnostic value
   survives only if the grid is published alongside the indicator, which is a publication-architecture
   recommendation, not a supply one.
3. The 12-nm provincial limit under Law 23/2014 is an administrative clip, not an ecological one, and
   TG-3.11 (Emerging/Draft) concedes the general case in its own text: "marine and coastal ecosystems
   rarely align with administrative boundaries". A reef complex or fish stock straddling the 12-nm line
   is split between a provincial and a national accounting area by legal artefact. Accounts make the
   clip explicit and auditable; they do not make it ecologically meaningful.
4. Insertion of a signed net-extent-change series is blocked by the index's own published weighting
   rule, not by any objection of ours: "indikator yang memiliki variabilitas lebih besar dan berkorelasi
   kuat dengan indikator lain dalam domainnya akan secara alami mendapatkan bobot yang lebih tinggi"
   (p. 29 [pdf 41]). A near-zero-variance, sign-changing annual stock difference across 38 provinces
   would attract a small weight that could swing between cycles, and the published flow diagram adds a
   second hazard: "Reversi nilai indikator (atau drop indikator) jika ditemukan relasi negatif". Hence
   the Tier C recommendation is a reported companion series under TG-3.8 (Applied/Draft), not a 45th
   indicator.
5. The empirical demonstration is already in the published weights: good-condition mangrove area carries
   0.3% of its sub-pillar (Rendah) against conservation area at 22.0% (Tinggi), and coral condition
   returns +0.41 in the sensitivity table, near the bottom of 58 rows. A variable can be measured
   perfectly and still be almost invisible to the composite. Stated as a fact about variance-based
   weighting, and worded so it cannot be mistaken for the withdrawn Z = 0 argument.
6. Two of the index's environmental inputs are not account targets and should not become ones.
   Indicators 22 and 23 are counts of coastal villages with a waste-holding site and with household
   latrines — PODES enumeration of settlements, carrying 18.7% and 20.8% of the resource-quality
   sub-pillar. Sub-pillar H is solar and hydropower capacity and generation at 50.0% of the environment
   pillar; hydropower alone is roughly half of that sub-pillar. Building an ocean account to serve
   either would be an instrument error.
7. A fifth limit is inherited rather than created: where a province lacks a compiled account, an
   account-derived indicator would itself be imputed from socio-economic proxies, and under Scenario 1
   the model trains on the previous cycle's historical data a newly introduced indicator does not have.
   One sentence here; §6 owns the full treatment.
8. The honest summary: with 34 provinces dashboard-selectable but one province carrying a documented
   published compilation, and the national extent series running 2018 opening to 2021 closing,
   everything §5 and §7 recommend rests on tag (b) pilot output.

**Breaks, stated plainly.**

**C1 — §1's forward dependency is real, not notional.** Every account-side sentence in §1 (pilot
status, ecosystem coverage, the 34/1 pair, the 2018/2021 series) is provisional until §4 is drafted
against primary documents. The chain from §1 to §2 holds, but §1 cannot be finished first. The PRD's
drafting order (§5 and §7 first, then §2) already implies this; §1 should be drafted last.

**C2 — §3 hands §4 an objection §4 does not answer.** §3 pre-empts the reviewer's strongest challenge
— that a well-documented statistical register could supply all four production properties without any
account — and states that the reply is empirical and belongs to §4: *no such register exists for
Indonesia's marine ecosystem variables.* §4's skeleton inventories account output and its maturity but
nowhere establishes the absence of an equivalent statistical register. **The objection is raised and
left unanswered across the §3/§4 boundary.** Either §4 must add that finding (with its search
boundary named, per the absence-claim rule), or §3 must drop the promise and answer the objection on
narrower ground.

cross-referring sentence on the explicit assumption that §6.5 carries it in full; §6.5 does carry it,
but §9 also restates it as a counter-move. Three sections touch one argument and none is unambiguously
its owner (D5).

multiplication, §5 reports 7.64% for the Tier A five, and §7 reports that the same headline figure has
been used elsewhere to include waste flows (~11.2%). A reader following the arithmetic across three
sections will meet two different definitions of the same number (D1). This is an arithmetic break, not
a rhetorical one, and it must be fixed before drafting rather than at reconciliation.

**C5 — §7 and §10 do not agree on which social figure the conclusion restates.** §10.1's restatement
is built on ~30.5%; §7.3's decomposition argues for ~13.7% as the classification-standard claim. The
conclusion cannot restate a figure the body has just qualified without saying which reading it uses
(D2).
