1	# Ocean accounts as compilation infrastructure for a blue economy index
2	
3	## Abstract
4	
5	Indonesia is statutorily obliged to score every province annually on a 44-indicator blue economy
6	composite compiled, on its own registration, as sectoral statistics with no primary collection
7	(BAPPENAS 2025). Indonesia's ocean accounts (United Nations et al. 2024) are assessed here as
8	compilation infrastructure for that index rather than as an alternative indicator source.
9	Here, the analysis demonstrates that what accounting offers such an index is a production
10	process, being a standing classification, a balancing identity, a re-aggregable spatial basis
11	(Rahayu et al. 2024) and a documented revision rule, rather than a stock of substitutable values.
12	Reading the register's verbatim constituent data items rather than its indicator names corrects
13	prior assessments: three condition indicators ask for the share of provincial area within a stated
14	cover band, a tabulation of extent by class rather than the index against reference condition they
15	found missing. Supporting that claim, the account types
16	Indonesia has compiled, at one pilot site, carry about 7.6% of the composite's
17	published weight, whilst those bearing on roughly 19% remain methodology under development, both
18	shares contingent on an undocumented ambiguity in how the weights are expressed. What an accounting
19	system can carry for a statutory composite is now a general question.
20	
21	
22	# 1. Introduction
23	
24	Composite indices have become a standard instrument of ocean governance, converting heterogeneous
25	marine statistics into a single number that ministries can target, budget against and report. Their
26	credibility rests less on indicator design than on the production system beneath them, since an index
27	can only be as traceable as the statistics it consumes. That dependency is rarely examined, in part
28	because indices are usually assessed on what they measure rather than on how their inputs are made.
29	
30	Ocean accounting has developed as the candidate infrastructure for that problem. The System of
31	Environmental-Economic Accounting Ecosystem Accounting framework, adopted by the UN Statistical
32	Commission in 2021 as an international statistical standard, supplies the extent, condition, service
33	and asset structures on which such accounts are built (United Nations et al., 2024), and a complete
34	national sequence has been specified in detail (IDEEA Group, 2021). Ocean accounts have been argued
35	to belong in the national statistical apparatus rather than in analysis, on the grounds that recurrent
36	policy reporting requires a production system and not a study (Fenichel et al., 2020), and
37	sub-national compilation across extent, service flows and ocean-economy production has been
38	demonstrated operationally (Gacutan et al., 2022). In Indonesia, the spatial basis such accounts
39	require has been tested empirically, with grid size and designation rule shown to change the extent
40	estimate materially (Rahayu et al., 2024). What none of that work establishes is whether an account
41	compiled to those specifications can carry the reporting load of a statutory index, which is a
42	question about production capacity rather than about accounting method.
43	
44	Indonesia is where the policy demand is most explicit. Its ocean policy lineage runs from the 2017
45	Indonesian Ocean Policy through a development framework (BAPPENAS, 2021) to a blue economy roadmap
46	(BAPPENAS, 2024), and a recent review of that lineage names limited data support among the obstacles to
47	implementation (Wuwung et al., 2024). Quantitative work on Indonesian blue economy indicators remains
48	thin, and Rinaldi et al. (2025) construct their own sub-national index for Sumatera on a human
49	development index template and regress it on regional product, which is the closest existing treatment
50	and is not an analysis of the official index. What has not been examined is the production system
51	beneath that index itself.
52	
53	The Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI) is an official national development indicator under
54	presidential regulation Perpres 12/2025, with provincial targets for 2045 assigned by joint circular
55	of the Ministry of Home Affairs and the Ministry of National Development Planning (BAPPENAS) for
56	carriage in long-term development plans. A province cannot decline to be scored, and BAPPENAS cannot
57	decline to score it. Against that recurring obligation, the compilation activity is registered in
58	Indonesia's statistical activity system (SIRUSA kegiatan 94936) as sectoral statistics gathered by
59	secondary collection from the publications of the Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (MMAF,
60	Indonesian KKP) and of Statistics Indonesia (BPS), so the index conducts no primary collection of its
61	own and inherits whatever those agencies publish, at whatever resolution they publish it. The
62	traceability defects that follow from compiling a 44-indicator provincial composite entirely from
63	secondary publications are legible in the index's own documentation and are documented in Section 2.
64	
65	Here, this study assesses Indonesia's ocean accounts as compilation infrastructure for an index the
66	state is already obliged to produce, rather than as an alternative source of indicator values. The
67	assessment is built on the IBEI's own published documentation, being the 2025 technical handbook
68	(Juknis) and the December 2025 revision of its report, on the primary reports of the Indonesian
69	account pilots, and on the international accounting standard, with every claim tagged for maturity.
70	It contributes a documented account of what makes provincial compilation hard, a three-tier indicator
71	mapping scored against the register's verbatim constituent data items rather than its indicator names,
72	a quantification of where in the index the account-relevant weight sits, and a register of the
73	conditions precedent for continued supply. Two boundaries hold throughout the assessment. Account
74	compilation has its own statutory and analytical purposes under MMAF's mandate, and supplying an index
75	is one downstream use among several, so nothing here proposes replacing the IBEI or subordinating
76	either framework, the relationship examined being statistical infrastructure feeding a policy
77	instrument. The object of study is bounded to the 44-indicator edition of the index, and every
78	Indonesian ocean account output discussed carries maturity tag (b), being experimental or pilot
79	compilation, with no statistical work-programme entry and no revision policy.
80	
81	Three findings follow. Read against verbatim constituent data items rather than indicator names, the
82	three ecosystem condition indicators are definitionally matched to account output rather than
83	category-mismatched, which corrects a judgement in earlier work, including an earlier provisional
84	mapping by the present authors that scored account-derivable shares at figures moving from 61% to 39%
85	to 2.8% as the assumed indicator set changed, the last over a 36-indicator denominator the register
86	does not have (Section 5.2). The account types Indonesia has compiled at pilot scale bear on a small
87	share of the composite's published weight, whilst the types bearing on a share several times larger,
88	being social classification and governance, are the least developed anywhere and are compiled in
89	Indonesia at no maturity above methodology under development (Section 7.3). Neither share describes
90	achievable score improvement, as both describe the share of the index whose production these account
91	types bear on, and what the mature account types offer at those indicators is a production process
92	rather than a stock of substitutable values (Section 9.1).

95	# 2. Why provincial compilation is hard
96	
97	A recurring statutory obligation to score every province annually sits on top of a compilation
98	activity that conducts no primary collection of its own. The obligation is assigned rather than
99	volunteered: Perpres 12/2025 carries the IBEI as an official national development indicator, and
100	joint circular SEB Mendagri/Bappenas No. 2/2024 distributes a 2045 target to each province for
101	carriage in its long-term development plan, with Papua Pegunungan carried at no baseline, achievement
102	or target (Juknis, Tabel 2, p. 69 [pdf 79]). That the compilation beneath it collects nothing
103	directly is a design decision rather than a lapse, it is recorded in Indonesia's own statistical
104	metadata system, and its consequences are legible as specific strains in the published output rather
105	than as commentary. This section establishes both from the IBEI's own documentation, and closes by
106	declaring the pillar structure the rest of this article computes on, since the handbook prints two.
107	
108	## 2.1 Compilation without collection
109	
110	SIRUSA registers the IBEI compilation as *Kompilasi Produk Administrasi* under *Statistik Sektoral*,
111	implemented by BAPPENAS's Directorate of Marine Affairs and Fisheries, with an empty *Identitas
112	Rekomendasi* (kegiatan 94936, retrieved 2026-08-13). The activity design is secondary compilation
113	throughout. Collection mode is *Pengumpulan data sekunder*, the instrument is *Lainnya: Publikasi KKP
114	dan BPS*, the unit is the province, and the register records no pilot survey, no non-response
115	adjustment, no staff training, zero supervisors and zero enumerators. The index's data supply is
116	therefore fixed to whatever KKP and BPS have already published, at the resolution and vintage they
117	published it. The collection capability the index draws on is real and sits with those two producers,
118	which is the statutory division this article's framing holds to: BAPPENAS plans, whilst MMAF and BPS
119	produce statistics. What the register establishes is not an absence of capability in the system but
120	the absence of any collection instrument inside the compilation itself, so the index can acquire
121	nothing that its two suppliers have not already published.
122	
123	The window is narrow and is published from two directions. SIRUSA schedules collection from 1 April to
124	1 May 2025 and dissemination from 1 August to 1 September 2025, whilst the Juknis puts the first phase
125	of its monitoring and evaluation cycle, covering the circular, the timeline, the standard reporting
126	format and contact points, in October to December of year *n* (p. 71 [pdf 81]). Read together these
127	imply that an upstream figure not public by roughly April of year *n+1* cannot enter that cycle's
128	index. They are stated conditionally, as neither document reconciles them and only the first phase was
129	extracted into the corpus `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Reference periods compound the problem in two independent
130	ways. Variables 12 to 14 carry T-2 where the register otherwise carries T-1, so a two-year lag sits
131	inside a single index vintage. Separately, the same reference periods appear twice in the source with
132	different renderings, as T-1 in the numbered variable table and as 2024 in the summary table, so a
133	prospective supplier cannot infer which vintage will be used.
134	
135	## 2.2 What the published output records
136	
137	Two defects carry the argument, and both are failures of provenance rather than of arithmetic.
138	
139	Gaps are filled by prediction, and the prediction is not recorded. Annex 1 of the Report specifies a
140	machine-learning framework predicting missing values from socio-economic proxies under two scenarios,
141	with SUSENAS named as the proxy source (pp. 64–66 [pdf 76–78]). The framework itself is
142	specified in detail, and what is missing is downstream of it: the annex names no imputed indicator, no count of imputed
143	cells, no selected model, no achieved error, and no proxy beyond SUSENAS. Under the first scenario an
144	indicator absent in every province is predicted for every province. No reader of any published table
145	can therefore distinguish a measured provincial value from a predicted one, and which indicators were
146	imputed is unrecoverable from the published record, the annex naming none of them `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
147	It must not be inferred from which indicators look hard to collect.
148	
149	Three provinces record no value across three sub-pillars. Papua Barat Daya, Papua Selatan and Papua
150	Tengah, three of the four provinces created in Papua in 2022, each record 0.00 for marine-based
151	industry, technology and governance, and Papua Tengah's headline value of 8.51 is the lowest in the
152	annex (Report p. 70 [pdf 82]). Juknis Tabel 2, reproducing the SEB values, carries the same three
153	provinces at 2023 baselines of 49.25, 33.50 and 21.48 against 2025 achievements of 13.16, 12.25 and
154	8.51 (p. 69 [pdf 79]), the two documents heading those columns differently. Whether the zeros reflect
155	data unavailability or a measured zero is not stated in either document, so they are reported as
156	printed and read as consistent with a compilation discontinuity following the 2022 provincial split
157	rather than as proof of one `[ASSUMPTION]`. This is where a new administrative unit needs a spatially
158	re-aggregable source rather than a republished provincial total (Section 3).
159	
160	Three further symptoms are recorded in Table 1 rather than argued here, being an underived rescaling
161	factor printed beneath all four regional tables, sub-pillar values far above the nominal scale on
162	tables headed *Normalized*, and provincial 2045 targets above the national target. Each is
163	uninterrogable from the published record, and none can be resolved without documents this study could
164	not obtain.
165	
166	The denominator of the obligation is itself unstable inside the guidance that creates it. Indonesia
167	has 38 provinces, whilst the Report's Annex 2 tabulates 37, Tabel 2 of the Juknis lists 38 rows plus a
168	national row with Papua Pegunungan carrying a dash in all three columns, and the monitoring chapter
169	asserts comparability over *37 provinsi* (p. 71 [pdf 81]). The number used in any sentence here is the
170	number that sentence is about.

173	
174	The handbook prints two pillar structures, four in Tabel 1 with an *Enabler (teknologi & tata kelola)*

176	not resolve it: all weight arithmetic here follows the three-pillar structure, for the stated reason

212	# 3. What makes accounts infrastructure
213	
214	Compilation infrastructure, as the term is used here, means a production system that turns primary
215	observation into a statistical output on a repeating cycle, carrying its own spatial unit, provenance
216	chain and revision rule. What makes ocean accounts compilation infrastructure is therefore not the
217	data they contain but four properties of the production process: a standing unit and classification
218	definition, fixed by the account types themselves, a balancing identity that renders an unexplained
219	change a visible residual, a spatial basis re-aggregable to any reporting boundary from one
220	compilation, and documented vintage and revision. The four-property scheme is this study's synthesis
221	rather than a construct the standard names as such, assembled from provisions the standard does state
222	separately, being the account structures and their balancing identity (United Nations et al., 2024,
223	paras 4.14–4.17, pp. 86–88), the treatment of basic spatial units and accounting areas (App. A1.1,
224	para. A1.3), and the consistency and revision requirements (para. 1.60, p. 18). Each is a property of
225	accounting rather than of any particular dataset. Those account types also divide against the register along a line that organises
226	the rest of this article (Table 2), as extent and condition produce quantities the 44 indicators
227	already demand whilst ecosystem services and monetary asset accounts produce quantities appearing
228	nowhere among them.
229	
230	The balancing identity is where the production argument lives. Closing stock equals opening stock plus
231	additions less reductions, which makes an unexplained movement fail to balance rather than pass
232	unnoticed. The extent account adds a further discipline, separating additions into managed and
233	unmanaged expansion and reductions into managed and unmanaged reduction, so that the nature of a
234	change and the policy response available to it are recorded rather than inferred (United Nations
235	et al., 2024, paras 4.14–4.15, p. 86), and so that an addition reported without its concurrent
236	reduction states gross planting where a reader would infer net gain. The contrast with the index is one of production
237	rather than arithmetic, as its standardisation and principal-component weighting are specified in
238	detail and what is absent is any step at which an inconsistency would be forced to surface (Section
239	2). The measured quantity differs too. An account reports a stock in physical units against a stated
240	reference, whilst SIRUSA records that a lower index value, including a negative one, indicates
241	performance lagging relative to other provinces (SIRUSA, indikator 105371, Interpretasi, retrieved
242	2026-08-13). A hectare figure is comparable across years without recomputing every other province, and
243	a weighted standardised score is not.
244	
245	The spatial property needs stating more carefully than it usually is. Basic spatial units retain their
246	place in SEEA Ecosystem Accounting but are "regarded as a means of implementing the ecosystem
247	accounting approach rather than as elements in a nested hierarchy" (SEEA EA 2021, App. A1.1, para.
248	A1.3), the ecosystem asset being the statistical unit and the reporting geography a compiler-delineated
249	accounting area. Provincial and district figures are accordingly two aggregations of one production
250	run rather than two data requests, but only where the grid, the minimum mapping unit, the landward and
251	seaward boundaries and the vintage are documented and held constant. That is a conditional property of
252	a well-specified account rather than an inherent property of accounts, and Rahayu et al. (2024)
253	measure what getting it wrong costs (Section 5.2). Whether cell boundaries nest cleanly within
254	provincial, fisheries-management and marine-spatial-planning frames is asserted rather than evidenced
255	`[ASSUMPTION]`, and no adopted national grid standard has been located `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Both are
256	conditions precedent (Section 6).
257	
258	Provenance is the property that speaks to traceability. An account is compiled to be consistent over
259	time and with respect to concepts and classifications (United Nations et al., 2024, para. 1.60,
260	p. 18), which requires the mapping
261	inputs, their vintage and their spatial resolution to be documented and held constant, so that patchy
262	coverage becomes reportable rather than concealable and a measured value stays distinguishable from a
263	modelled one after aggregation, where the index publishes no microdata and no per-indicator imputation
264	record (Section 2). Revision is likewise a property of the account form, the standard requiring
265	previous estimates to be revised where updated imagery, methods or classifications permit a
266	reassessment, with no distinct revision entry recorded and the size of the revision available as the
267	difference between the historical and the revised account for the same period (para. 4.17, p. 88).
268	
269	A reviewer may object that a well-documented statistical register could supply all four properties
270	without any account being compiled, and on two of the four the objection holds outright. A standing
271	unit and classification definition is an act of codification, requiring a published definition of the
272	mapping unit, the ecosystem classes and the landward and seaward limits, and nothing about compiling
273	an account is needed to write one down. Documented vintage and provenance is likewise a metadata
274	requirement that any well-run statistical register discharges, and the spatial property is
275	intermediate, since a register can mandate a grid and a minimum mapping unit but cannot by itself
276	produce the re-aggregable cell-level tabulation the mandate presupposes. What does not reduce to
277	codification is the residual: the balancing identity, which forces an unexplained movement to appear
278	as a failure to balance rather than as a revised level, and the revision rule that alters the opening
279	and closing entries themselves rather than adding a separate revision line, so that the size of a
280	revision is recoverable by difference. Those two are properties of a compiled account because they are
281	properties of double-entry over a stock, not statements about a stock. The residual is deliberately
282	narrow, and the article's claim is correspondingly narrow, that the near-term contribution of accounts
283	at the indicators they touch is that residual and the production cycle carrying it, rather than a
284	volume of otherwise unavailable values (Section 9.1). Nothing here asserts an Indonesian capability.

300	# 4. What Indonesia has already compiled
301	
302	Indonesian ocean accounting has produced a compiled record small enough to inventory item by item,
303	carrying a maturity tag on every claim, where the tag describes the datum rather than the document
304	reporting it. Two findings follow, and they pull against each other. That accounting has produced the
305	measurement basis IBEI indicators 19, 20 and 21 are defined over, being ecosystem area on a spatial
306	grid with cover measured against documented reference levels, whilst it has not produced the form the
307	index specifies, which is the share of area falling in a cover class rather than a mean cover value.
308	It has produced that basis once, in one marine protected area, in one province, and the properties an
309	annually reported official index requires of a supplier are not yet evidenced in the obtained record.
310	
311	## 4.1 The compiled record
312	
313	The compiled record comprises one pilot in a marine protected area, being Gili Matra Marine
314	Recreational Park off north Lombok, together with one peer-reviewed grid-optimisation study at Saleh
315	Bay, Sumbawa (Rahayu et al., 2024). Both sit in West Nusa Tenggara, so the single province carrying a
316	documented published compilation located by this study is NTB. A national Ocean Accounts Dashboard was
317	launched in July 2025 and is described by GOAP as publicly available and interactive on a harmonised
318	spatial frame (GOAP country page, retrieved 2026-08-13), a programme self-statement about a platform
319	rather than a statistical release, and one that evidences no compilation beyond the pilot record
320	below.
321	
322	The pilot's footprint belongs in its own units, as the smallness is part of the finding: 2,980.84 ha
323	on a 25 m by 25 m marine basic spatial unit (MBSU) grid of 48,211 cells, opening in 2015 and closing
324	in 2021, those years chosen on data availability (MMAF, 2022, pp. 30, 39 [pdf 44, 53]; Wulandari and
325	Jannah, 2023, slide 11). The two figures do not reconcile arithmetically, as 48,211 cells at 625 m2
326	each is 3,013.19 ha against the 2,980.84 ha printed, a shortfall of 32.35 ha or 1.1% that would be
327	consistent with cells clipped at the park boundary but is nowhere explained in the report, and is
328	recorded here as observed rather than resolved `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Against a statutory obligation to score every province annually (Section 2),
329	that is an area smaller than many single districts, compiled once.
330	
331	Maturity is the programme's own self-description rather than an external verdict. GOAP labels the
332	three 2022 MMAF outputs 'preliminary consultation drafts', and BPS lists scaling up to the total
333	Indonesian marine area among its outstanding challenges (Tasriah et al., 2022, slide 9). Every
334	Indonesian ocean account output discussed here therefore carries maturity tag (b), experimental or
335	pilot compilation. There is no BPS ocean account release, no entry in the national statistical work
336	programme, no published revision policy, and no published compilation cost per site or per unit, which
337	constrains what may responsibly be recommended (Sections 6 and 10). The ecosystem scope is confined to
338	mangrove, seagrass and coral reef.
339	
340	## 4.2 One maturity, three completenesses
341	
342	The claim that Indonesia has ocean accounts decomposes into three claims of unequal strength. All
343	three account types carry the same maturity tag (b), and what separates them is completeness of the
344	account form rather than the standing of the compilation producing it.
345	
346	Extent is the only account type with a verified opening, change and closing series. The pilot
347	publishes a complete asset account (MMAF, 2022, Table 5, p. 52 [pdf 66]) in which coral falls from
348	259.50 ha to 247.50 ha, seagrass rises from 76.75 ha to 102.50 ha and mangrove falls from 21.50 ha to
349	10.69 ha, with additions and reductions itemised as natural expansion, natural regression and managed
350	regression, and with an ecosystem-conversion matrix at Table 6 (p. 53 [pdf 67]). This is the stock and
351	flow identity in Indonesian practice rather than asserted from the SEEA Ecosystem Accounting standard.
352	
353	Extent is also where two official series diverge. A BPS presentation reports the same pilot,
354	ecosystems and years throughout with coral falling from 183.34 ha to 165.08 ha, seagrass rising from
355	56.50 ha to 64.93 ha and mangrove falling from 13.53 ha to 6.15 ha (Tasriah et al., 2022, slide 3).
356	Both series trace to one layer, being the Geospatial Information Agency's 2020 mapping validated by
357	2021 field survey, which MMAF overlaid on the MBSU grid with 194 verification points (pp. 23, 30 [pdf
358	37, 44]) and which BPS cites as BIG (2020), validated 2021. Neither document states its relationship
359	to the other and no reconciliation is published, so the discrepancy stands as an open conflict between
360	two official documents, neither averaged nor adjudicated here `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
361	
362	The divergence is not a constant offset, which is the property that matters for an account. Taking the
363	ratio of the BPS figure to the MMAF figure ecosystem by ecosystem and year by year gives coral 0.707
364	in 2015 against 0.667 in 2021, seagrass 0.736 against 0.633 and mangrove 0.629 against 0.575, so the
365	ratio moves both across ecosystems and across the accounting window and cannot be explained as a
366	single boundary clip, a units convention or a fixed classification threshold. The consequence is that
367	the two series disagree on the change and not merely on the level, MMAF recording a coral loss of
368	12.00 ha over the window against a BPS loss of 18.26 ha. Change is the quantity a balancing identity
369	exists to report, so the one compiled Indonesian account has produced two closing stocks and two
370	deltas for the same site, and that fact is carried into the limitations and interrogated against this
371	article's own provenance claim (Sections 8.5 and 9.1).
372	
373	Condition is compiled in a limited form, being cover-based measurement at Tier 1 to Tier 2 read
374	against documented reference bands rather than a normalised multi-indicator composite. Coral was
375	measured by underwater photo transect with CPCe analysis following Muttaqin et al. (2020), seagrass by
376	the Seagrass Watch protocol and mangrove by plot sampling (MMAF, 2022, pp. 31–33 [pdf 45–47]). Values
377	are classified against published reference levels, with 16.99% live hard coral cover in 2021 read
378	against the bands of Giyanto et al. (2017), and mangrove classified as good at a density of at least
379	1,500 individuals per hectare under Ministry of Environment Decree 201/2004 (pp. 40, 47 [pdf 54, 61]).
380	That the pilot measures cover as a value against a documented reference level, rather than scoring it
381	as a composite, is both a limit of the account and what leaves the register's class-tabulation form
382	within reach (Section 5.2).
383	
384	Two facts are recorded here without adjudication. The pilot reports mean cover by zone rather than
385	share of area by class, so producing indicators 19, 20 and 21 from it would require re-tabulating the
386	underlying transect and grid data to a different class boundary `[ASSUMPTION]`. And 16.99% cover is
387	reported, then classified as moderate against a band running from 19% to 35%, a tension recorded as
388	observed and unexplained.
389	
390	Valuation is the least developed of the three and may not be described as an ecosystem services
391	account in physical units. What exists is
392	benefit-transferred monetary valuation of ecosystem functions at pilot scale rather than supply and
393	use of ecosystem services in physical units. Coral values are transferred from Bohol (Samonte-Tan et
394	al., 2007) and the Mariana Islands (van Beukering et al., 2006), seagrass from Mexico, and mangrove
395	from Gazi Bay, Kenya (Hoberg, 2011) and Bintuni Bay, Indonesia (Ruitenbeek, 1992), giving pilot totals
396	rising from IDR 45.24 bn per year in 2015 to IDR 64.17 bn per year in 2021 (pp. 36, 60 [pdf 50, 74]).
397	Benefit transfer is not the exchange-value basis the SEEA Ecosystem Accounting standard requires, so
398	no exchange-value or depreciation claim here can rest on it, a limit BPS states itself in listing the
399	absence of implemented standard valuation methods among its challenges (Tasriah et al., 2022, slide
400	9). On this evidence, no ecosystem services account in physical units has been compiled in Indonesia.
401	
402	## 4.3 Institutional and spatial standing
403	
404	A national index would require of an account supplier a standing custodian carrying a periodicity and
405	a revision policy, together with an adopted spatial standard. Both are unsettled on the primary
406	record, and both on the programme's own instruments rather than by inference from silence.
407	
408	Kepmen KP 44/2023, which constituted the Tim Penyusun Neraca Sumber Daya Laut on 16 February 2023,
409	cuts both ways. It establishes five working groups and names five accounts, covering ecosystem extent
410	and condition with monetary value, flows to the economy, waste flows, the ocean economy and
411	governance, and it places the BPS Director of Production Accounts in the chair of environmental
412	accounts implementation (Pokja C), with BAPPENAS a single member of the governance working group
413	(Pokja D) rather than a co-lead. Its own diktum KEEMPAT set the team's term to expire on 31 December
414	2023, and no renewal instrument was located. The July 2025 dashboard launch indicates continued
415	activity under some instrument not obtained, so the defensible statement is that no renewal instrument
416	was located rather than that the mandate lapsed `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
417	
418	Rahayu et al. (2024), the only peer-reviewed Indonesian account source, state that a standardised grid
419	has yet to be established. Grid choice changes the extent tabulation itself, which makes an adopted
420	grid a substantive prerequisite for supply rather than a technical detail (Section 6).
421	
422	One bounded search result is reported on what this study read rather than on an exhaustive survey. The
423	searched set was assembled between 11 and 16 August 2026 and comprises the MMAF 2022 series, the BPS
424	materials prepared for the SEEA workshops, Rahayu et al. (2024), Kepmen KP 44/2023, the GOAP country
425	page (retrieved 2026-08-13) and the SIRUSA metadata for the IBEI compilation activity (kegiatan 94936
426	and indikator 105371, both retrieved 2026-08-13), searched bilingually on the Indonesian terms *neraca
427	laut*, *akun laut*, *neraca ekosistem*, *unit spasial dasar laut*, *peta jalan ekonomi biru* and *Satu
428	Data Indonesia* together with their English equivalents, with no date restriction applied. Within that
429	set, no Indonesian statistical register supplying provenance, spatial unit, vintage and revision
430	policy for marine ecosystem extent and condition products, separately from an account structure, was
431	encountered `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. No dedicated search of the BPS statistical catalogue or of the Satu Data
432	Indonesia portal was undertaken, and no engagement with any Indonesian agency was sought, so the
433	statement is bounded to the named set and is not a claim about the Indonesian statistical system.
434	
435	Three properties remain unestablished by this inventory: national coverage, an annual cycle capable of
436	meeting the index's T-1 reporting window, and a documented custodial chain. What the record supports
437	is narrower than a claim that supply exists, being a method demonstrated in one province at pilot
438	scale, on one vintage, with cover-based condition and benefit-transferred money.

443	# 5. Mapping: the compiled accounts
444	
445	Of the register's 44 rows, five are the points at which an ocean account touches the IBEI at all.
446	Three of them, indicators 19, 20 and 21, name quantities that an account's output structure already
447	contains, whilst indicators 24 and 27 sit adjacent to account output and are disposed of here rather
448	than claimed. Which rows qualify is established by a membership test applied to the verbatim
449	constituent data items of the register, not to indicator names, indicator names having proved an
450	unreliable guide in earlier assessments.
451	
452	## 5.1 What Tier A means
453	
454	The unit of analysis is the register row, of which there are 44, and the object tested is not the row's
455	title but the *Data Pembentuk Indikator* printed against it, the constituent data items that specify
456	what must be measured. Across the 44 rows those items decompose into 62 distinct constituent data
457	items, some rows naming one and others several (Juknis, Tabel 1, pp. 11–13 [pdf 21–23], this study's
458	count over the register as printed). The test is applied to all 62 in Indonesian, and grades each row
459	on one of three grounds. A row is *matched* where a constituent data item names a quantity that a
460	defined SEEA Ecosystem Accounting account output contains, which yields indicators 19, 20 and 21,
461	n = 3. A row is *partial* where account output supplies a component of the quantity but not the
462	quantity itself, which yields indicator 24, n = 1. A row is a *provenance question* where account
463	output bears on the row without supplying it, which yields indicator 27, n = 1. Tier A is the union of
464	the three grades and has five members out of 44. Every other row is scored none, on the ground that no
465	constituent data item in it names a quantity any account output contains.
466	
467	Membership is therefore a claim about correspondence between two published specifications, being the
468	index's register and the SEEA Ecosystem Accounting output structure, and it carries no claim about
469	compilation state. The distinction matters because a label such as 'can serve now' collapses the two
470	axes, asserting a production capability from a definitional property.
471	
472	On the second axis the concession is complete. Every Indonesian ocean account output carries maturity
473	tag (b) (Section 4.1), and none has published any of the five indicator values, for any province or
474	any site `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Every capability claim here is accordingly a claim about definitional
475	correspondence and a demonstrated input layer, never about an existing output. The tier is named on
476	the first axis and conceded on the second at each use: Tier A, definitionally matched, compilation
477	demonstrated at pilot scale. All five rows sit in the environment pillar under both pillar loci, so
478	the unresolved conflict does not reach this section's arithmetic.
479	
480	What the test yields is a correction of the literature rather than a capability claim. Earlier
481	assessments judged that Indonesia lacks the condition account indicators 19, 20 and 21 require. Those
482	indicators do not require one, so the judgement answers a question the index does not pose, and the
483	barrier at these rows is compilation and geographic coverage rather than measurement target.
484	
485	## 5.2 The cover-class match, and its frays
486	
487	The three condition indicators share one grammatical form. Indicator 19 asks for the *Persentase
488	kawasan terumbu karang dengan tutupan karang hidup antara 50-100% per provinsi*, indicator 20 for the
489	*Persentase kawasan lamun dengan tutupan lamun hidup antara 50-100% per provinsi*, and indicator 21
490	for the *Persentase kawasan mangrove dengan kerapatan tutupan antara 50-100% per provinsi* (Juknis,
491	Tabel 1, p. 12 [pdf 22]). Each names a percentage of area falling within a stated cover or density
492	band, per province, which is a tabulation of extent by class rather than a normalised index against a
493	reference condition.
494	
495	What the pilot sets beside that specification is the input layer and not the tabulation. The MBSU
496	extent surface exists (Section 4.1), and cover is measured at survey stations, giving live coral cover
497	of 18.11% in 2016 and live hard coral cover of 16.99% in 2021, which the report does not state to be
498	the same measure and which are therefore not read here as a series, and seagrass cover averaging
499	32.28% with a station maximum of 82.01% (MMAF, 2022, pp. 40, 45 [pdf 54, 59]). The 2021 coral figure is
500	unreconciled in the source, where the narrative reports 16.99% whilst the report's own Table 3 on the same page prints
501	an average of 18.36%. The cross-tabulation of extent by cover class is not published, and the step
502	from the input layer to indicator 19 is one tabulation that has not been performed.
503	
504	The match frays at four edges, and the first two are definitional. The 50% threshold is the index's
505	own and matches no Indonesian condition class boundary in use, as the pilot classifies reef condition
506	on the bands of Giyanto et al. (2017), where moderate runs from 19% to below 35% live hard coral.
507	Reclassifying a continuous per-cell cover surface at 50% would be arithmetically trivial, but that
508	holds only where condition is mapped wall to wall per grid cell rather than surveyed at stations, and
509	no obtained document evidences a wall-to-wall condition surface `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Where national
510	condition data exist only as classes pre-binned on the Giyanto boundaries, the 50% cut cannot be
511	recovered, and the coral row becomes a definition gap rather than a coverage gap `[ASSUMPTION]`. The
512	mangrove unit differs outright: indicator 21 asks for canopy density as a percentage, whilst the pilot
513	reports mangrove canopy cover in m² per ha and stem density in individuals per ha, with no documented
514	conversion between them (p. 47 [pdf 61]) `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
515	
516	The second two edges are unfixed choices. Neither the minimum mapping unit nor the denominator is
517	fixed by the register or by the SIRUSA definitions, so whether the percentage is taken over mapped
518	ecosystem extent or over total provincial marine area remains a definitional residue `[ASSUMPTION]`.
519	And the extent estimate is itself a function of the grid rule, as Rahayu et al. (2024) report mangrove
520	error of 44.06% under a 25 m hierarchy scheme against 1.59% under 25 m dominance. Until a grid rule is
521	adopted, the account's figure is a family of figures, and the index would be importing a
522	methodological choice.
523	
524	A vintage gap sits alongside the definitional ones, as the only verified account series closes in 2021
525	against the index's T-1 requirement and its unsettled rendering (Section 2.1).
526	
527	Restated after those concessions, the correction narrows and holds. The measurement task reduces from
528	constructing reference-condition norms for three ecosystems in every province to classifying cover per
529	cell against a single threshold, which is a smaller object to build, and the wall-to-wall condition
530	surface it needs remains unbuilt. Where these three values are currently predicted under the
531	imputation framework the Report publishes (pp. 64–66 [pdf 76–78]), supplying them from an account
532	would replace a prediction with a measurement, though which indicators were imputed, for which
533	provinces, is not recoverable `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
534	
535	## 5.3 An addition term and a designation
536	
537	Indicator 24 asks for the *Total luas kawasan mangrove, rawa, dan lahan gambut yang direstorasi per
538	provinsi*, glossed in SIRUSA as degraded area subsequently replanted, which is an activity input
539	measured in hectares planted. The pilot's asset account carries a managed-expansion line in its
540	structure that is unpopulated at Gili Matra, where every addition is natural expansion and the only
541	managed entry is 11.19 ha of managed mangrove regression (MMAF, 2022, Table 5, p. 52 [pdf 66]). The
542	correspondence at this row is therefore structural rather than demonstrated: an account of this form
543	would record realised managed change, whilst the indicator counts hectares planted. The scope
544	mismatch compounds it, as the indicator's three land covers are mangrove, swamp and peatland against
545	the marine account's mangrove, seagrass and coral reef, so even complete national coverage would leave
546	indicator 24 partly unserved.
547	
548	The pilot's seagrass row illustrates what the distinction buys. Of the 51.50 ha gained, 8.25 ha came
549	from former reef and 43.25 ha from other substrate, against 25.75 ha lost to coral and to other
550	substrate. A hectares-planted count cannot see either side of that ledger, whilst the account's change
551	matrix records which is which.
552	
553	Indicator 27 asks for the *Total luas kawasan konservasi perairan yang ditetapkan oleh KKP, KLHK, dan
554	Pemerintah Daerah per provinsi*, and is in the tier for a different reason again. At 22.0% it is the
555	largest single variable in the resource-quality sub-pillar, about 4.06% of the composite, and it needs
556	no account to be produced: designated area is an administrative datum already held by its custodians
557	and broken out in SIRUSA as nationally managed, regionally managed and KLHK-managed areas. The
558	account's near-term contribution here is reconciliation, being the attachment of mapped ecosystem
559	extent to the designated polygon as the pilot does by zone, and not supply.
560	
561	## 5.4 How the gaps sort
562	
563	Sorted by the kind of gap that remains, the tier splits three ways (Table 3). Indicators 19 and 20 are
564	coverage and vintage rows on sound definitions, implying a compilation programme beyond the pilot
565	sites and inside the index's reporting window (Section 6). Indicators 21 and 24 are
566	definition rows no amount of coverage will close, the first on units and the second on the difference
567	between an activity and an outcome, implying threshold, unit and denominator rules agreed with the
568	index's custodian. Indicator 27 is not a gap but a provenance question. A fourth gap kind, the spatial
569	standard, is cross-cutting rather than attaching to any row, and implies an adopted national grid and
570	clipping rule. That sort, and not the tier's total weight, is what tells a compiler what to do next.
571	
572	The weight arithmetic is shown openly as a multiplication, carried to two decimal places from the
573	one-decimal percentages the handbook prints and reported in prose to one decimal with 'about', a
574	convention held throughout the article. The environment pillar carries 36.9% of the composite and the
575	resource-quality sub-pillar 50.0% of the pillar (Juknis, Tabel 4 and Tabel 5, p. 94 [pdf 104]), giving
576	18.45% of the composite, of which the five Tier A variables sum to about 7.64% (Table 3). That total
577	is tagged `[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]` throughout this article, and the tag names one specific objection
578	rather than the pillar conflict, which does not reach this arithmetic: 7.64% is a defensible measure of
579	the share of the index these account types bear on, and an indefensible measure of the share an
580	account would supply, because most of it sits at rows the mapping has itself disqualified from supply.
581	The decomposition is therefore reported in three parts, as the tier's own sort requires: about 2.33%
582	at the three matched condition rows, about 1.25% at indicator 24 as a partial, and about 4.06% at
583	indicator 27, which the account does not supply. The share at which an account is the prospective
584	supply route is accordingly about 2.33%, rising to about 3.58% only if indicator 24's structural
585	correspondence is credited in full, which the activity-against-outcome mismatch and the swamp and
586	peatland scope gap indicate it should not be (Section 5.3). The lower figure is the operative one and
587	the upper an outer bound. Whether the printed percentages are raw first principal component loadings
588	or normalised contributions is not stated, so these products are indicative rather than exact
589	`[ASSUMPTION]`, and the Tinggi, Sedang and Rendah bands are the handbook's own rank-based
590	simplification. None of these figures travels alone, as the account types bearing on the larger shares
591	of the index, being social classification and governance, are the least developed anywhere, and are
592	priced in Section 7.3.
593	
594	Under the published weights that share is small, so substituting account-derived values would be
595	unlikely to move the composite materially, though weights derived by principal components are
596	re-estimated from the data, so a substitution is not weight-invariant.
597	
598	Neither reading is the finding. What changes at these rows is how the numbers are made. A cover-class
599	percentage compiled from a gridded extent surface and a classified cover layer carries its own spatial
600	unit, its own opening and closing stocks, and a provenance chain back to the imagery and field survey
601	behind it, and it is reproducible in the next cycle by repeating the compilation. A value taken from a
602	secondary publication, or predicted where no publication exists, carries none of those properties, and
603	the published record does not distinguish the two (Section 2). That is a production-process claim
604	about five rows, not a claim about the index's score. Nothing here proposes that accounts be compiled
605	in order to serve the index, and nothing moves an indicator out of its custodian's hands, on which the
606	register is silent in a way worth recording: no producing agency is stated for any of the 44 rows
607	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`.

625	# 6. Conditions precedent for supply
626	
627	A definitional match is not a supply arrangement. Five conditions must hold before pilot account output
628	could serve the IBEI as a repeating input rather than a single demonstration, none is satisfied in the
629	obtained record, and they are not equally binding (Table 4). Three are one-off acts of codification
630	that a single decision by MMAF, BPS or BAPPENAS could discharge. Two are discharged only by sustaining
631	a production cycle, and those two are what decide whether the mapping in Section 5 is a recommendation
632	or a wish. The register would be refuted by producing an adopted national marine spatial standard, a
633	BPS statistical work-programme entry for a marine ecosystem account, or an extent compilation covering
634	more than the pilot sites and closing after 2021. Each condition is read against the accounting
635	standard where the standard speaks to it, and is otherwise this study's own specification, which is
636	stated as such at each row.
637	
638	## 6.1 The test: a dated window
639	
640	The obligation is recurring and annual, set by Perpres 12/2025 and SEB 2/2024 and registered at annual
641	frequency in SIRUSA, and the monitoring architecture fixes its first phase at October to December of
642	year *n* (Section 2.1). It can receive only what an upstream producer has already released, at that
643	producer's resolution and vintage. The operative test for a prospective supplier is therefore repeatability into a dated window rather than
644	demonstrated capability. Two of the system's own principles are supply-side conditions in disguise,
645	being temporal continuity and a standardisation guaranteeing that provinces aggregate on comparable
646	terms, and a standardised reporting format cannot in any case be issued without stating which of the
647	handbook's two pillar structures it follows (Section 2.3).
648	
649	## 6.2 Spatial conditions
650	
651	Provincial account output is not comparable across provinces or across vintages until one minimum
652	mapping unit and one basic spatial unit are adopted nationally. Both spatial conditions are acts of
653	codification, discharged once and requiring no new compilation. The standard requires an account to be
654	consistent over time and with respect to concepts and classifications (United Nations et al., 2024,
655	para. 1.60), and requires previous estimates to be revised for continuity of the time series wherever
656	updated data, methods or classifications permit a reassessment of area (para. 4.17). Grid-based
657	spatial units are the mechanism by which an account aggregates to any administrative boundary
658	(Section 3), conditional on the grid existing and being national. No adopted national marine grid or minimum mapping unit was found in any
659	obtained Indonesian document, having searched the set stated at Table 4 `[EVIDENCE GAP]`, whilst the
660	pilot literature records different
661	resolutions at different sites, the case the rule exists to prevent.
662	
663	The second spatial condition is a codified clipping rule. Provincial marine authority extends to 12
664	nautical miles under Law 23/2014, cited here at second hand and not read at source `[EVIDENCE GAP]`,
665	so an account reaching further must be clipped before it can yield a provincial number. The standard
666	leaves the accounting area to the compiler (para. 2.12) and supplies no sub-national allocation rule
667	for an asset spanning a boundary, so the convention this study takes as necessary, being allocation by
668	physical extent within each side with condition compiled per spatial unit rather than inherited whole,
669	is a specification rather than a citation `[ASSUMPTION]`, and neither it nor the statutory line is
670	codified in any account obtained by this study `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. One consequence follows from the
671	standard directly. Condition is not additive across areas, its variable account being an area-weighted
672	arithmetic mean over the assets of an ecosystem type (para. 5.54, p. 105), so because indicators 19, 20
673	and 21 ask for a ratio, a provincial value cannot be recovered by averaging district values, and a
674	supplier would need to publish the denominator alongside the percentage for a provincial value to be
675	recoverable.
676	Clean nesting of cell boundaries across administrative, fisheries-management and marine-spatial-planning
677	frames is asserted rather than evidenced `[ASSUMPTION]`, and forms part of this condition rather than a
678	property that may be assumed.
679	
680	## 6.3 Temporal conditions
681	
682	The reference period is the binding constraint on the mapped indicators, and it binds on repetition
683	rather than on speed. SIRUSA specifies T-1 for all five, being variables 19, 20, 21, 24 and 27, the
684	same five matched definitionally in Section 5. The single verified Indonesian compilation closes at
685	2021 and was published the following year, a lag the T-1 requirement can accommodate. What
686	is absent is the second vintage. One compilation, however timely, cannot meet a T-1 requirement in any subsequent
687	cycle, and no repeat compilation of the pilot sites was found in any obtained document
688	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. This condition is therefore recurring rather than one-off, discharged by running the
689	cycle again and not by a decision, and it survives the index's own inconsistent rendering of its
690	reference periods (Section 2.1) `[ASSUMPTION]`. Sustaining a cycle is a resourcing question, and no
691	compilation cost was located in the public record, so this study cannot price any condition in the
692	register.
693	
694	A revision policy is the second recurring condition. The standard already supplies its substance,
695	requiring revision of previous estimates for continuity of the series, recording no distinct revision
696	entry but altering the opening, closing, addition and reduction entries themselves, and allowing the
697	size of a revision to be shown as the difference between the historical and the revised account for
698	the same period (paras 4.17–4.18, p. 88). What is unwritten is the Indonesian instrument that would
699	designate a baseline year and disclose where the latest extent data refer to an earlier period, and
700	the index's own principle of controlled adaptability presupposes that discipline on the supply side.
701	No revision policy was found for any Indonesian ocean account output in the searched set
702	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. The consequence is specific: an account silently improved between vintages would
703	move a province's index value with no ecosystem change behind it, and nothing in the published record
704	would flag that. The instrument requires no new compilation, being a statement of when a series is
705	restated and how a provisional estimate is marked.
706	
707	## 6.4 The institutional condition
708	
709	Account output cannot become a durable input to an official index whilst it remains an unregistered
710	ministry product feeding an activity itself registered only as sectoral statistics. The IBEI is
711	recorded in SIRUSA as *Kompilasi Produk Administrasi* under *Statistik Sektoral* with an empty
712	*Identitas Rekomendasi* (kegiatan 94936), whilst the Juknis names BAPPENAS as *pengampu* of the
713	calculation (p. 68 [pdf 78]). Indonesian ocean account output is an MMAF product constituted under
714	Kepmen KP 44/2023 (Section 4.3), and no BPS statistical work-programme entry appears in any document
715	obtained by this study, no dedicated search of the BPS statistical catalogue having been undertaken
716	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. The condition is unmet against an instrument whose own term has run, that Kepmen
717	having set the team's expiry at 31 December 2023 with no renewal instrument located (Section 4.3), so
718	a supply arrangement would be settled by whichever body now holds the coordinating function rather
719	than by the standing arrangement the record documents. Two sectoral products feeding one another does not by itself
720	constitute an accredited statistical input. The condition is a documented supply arrangement
721	between MMAF as producer, BPS as statistical authority and BAPPENAS as compiler, whose minimum content
722	is stated here as this study's own specification rather than taken from any standard, being scope,
723	frequency, quality standards, confidentiality, format and dispute resolution `[ASSUMPTION]`. Written
724	that way it is a short document rather than a programme. That
725	accreditation would be the route is inferred from the index's own registration rather than stated
726	anywhere, so it is put as something BPS and MMAF would have to settle `[ASSUMPTION]`. The gap runs in both directions, since no
727	microdata are released (Section 3), so even a compliant supplier could not verify that its value
728	survived into the published provincial score. This condition, like the two spatial ones, is discharged
729	once, and all three are conditions the index would need met rather than obligations the accounts
730	programme owes it.
731	
732	## 6.5 Why coverage must lead insertion
733	
734	One design recommendation follows from the conditions. Where a province has no account, an account-derived indicator would itself be predicted from
735	socio-economic proxies under the framework the Report publishes (pp. 64–66 [pdf 76–78]), so an
736	environmental value would become partly a function of economic and social covariates. The framework's
737	first scenario trains on the previous cycle's historical data, which a newly introduced indicator does
738	not have. Insertion should
739	therefore be phased behind coverage: compile first, insert second. This is a sequencing recommendation
740	and not a change to the index's method.
741	
742	Proportion should temper the register. Three of the five conditions are acts of codification rather
743	than compilation programmes, and none of the five could be costed here (Section 6.3),

786	tiers enter it. Tier B fails the criterion on the content side rather than on the maturity side, as

792	(Juknis, Tabel 5 and Gambar 11, pp. 93–94 [pdf 103–104]). The pillar's remaining weight is the
793	governance sub-pillar at 15.8% and the technology sub-pillar at about 4.11%, so the six sub-pillars

800	whom, cannot be established from the register, which names no producer for any of the 44 rows
801	`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. What is established is narrower than invariance: no route in Table 5 supplies a

824	## 7.2 Tier C: what cannot now be measured
825	
826	None of the 44 constituent data items names an ecosystem service flow, an asset value or a depletion
827	term. That absence is the tier's subject, and its sharpest instance is an identity rather than an
828	aspiration. Indicators 2 to 5 are unsigned production volumes, so a province raises its score by
829	producing more, and no term anywhere in the register records whether the stock producing it is being
830	drawn down. The missing term is depletion, defined in the SEEA Central Framework
831	as the using up of a stock of resources relative to rates of regeneration (United Nations et al.,
832	2024, para. A1.25). The obstacle is that sustainable yield by province and by stock is not published,
833	which makes stock assessment a prerequisite rather than an alternative.

836	2024, ch. 7) and are compiled nowhere in Indonesia (Section 4.2). The nearest candidate is coastal protection from
837	mangrove and reef, measured in physical units against the extent and condition layers the pilot
838	already produces, which is named here as the specific service rather than left as a category. Net
839	extent change as a stock-and-flow identity is recommended as a reported companion series and not as a
840	forty-fifth indicator, for a reason developed later (Section 8). Sub-pillar H is
841	50.0% of the environment pillar and contains only solar and hydropower variables, at 24.5% and 24.2%
842	for photovoltaic capacity and generation and 26.5% and 24.8% for hydropower (Tabel 6, p. 96 [pdf
843	106]), so half the environment pillar measures non-marine energy. An offshore energy variable is the route by
844	which that sub-pillar could measure something marine, which makes this a recommendation about
845	indicator design rather than about compilation, and its obstacle is a matter of installed Indonesian
846	marine capacity on which no figure was located `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. Combined presentations
847	(United Nations et al., 2024, paras 14.56–14.57) are the one row available immediately, being a
848	presentation choice rather than a data gap.

993	# 8. What account supply does not fix
994	
995	## 8.1 Extending published limitations
996	
997	The index's custodian has printed its own methodological limits, and account supply reaches none of
998	them. Four are conceded in the index's own methods statement (Report Section 3.1.1, p. 34 [pdf 46]),
999	being an assumption of linear and monotonic relationships, whose worked example is that the benefit of
1000	fishing intensity holds below a threshold whilst damage rises exponentially above it, sensitivity to
1001	outliers, weights set on 2024 data that *mungkin saja menjadi tidak relevan untuk tahun-tahun
1002	mendatang* should the structure or coverage of the blue economy change fundamentally, and a single
1003	weight structure applied across a geographically and socio-economically heterogeneous archipelago. Every one is a property of the
1004	estimator, whilst an account changes the input. The first is worth dwelling on, since the threshold
1005	relationship BAPPENAS describes is exactly what an ecosystem account measures well, and better inputs
1006	cannot reach a relationship the estimator represents linearly by design, which is the limitation
1007	already printed. The remedies BAPPENAS proposes are compatible with account supply, and the first of
1008	them, complementary sectoral analysis reported alongside the index, is compatible in turn with the
1009	combined presentations the accounting standard describes, which summarise data from several accounts
1010	beside related economic and social data without requiring that ecosystem services be valued in
1011	monetary terms (United Nations et al., 2024, paras 14.56–14.57, pp. 356–357). What follows is therefore the limits register
1012	any supplier of inputs must accept, rather than a critique of the index.
1013	
1014	## 8.2 What aggregation discards
1015	
1016	The index consumes a scalar. The constituent data items for indicators 19, 20 and 21 each end *per
1017	provinsi* (Juknis, Tabel 1, p. 12 [pdf 22]), so an account compiled on a fine spatial grid discards its
1018	within-province distribution at the moment it becomes an indicator. The loss is asymmetric in value, because the account can tell a provincial
1019	planner which reef complex is driving a change whilst the index can report only that a percentage
1020	moved. That loss is not a necessary property of accounts but a consequence of publishing the indicator
1021	without the grid `[ASSUMPTION]`, so the remedy is an architecture in which both are released, which is
1022	a publication recommendation rather than a supply one (Section 9).
1023	
1024	The provincial boundary is an administrative line and not an ecological one (Section 6.2), and the
1025	standard is explicit that an accounting area may be a country, a subnational administrative area, a
1026	catchment or a protected area (United Nations et al., 2024, para. 2.12), which leaves the choice of
1027	boundary to the compiler and its ecological consequences to the account. A reef complex straddling
1028	that line
1029	is divided between two accounting areas by legal artefact. Accounts make the division explicit and
1030	auditable, which is worth having, and they do not make it ecologically meaningful, which is why the
1031	clipping rule is a condition precedent rather than a solved problem (Section 6.2). One further limit is
1032	inherited rather than created, since in a province without a compilation an account-derived indicator
1033	would itself be predicted from socio-economic proxies (Section 6.5).
1034	
1035	## 8.3 Why net extent change cannot enter
1036	
1037	The index states its own weighting rule: an indicator with greater variability and strong correlation
1038	to others in its domain *akan secara alami mendapatkan bobot yang lebih tinggi* (p. 29 [pdf 41]). A
1039	signed annual difference of a large, slowly changing stock is low-variance by construction and can
1040	change sign between cycles, so it would attract a small and unstable weight and might meet the rule
1041	printed in the sub-pillar analysis loop as *Reversi nilai indikator (atau drop indikator) jika
1042	ditemukan relasi negatif* (p. 30 [pdf 42]), where the negative relation is to the other indicators in
1043	the same sub-pillar. Whether a sign-changing series would trigger it is not specified in the Report and
1044	is read here as an inference. Net extent change belongs
1045	beside the composite as a reported companion series in a combined presentation, not inside it as a
1046	forty-fifth indicator (Table 5).
1047	
1048	## 8.4 What no account should supply
1049	
1050	Half the environment pillar measures something no ocean account should be built to measure. The
1051	renewable-energy sub-pillar carries 50.0% of the pillar (Juknis, Tabel 5, p. 94 [pdf 104]) and contains
1052	no marine variable, being solar and hydropower capacity and generation throughout (Section 7.2).
1053	Offshore energy accounting is a forward recommendation, and the point here is narrower: no existing
1054	account can supply what that sub-pillar currently asks for, and building one to do so would be an
1055	instrument error. The article's coverage claim stops at that boundary, and stopping is what makes it
1056	credible. Everything the mapping and the forward register recommend rests on tag (b) pilot compilation,
1057	and the case for it is a production-process case rather than a weight case.
1058	
1059	## 8.5 Limitations of this study
1060	
1061	Eight limitations follow. The first bears on the argument itself and the rest on specific figures, and
1062	they are ordered accordingly rather than by the sequence in which they arose.
1063	
1064	The first is the counter-instance this article's own evidence supplies to its own positive claim. The
1065	only compiled Indonesian account has produced two official extent series for the same pilot, the same
1066	three ecosystems and the same two years, and they disagree not by a constant offset but by a ratio
1067	that moves across ecosystems and across the accounting window, so the two documents disagree on the
1068	change as well as on the level, MMAF recording a coral loss of 12.00 ha against a BPS loss of 18.26 ha
1069	(Section 4.2). The pilot's own reporting compounds it, the narrative giving live hard coral cover of
1070	16.99% for 2021 against 18.36% in the report's table on the same page (Section 5.2). Change is the
1071	quantity a balancing identity exists to report, and provenance is what this article claims accounts
1072	buy, so a divergence of this kind is the strongest available evidence against that claim and is
1073	interrogated rather than set aside (Section 9.1). No reconciliation is published and none is attempted
1074	here `[EVIDENCE GAP]`.
1075	
1076	Second, the printed aggregation formulae are illegible in the PDF text layer at Juknis pp. 64–65 and
1077	Report pp. 31–32. They are recorded here from their variable definitions, which are unambiguous about
1078	Z-score inputs and first-principal-component loading weights, so what is reconstructed is the notation,
1079	and the exact summation form at medium-high confidence rather than the method. Third, whether Tabel 4
1080	to Tabel 6's percentages are raw loadings or normalised contributions is not stated anywhere, and every
1081	derived figure in this article inherits that, including 7.64% `[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]`, 13.7%, 5.15%
1082	and 2.33% `[ASSUMPTION]`. The ambiguity is bounded rather than open, because the test is to renormalise
1083	each sub-pillar's printed variable weights to sum to 100 and recompute. In the resource-quality
1084	sub-pillar the nine printed weights already sum to exactly 100.0, so renormalisation leaves 18.45%,
1085	7.64%, 3.58% and 2.33% unchanged to the precision the handbook prints, and the residual uncertainty is
1086	the half-unit rounding of one-decimal inputs, which moves 7.64% by less than 0.1 percentage points. In
1087	the economy pillar the five printed sub-pillar weights sum to 96.0 rather than 100, the residual being
1088	the technology sub-pillar, which is not printed and is recovered here as 4.11%. Renormalising over the
1089	five printed weights alone would instead give about 27.2% for the four Tier B sub-pillars and about
1090	5.37% for governance, and Gambar 11's independently printed integers of 26 and 5 favour the reading
1091	adopted here, so the alternative is reported as an upper bound of about one percentage point rather
1092	than carried. Fourth, the per-indicator imputation record is
1093	unpublished, so which indicators were predicted, for which provinces, by which model and at what error
1094	are all unknown, which is the largest single unknown among the figure-level limitations because it
1095	determines how much of any published provincial value is measurement and how much is prediction.
1096	Fifth, two items sit off the document: Annex 3's per-province analysis is behind a short link, and the
1097	normalised factor of 0.6819 is printed under four provincial tables without formula or derivation
1098	(Table 1).
1099	
1100	Sixth, this study engaged no Indonesian agency, by project constraint, so the limitations above are
1101	conditions to live with rather than future work, and that distinction matters for how a reader should
1102	weigh them. Seventh, the mapping is an assessment against stated criteria rather than an independently
1103	verifiable quantity, which is why the score and the exclusion ground for each of the 44 register rows
1104	are deposited in full rather than summarised, so that a reader may reject a row and recompute. Eighth,
1105	compilation cost is absent from the public record at every site, so the conditions precedent are not
1106	costed and could not be.

1109	# 9. Discussion: gains from compilation
1110	
1111	## 9.1 What Tier A compilation changes
1112	
1113	Three results carry the argument. Examining the production system beneath the index found defects that
1114	are properties of provenance rather than of arithmetic, being an imputation framework whose
1115	per-indicator application is unpublished, a rescaling factor printed without derivation, and two
1116	official extent series for the one compiled Indonesian account that cannot be reconciled (Sections 2
1117	and 4.2). Reading the register's verbatim constituent data items rather than its indicator names
1118	established that three ecosystem condition indicators are definitionally matched to account output
1119	rather than category-mismatched, that five of the 44 rows are the whole of what existing accounts
1120	touch, and that the account types bearing on the larger share of the index are the least developed
1121	(Sections 5 and 7). What follows from those is a production-process case and not a weight case, and it
1122	is narrower than an efficiency claim, which the evidence assembled here would not support.
1123	
1124	The register decomposes into 62 distinct constituent data
1125	items across its 44 indicators (Section 5.1), of which the resource-quality sub-pillar accounts for 9
1126	items across 9 indicators, and the five mapped indicators for 5 items. Three of those five, being indicators 19, 20
1127	and 21, are cross-classifications of one extent-and-condition compilation, whilst indicator 24
1128	corresponds to the account's structure without being produced by it and indicator 27 is a designation
1129	total already held by its custodians rather than measured by an account (Section 5.3). The
1130	consolidation is therefore three items into one production run, out of 62. That is a small share of the
1131	register, and the argument is not that the count is large. It is that those three are among the items
1132	the index presently has no way to show it measured rather than predicted.
1133	
1134	The count is a surrogate for what a reader would want, being the number of separate source publications
1135	a compiler must assemble, which the published record cannot yield, since no register row states a
1136	producing agency and the only producer statement sits at activity level, naming KKP and BPS
1137	collectively (SIRUSA kegiatan 94936).
1138	
1139	What compilation adds is provenance of a specific kind. An account carries an opening stock, additions,
1140	reductions and a closing stock over a defined spatial unit, which allows a provincial figure to be
1141	revised on improved information and defended afterwards, where the published output offers an underived
1142	rescaling factor beneath its regional tables (Table 1).
1143	
1144	That claim has to be tested against the strongest counter-instance in this article's own evidence, and
1145	the counter-instance is the account itself. Two official series for the Gili Matra pilot disagree at
1146	the level and, because the ratio between them moves by ecosystem and by year, on the change as well,
1147	so the one compiled Indonesian account has produced two closing stocks and two deltas for the same
1148	site and the same window (Sections 4.2 and 8.5). Read one way this refutes the provenance claim
1149	outright. Read against what the two documents actually are, it qualifies it in a specific place. The
1150	two series are two parallel releases of the same underlying mapping layer, published in a presentation
1151	and a report respectively, and neither is an account of the other, so what the record demonstrates is
1152	that publishing account-shaped tables is not the same as running an account. The property being
1153	claimed is not that compilation prevents divergence, which nothing in the standard promises, but that
1154	a balancing identity gives divergence somewhere to appear, the opening stock, the additions, the
1155	reductions and the closing stock being four entries that must reconcile and that a second compilation
1156	must restate rather than replace. The Gili Matra case is evidence for that reading in a negative form,
1157	as it is precisely because neither document was compiled under such an identity, and because no
1158	revision rule required either to restate the other, that a 6.26 ha disagreement about coral change
1159	could be published twice and reconciled nowhere. The reading is an argument from the account form
1160	rather than a demonstration, since no Indonesian compilation has yet been revised under such a rule
1161	and the claim therefore remains untested in Indonesia `[ASSUMPTION]`.
1162	
1163	The objection that a well-documented statistical register could supply the same properties without any
1164	account (Section 3) bears directly on that argument, and it is conceded in part. Standing unit and
1165	classification definitions and documented vintage are acts of codification that a register discharges
1166	unaided, and three of the five conditions precedent are codification rather than compilation
1167	(Section 6). What does not reduce to a register is the residual named earlier, being the balancing
1168	identity and the revision rule that alters the entries themselves. The residual is thin, and saying so
1169	is more useful than defending a wider claim: for the three matched indicators, most of what an account
1170	would contribute in the near term could be contributed by a published classification standard, and
1171	what could not is the part that makes a change defensible rather than merely stated. A reader who
1172	concludes that Indonesia should codify definitions before compiling anything has read the evidence
1173	correctly, and that ordering is what Section 6.5 recommends.
1174	
1175	A gain in
1176	imputation is stated conditionally rather than claimed, as no baseline exists against which to measure
1177	one (Section 2), so what can be claimed is that measured values for the three consolidated items would
1178	be distinguishable from predicted ones.
1179	Introducing an account-derived indicator would in any case relocate imputation rather than remove it,
1180	which is why insertion should follow coverage (Section 6.5).
1181	
1182	Everything downstream of the input is untouched. Account supply changes the value entering a slot, not
1183	the slot, its weight or the aggregation rule, so the three-stage principal component procedure, the
1184	loading-factor weights, the pillar shares and every limitation the index publishes about itself stand
1185	exactly as they are (Section 8.1). What follows for the composite's stability is conditional on a
1186	reading the handbook does not settle. If Tabel 4 to Tabel 6's percentages are loading-normalised
1187	contributions, the mature route bears on about 7.64% of the composite `[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]`,
1188	against roughly 18.9% resting on account types compiled nowhere, and inserting account-derived values
1189	at those rows would perturb the composite little, which cuts against the fear of destabilisation and
1190	against any claim of a transformed environmental signal in equal measure. If they are raw loadings,
1191	the share is not calculable from the published record and no statement about perturbation is available
1192	in either direction. The renormalisation test bounds the difference between the two readings at under
1193	a percentage point wherever it can be run (Section 8.5), and the conditional is stated rather than
1194	collapsed because the test cannot be run on the pillar weights themselves. The
1195	design findings this study reports are likewise unaffected by accounts and not repaired by them.
1196	
1197	## 9.2 Loose coupling as architecture
1198	
1199	Where substitution is not warranted, and on the published maturity evidence it is not, the arrangement
1200	that delivers the gain is loose coupling. It has three requirements, being (1) shared indicator definitions at the
1201	cover-class thresholds the Juknis already prints, so an account can tabulate to the index's own
1202	definition, with the unit and denominator rules the register leaves open settled with its custodian
1203	(Section 5.4), (2) a shared spatial unit that aggregates to the province, and (3)
1204	parallel publication of the account alongside the index. It is defined as much by what it does not
1205	require, being no institutional transfer, no change to the index's data pipeline, and no dependency of
1206	a statutory index on an experimental compilation.
1207	
1208	Both halves have a route in the standard. Parallel publication is a combined presentation
1209	(United Nations et al., 2024, paras 14.56–14.57) and is the nearer term of the two, being a
1210	presentational choice rather than a data gap, whilst sub-national compilation follows from the
1211	accounting area being definable at a subnational administrative boundary (para. 2.12) and has been
1212	demonstrated at that scale elsewhere (Gacutan et al., 2022), though nowhere in Indonesia beyond the
1213	pilot (Section 4.1). Timing governs adoption either way (Section 6.1), and loose coupling has the
1214	property that a compilation cycle missing the window fails without the index failing with it.
1215	
1216	The institutional division holds at the point of recommendation, where it is most at risk. BAPPENAS is
1217	*pengampu* of the calculation whilst the constituent data are provincial (Juknis, p. 68 [pdf 78]), so
1218	nothing here asks either institution to take on the other's mandate. The arrangement would also have
1219	to be settled by whichever body now holds the coordinating function, the team constituted under Kepmen
1220	KP 44/2023 having had its term expire on 31 December 2023 with no renewal instrument located
1221	(Sections 4.3 and 6.4), so an architecture requiring inter-agency coordination is being proposed
1222	against a documentary record in which the standing coordination instrument has run its term.
1223	Substitution becomes arguable when the conditions precedent are met (Section 6), and not before.
1224	
1225	The evidence ceiling on all of this is one marine protected area, in one province, at one vintage,
1226	carrying maturity tag (b) throughout (Section 4.1), and the architecture above is therefore a
1227	specification of what would have to be true rather than a generalisation from a demonstrated national
1228	capability. Two readings of the same record would lead elsewhere. Rinaldi et al. (2025) respond to the
1229	weakness of Indonesian blue economy measurement by constructing an alternative index on a human
1230	development index template, which treats the problem as one of index design rather than of the
1231	production system beneath it, and on that reading the effort recommended here would be misdirected.
1232	Wuwung et al. (2024) locate the constraint on Indonesian ocean policy in institutional fragmentation
1233	across mandates, which would make an inter-agency supply arrangement the hardest rather than the
1234	cheapest of the conditions precedent, against the ordering proposed here. No source located by this
1235	study argues the contrary position directly, that accounts cannot serve as statistical infrastructure
1236	for a composite index, and the position advanced here should be read as adjacent to those two rather
1237	than as contested by a settled literature.

1240	# 10. Conclusion and follow-on
1241	
1242	## 10.1 The single take-home
1243	
1244	The single take-home is that ocean accounts are worth building for this index because of how the
1245	handful of indicators they touch would be produced, and not because of how many indicators they could
1246	fill, the share an account would itself supply being small on any reading of the published weights and
1247	resting in part on a denominator the source documents do not settle (Section 9.1). The cost argument
1248	below, the two follow-on studies and the statutory boundary are supporting rather than co-equal, and
1249	each is presented as such.
1250	
1251	## 10.2 Compilation cost, unpublished
1252	
1253	No compilation cost appears anywhere in the record obtained for this study. There is no staff-day
1254	figure, no budget line, no imagery cost and no field-survey cost for any Indonesian ocean account, at
1255	any site `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. That absence, rather than any methodological obstacle in the conditions
1256	precedent, is the binding constraint on acting on this study's recommendation, because a
1257	province-by-province annual obligation is a scope decision before it is a method decision. This study
1258	declines to estimate a cost, since an order-of-magnitude range would be read as a figure. The only
1259	signal in the obtained record is computational rather than financial, in the grid-cell counts a single
1260	bay generates at two resolutions (Rahayu et al., 2024), and processing burden for one bay is not a
1261	compilation cost for a province.
1262	
1263	## 10.3 Follow-on studies, and one risk
1264	
1265	The claim that indicators 19, 20 and 21 are definitionally matched to account output, with the barrier
1266	lying in compilation and geographic coverage rather than in measurement target, is empirically testable
1267	today at exactly one site. Re-tabulating the Gili Matra compilation to the index's own 50% to 100% cut
1268	would settle whether an account's extent and cover layers yield the register's class-share form at all,
1269	and would generate the first real compilation-cost observation. Its limits are set by the record
1270	itself, since the coral and seagrass cover behind that compilation was surveyed at stations rather than
1271	mapped per cell (Section 5.2), the mangrove row is blocked on an undocumented unit conversion, and a
1272	footprint of 2,980.84 ha supports no comparison against a published provincial value. Extending the
1273	same tabulation to NTB as a whole is the step that would test provincial resolution, and it requires
1274	the province-wide compilation that does not exist. Neither settles national coverage, the absent
1275	spatial standard or the absent revision policy, and both carry an unknown on the index's side, since
1276	whether the published values were themselves predicted is not recoverable.
1277	
1278	The second is a compilation-guidance output rather than a research one. No published guidance sets out
1279	how a sub-national marine ecosystem account should be compiled to serve a provincial indicator in an
1280	Indonesian setting, and the material in Sections 2, 4 and 5 here is much of what such a document would
1281	need. The sensible division is that the article supplies the analytical basis and the guidance document
1282	the practitioner-facing output, each citing the other rather than duplicating it.
1283	
1284	Both assume continued external technical assistance to the Indonesian programme. That is an assumption
1285	rather than an established condition, no funding or programme-continuity document having been obtained
1286	by this study `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. The cost study is the one item that becomes more valuable if such
1287	assistance lapses, since it is what would let the decision be taken domestically.
1288	
1289	What would falsify the argument is worth stating, because a paper of this shape is otherwise
1290	structurally unfalsifiable. If a single-province compilation reproduces the published provincial value
1291	no better than the current secondary-publication route, or does so at a cost per province that an
1292	annual national cycle cannot bear, then the production-process case fails on its own terms.
1293	
1294	Within the statutory boundary held throughout (Section 1), what this study describes is a capacity gap
1295	under a statutory obligation, in a country that has demonstrated, at one experimental pilot site, one
1296	instance of the production process the obligation would require at scale.

1299	# Methods
1300	
1301	**Design.** The study is a documentary analysis of two published specifications read against each
1302	other: the Indonesia Blue Economy Index register as printed by its custodian, and the account
1303	structures of the SEEA Ecosystem Accounting standard together with the specified sequence of national
1304	ocean accounts. No sampling, no statistical inference and no hypothesis test is involved, and no
1305	result reported here is an estimate with a sampling distribution. The equivalent burden is
1306	reproducibility of a scoring rule and of an arithmetic chain, which the deposited materials discharge.
1307	
1308	**Corpus.** A citation-anchored corpus of 199 chunks was built over the index's own published
1309	documentation and released with this article, so that every quotation and every figure in the analysis
1310	resolves to a numbered record rather than to a page range. Extraction was purposive rather than
1311	exhaustive: the passages carrying the register, the weights, the calculation method, the sensitivity
1312	analysis, the imputation framework, the provincial output and the cascading obligation were sliced from
1313	each source at stated printed-page ranges, and nothing outside those ranges was chunked. The build,
1314	dated 2026-08-14, comprises 106 chunks from the Juknis (BAPPENAS 2025a) over pp. 11–13, 63–66, 67–71,
1315	93–96 and 101–104, 49 from the IBEI Report 2024 in its December 2025 revision (BAPPENAS 2025b) over
1316	pp. 28–35, 64–66 and 67–70, 15 from Rinaldi et al. (2025) and 29 from the SIRUSA metadata for the
1317	compilation activity and its indicator record (kegiatan 94936 and indikator 105371, both retrieved
1318	2026-08-13). Each record carries a chunk identifier, its document and slice, the verbatim Indonesian
1319	text, an English drafting gloss used nowhere as a quotation source, the printed page with the PDF page
1320	in brackets, a content type, a maturity tag and a flag marking whether any part of the record was
1321	reconstructed rather than read directly, which 17 of the 199 records carry. Chunks in conflict with one
1322	another are recorded as conflicts rather than reconciled, and the four-against-three pillar contradiction
1323	is held as one such record.
1324	
1325	Account-side evidence sits outside that corpus and was read directly: the three MMAF 2022 reports
1326	including the Gili Matra pilot study, the BPS materials prepared for the SEEA workshops, Rahayu et al.
1327	(2024), Kepmen KP 44/2023 and the GOAP country page (retrieved 2026-08-13). All source documents carry
1328	clean text layers and no optical character recognition was applied anywhere, so the two passages
1329	recorded as illegible (Section 8.5) are defects of the published files rather than of extraction.
1330	Retrieval and assembly ran from 11 to 16 August 2026. Alongside the corpus, a join table reproduces all
1331	44 register rows with each row's *Data Pembentuk Indikator* constituent data items verbatim,
1332	transcribed from Juknis Tabel 1, pp. 11–13 [pdf 21–23], and totalling 62 constituent data items. That
1333	join table, and not the running text of the handbook, is the object the membership rule was applied to,
1334	which is what makes the scoring reproducible from the deposit alone.
1335	
1336	**Search protocol.** Searching was bilingual and was run over the document set named above rather than
1337	over a statistical catalogue. The Indonesian terms used were *indeks ekonomi biru*, *neraca laut*,
1338	*akun laut*, *neraca ekosistem*, *unit spasial dasar laut*, *peta jalan ekonomi biru* and *Satu Data
1339	Indonesia*, together with their English equivalents, with no date restriction. No dedicated search of
1340	the BPS statistical catalogue or of the Satu Data Indonesia portal was undertaken, and by project
1341	constraint no Indonesian agency was approached for data, interview or correspondence. Every absence
1342	claim in the article is therefore stated as unfound within the named set and is not a claim about the
1343	Indonesian statistical system.
1344	
1345	**Unit of analysis and the membership rule.** The unit is the register row, of which there are 44, and
1346	the object tested is the row's constituent data items rather than its title, on the Indonesian wording.
1347	Each row was graded on one of three grounds: *matched*, where a constituent data item names a quantity
1348	that a defined SEEA Ecosystem Accounting output contains, *partial*, where account output supplies a
1349	component of the quantity but not the quantity itself, and *provenance question*, where account output
1350	bears on the row without supplying it. Tier A is the union of the three grades and has five members,
1351	being indicators 19, 20 and 21 matched, 24 partial and 27 a provenance question. All remaining 39 rows
1352	are graded none. The grade and the ground for every row, with the constituent data items reproduced,
1353	are deposited as Supplementary Table S1 so that a reader may reject a row and recompute. The rule was
1354	applied item by item rather than row by row, so a row carrying several constituent data items takes the
1355	highest grade any one of its items attains, which is why the 62 items and not the 44 rows are the
1356	tested population. Two decisions were fixed before scoring and held throughout: that indicator titles
1357	are not evidence of what a row requires, titles having produced the predecessor figures the analysis
1358	rejects, and that a definitional match carries no claim about whether anything has been compiled, the
1359	two axes being reported separately at every use. Grading was performed by the authors against those
1360	criteria, not blind, with no second coder and no adjudication protocol, which bounds what the exercise
1361	can claim and is recorded among the limitations (Section 8.5). Replication of the scoring therefore
1362	requires the deposited join table, the rule above and the SEEA Ecosystem Accounting output structures,
1363	and requires nothing this study holds privately.
1364	
1365	**The repeatability test.** Membership in Tier A is a definitional property and carries no claim about
1366	compilation. The separate operative test applied to the account side is repeatability into a dated
1367	window rather than demonstrated capability, since the obligation is annual and can receive only what an
1368	upstream producer has already released at the required vintage (Section 6.1). Maturity is tagged on
1369	every claim that something exists, as (a) published official statistics, (b) experimental or pilot
1370	compilation, (c) methodology under development, or (d) announced but not started, and the tag describes
1371	the datum rather than the document reporting it.
1372	
1373	**Weight arithmetic.** Weights are taken from the Juknis as printed: pillar weights from Tabel 4, p. 94
1374	[pdf 104], the environment pillar's sub-pillar split from Tabel 5, p. 94 [pdf 104], variable weights
1375	from Tabel 6, pp. 95–96 [pdf 105–106], and the sub-pillar shares of Gambar 11, p. 93 [pdf 103], used as
1376	an independent integer cross-check. Shares of the composite are products of those published
1377	percentages, carried to two decimal places from one-decimal inputs and reported in prose to one decimal
1378	with 'about'. The technology sub-pillar's weight is not printed and is recovered as the residual of the
1379	economy pillar, at 4.11%. All weight arithmetic follows the three-pillar structure of Tabel 4, Tabel 5
1380	and Gambar 11, on the ground that weights are published only there, whilst register rows keep their
1381	Tabel 1 labels. The two structures disagree over indicators 15 to 18, and figures computed over the
1382	pillars across which those rows move are identified as structure-dependent at each use (Section 2.3).
1383	
1384	**Sensitivity to the weight ambiguity.** The handbook does not state whether the percentages in Tabel 4
1385	to Tabel 6 are raw first-principal-component loadings or normalised contributions. The test applied is
1386	to renormalise each sub-pillar's printed variable weights to sum to 100 and recompute. In the
1387	resource-quality sub-pillar the nine printed weights already sum to exactly 100.0, so the renormalised
1388	and printed readings coincide and 18.45%, 7.64%, 3.58% and 2.33% are unchanged to the precision
1389	printed, with a residual half-unit rounding uncertainty moving 7.64% by under 0.1 percentage points. In
1390	the economy pillar the five printed sub-pillar weights sum to 96.0, and renormalising over those five
1391	alone would give about 27.2% for the four Tier B sub-pillars and about 5.37% for governance against the
1392	26.1% and 5.15% adopted here, an upper bound of roughly one percentage point which Gambar 11's printed
1393	integers of 26 and 5 do not support. The test cannot be run on the pillar weights themselves, which
1394	already sum to 100.0 as printed.
1395	
1396	**Reporting conventions.** Indonesian decimal commas are preserved inside quotation and rendered as
1397	points in prose. Quotations are from the Indonesian source text. Three inline flags mark the evidential
1398	status of statements: `[EVIDENCE GAP]` where a required source was not found, `[ASSUMPTION]` where the
1399	argument depends on something unconfirmed, and `[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]` on the mature-route share of
1400	7.64% and on figures derived from it, recording that the share measures what these account types bear
1401	on rather than what an account would supply (Section 5.4). Conflicting sources are reported as
1402	conflicts and are not averaged or adjudicated.
