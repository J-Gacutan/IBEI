# 6. Conditions precedent for supply

A definitional match is not a supply arrangement. Five conditions must hold before pilot account output
could serve the IBEI as a repeating input rather than a single demonstration, none is satisfied in the
obtained record, and they are not equally binding (Table 4). Three are one-off acts of codification
that a single decision by MMAF, BPS or BAPPENAS could discharge. Two are discharged only by sustaining
a production cycle, and those two are what decide whether the mapping in Section 5 is a recommendation
or a wish. The register would be refuted by producing an adopted national marine spatial standard, a
BPS statistical work-programme entry for a marine ecosystem account, or an extent compilation covering
more than the pilot sites and closing after 2021. Each condition is read against the accounting
standard where the standard speaks to it, and is otherwise this study's own specification, which is
stated as such at each row.

## 6.1 The test: a dated window

The obligation is recurring and annual, set by Perpres 12/2025 and SEB 2/2024 and registered at annual
frequency in SIRUSA, and the monitoring architecture fixes its first phase at October to December of
year *n* (Section 2.1). It can receive only what an upstream producer has already released, at that
producer's resolution and vintage. The operative test for a prospective supplier is therefore repeatability into a dated window rather than
demonstrated capability. Two of the system's own principles are supply-side conditions in disguise,
being temporal continuity and a standardisation guaranteeing that provinces aggregate on comparable
terms, and a standardised reporting format cannot in any case be issued without stating which of the
handbook's two pillar structures it follows (Section 2.3).

## 6.2 Spatial conditions

Provincial account output is not comparable across provinces or across vintages until one minimum
mapping unit and one basic spatial unit are adopted nationally. Both spatial conditions are acts of
codification, discharged once and requiring no new compilation. The standard requires an account to be
consistent over time and with respect to concepts and classifications (United Nations et al., 2024,
para. 1.60), and requires previous estimates to be revised for continuity of the time series wherever
updated data, methods or classifications permit a reassessment of area (para. 4.17). Grid-based
spatial units are the mechanism by which an account aggregates to any administrative boundary
(Section 3), conditional on the grid existing and being national. No adopted national marine grid or minimum mapping unit was found in any
obtained Indonesian document, having searched the set stated at Table 4 `[EVIDENCE GAP]`, whilst the
pilot literature records different
resolutions at different sites, the case the rule exists to prevent.

The second spatial condition is a codified clipping rule. Provincial marine authority extends to 12
nautical miles under Law 23/2014, cited here at second hand and not read at source `[EVIDENCE GAP]`,
so an account reaching further must be clipped before it can yield a provincial number. The standard
leaves the accounting area to the compiler (para. 2.12) and supplies no sub-national allocation rule
for an asset spanning a boundary, so the convention this study takes as necessary, being allocation by
physical extent within each side with condition compiled per spatial unit rather than inherited whole,
is a specification rather than a citation `[ASSUMPTION]`, and neither it nor the statutory line is
codified in any account obtained by this study `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. One consequence follows from the
standard directly. Condition is not additive across areas, its variable account being an area-weighted
arithmetic mean over the assets of an ecosystem type (para. 5.54, p. 105), so because indicators 19, 20
and 21 ask for a ratio, a provincial value cannot be recovered by averaging district values, and a
supplier would need to publish the denominator alongside the percentage for a provincial value to be
recoverable.
Clean nesting of cell boundaries across administrative, fisheries-management and marine-spatial-planning
frames is asserted rather than evidenced `[ASSUMPTION]`, and forms part of this condition rather than a
property that may be assumed.

## 6.3 Temporal conditions

The reference period is the binding constraint on the mapped indicators, and it binds on repetition
rather than on speed. SIRUSA specifies T-1 for all five, being variables 19, 20, 21, 24 and 27, the
same five matched definitionally in Section 5. The single verified Indonesian compilation closes at
2021 and was published the following year, a lag the T-1 requirement can accommodate. What
is absent is the second vintage. One compilation, however timely, cannot meet a T-1 requirement in any subsequent
cycle, and no repeat compilation of the pilot sites was found in any obtained document
`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. This condition is therefore recurring rather than one-off, discharged by running the
cycle again and not by a decision, and it survives the index's own inconsistent rendering of its
reference periods (Section 2.1) `[ASSUMPTION]`. Sustaining a cycle is a resourcing question, and no
compilation cost was located in the public record, so this study cannot price any condition in the
register.

A revision policy is the second recurring condition. The standard already supplies its substance,
requiring revision of previous estimates for continuity of the series, recording no distinct revision
entry but altering the opening, closing, addition and reduction entries themselves, and allowing the
size of a revision to be shown as the difference between the historical and the revised account for
the same period (paras 4.17–4.18, p. 88). What is unwritten is the Indonesian instrument that would
designate a baseline year and disclose where the latest extent data refer to an earlier period, and
the index's own principle of controlled adaptability presupposes that discipline on the supply side.
No revision policy was found for any Indonesian ocean account output in the searched set
`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. The consequence is specific: an account silently improved between vintages would
move a province's index value with no ecosystem change behind it, and nothing in the published record
would flag that. The instrument requires no new compilation, being a statement of when a series is
restated and how a provisional estimate is marked.

## 6.4 The institutional condition

Account output cannot become a durable input to an official index whilst it remains an unregistered
ministry product feeding an activity itself registered only as sectoral statistics. The IBEI is
recorded in SIRUSA as *Kompilasi Produk Administrasi* under *Statistik Sektoral* with an empty
*Identitas Rekomendasi* (kegiatan 94936), whilst the Juknis names BAPPENAS as *pengampu* of the
calculation (p. 68 [pdf 78]). Indonesian ocean account output is an MMAF product constituted under
Kepmen KP 44/2023 (Section 4.3), and no BPS statistical work-programme entry appears in any document
obtained by this study, no dedicated search of the BPS statistical catalogue having been undertaken
`[EVIDENCE GAP]`. The condition is unmet against an instrument whose own term has run, that Kepmen
having set the team's expiry at 31 December 2023 with no renewal instrument located (Section 4.3), so
a supply arrangement would be settled by whichever body now holds the coordinating function rather
than by the standing arrangement the record documents. Two sectoral products feeding one another does not by itself
constitute an accredited statistical input. The condition is a documented supply arrangement
between MMAF as producer, BPS as statistical authority and BAPPENAS as compiler, whose minimum content
is stated here as this study's own specification rather than taken from any standard, being scope,
frequency, quality standards, confidentiality, format and dispute resolution `[ASSUMPTION]`. Written
that way it is a short document rather than a programme. That
accreditation would be the route is inferred from the index's own registration rather than stated
anywhere, so it is put as something BPS and MMAF would have to settle `[ASSUMPTION]`. The gap runs in both directions, since no
microdata are released (Section 3), so even a compliant supplier could not verify that its value
survived into the published provincial score. This condition, like the two spatial ones, is discharged
once, and all three are conditions the index would need met rather than obligations the accounts
programme owes it.

## 6.5 Why coverage must lead insertion

One design recommendation follows from the conditions. Where a province has no account, an account-derived indicator would itself be predicted from
socio-economic proxies under the framework the Report publishes (pp. 64–66 [pdf 76–78]), so an
environmental value would become partly a function of economic and social covariates. The framework's
first scenario trains on the previous cycle's historical data, which a newly introduced indicator does
not have. Insertion should
therefore be phased behind coverage: compile first, insert second. This is a sequencing recommendation
and not a change to the index's method.

Proportion should temper the register. Three of the five conditions are acts of codification rather
than compilation programmes, and none of the five could be costed here (Section 6.3),
whilst the mature route they bear on is about 7.64% of the composite `[CONTESTED DENOMINATOR]`, of
which about 2.33% is the share an account would itself supply, rising to about 3.58% only if indicator
24 is credited in full (Section 5.4), against roughly 18.9% resting on account types compiled nowhere
(Section 7.3). The leverage of the mapped indicators alone
does not justify them, and the two recurring conditions decide whether even that leverage is a
prospect or a wish.
