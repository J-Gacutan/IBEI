# 10. Conclusion and follow-on

## 10.1 The single take-home

The single take-home is that ocean accounts are worth building for this index because of how the
handful of indicators they touch would be produced, and not because of how many indicators they could
fill, the share an account would itself supply being small on any reading of the published weights and
resting in part on a denominator the source documents do not settle (Section 9.1). The cost argument
below, the two follow-on studies and the statutory boundary are supporting rather than co-equal, and
each is presented as such.

## 10.2 Compilation cost, unpublished

No compilation cost appears anywhere in the record obtained for this study. There is no staff-day
figure, no budget line, no imagery cost and no field-survey cost for any Indonesian ocean account, at
any site `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. That absence, rather than any methodological obstacle in the conditions
precedent, is the binding constraint on acting on this study's recommendation, because a
province-by-province annual obligation is a scope decision before it is a method decision. This study
declines to estimate a cost, since an order-of-magnitude range would be read as a figure. The only
signal in the obtained record is computational rather than financial, in the grid-cell counts a single
bay generates at two resolutions (Rahayu et al., 2024), and processing burden for one bay is not a
compilation cost for a province.

## 10.3 Follow-on studies, and one risk

The claim that indicators 19, 20 and 21 are definitionally matched to account output, with the barrier
lying in compilation and geographic coverage rather than in measurement target, is empirically testable
today at exactly one site. Re-tabulating the Gili Matra compilation to the index's own 50% to 100% cut
would settle whether an account's extent and cover layers yield the register's class-share form at all,
and would generate the first real compilation-cost observation. Its limits are set by the record
itself, since the coral and seagrass cover behind that compilation was surveyed at stations rather than
mapped per cell (Section 5.2), the mangrove row is blocked on an undocumented unit conversion, and a
footprint of 2,980.84 ha supports no comparison against a published provincial value. Extending the
same tabulation to NTB as a whole is the step that would test provincial resolution, and it requires
the province-wide compilation that does not exist. Neither settles national coverage, the absent
spatial standard or the absent revision policy, and both carry an unknown on the index's side, since
whether the published values were themselves predicted is not recoverable.

The second is a compilation-guidance output rather than a research one. No published guidance sets out
how a sub-national marine ecosystem account should be compiled to serve a provincial indicator in an
Indonesian setting, and the material in Sections 2, 4 and 5 here is much of what such a document would
need. The sensible division is that the article supplies the analytical basis and the guidance document
the practitioner-facing output, each citing the other rather than duplicating it.

Both assume continued external technical assistance to the Indonesian programme. That is an assumption
rather than an established condition, no funding or programme-continuity document having been obtained
by this study `[EVIDENCE GAP]`. The cost study is the one item that becomes more valuable if such
assistance lapses, since it is what would let the decision be taken domestically.

What would falsify the argument is worth stating, because a paper of this shape is otherwise
structurally unfalsifiable. If a single-province compilation reproduces the published provincial value
no better than the current secondary-publication route, or does so at a cost per province that an
annual national cycle cannot bear, then the production-process case fails on its own terms.

Within the statutory boundary held throughout (Section 1), what this study describes is a capacity gap
under a statutory obligation, in a country that has demonstrated, at one experimental pilot site, one
instance of the production process the obligation would require at scale.
