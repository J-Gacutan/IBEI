Empty columns and maturity. No register indicator maps to an ecosystem services account or to an asset/monetary account: the environment pillar contains no service flow, no asset value and no depletion term. The emptiness of those two columns is therefore an index-design fact, not an account-supply one. The column is labelled ecosystem services in physical units deliberately. What the Indonesian record shows is benefit-transferred monetary valuation of ecosystem functions at pilot scale — coral values transferred from Bohol and the Marianas — which is a different thing from SEEA-EA services supply and use in physical units, and the two must never be run together under the bare word “services”. Benefit-transferred values are also not the exchange-value basis SEEA-EA requires. On the account side, extent is the only type with any opening / change / closing structure; condition is measured on cover-based Tier 1–2 indices against documented reference levels, not as a full multi-indicator composite — which is exactly the resolution indicators 19–21 ask for.

Three account-side formulations are contested and are carried here as evidence gaps, not as fact. (i) The count of “34 provinces dashboard-selectable” is a platform observation, not a documented compilation count; it is not used here as a maturity anchor. What can be stated is that one province (NTB) carries a documented published compilation, and that selectability is not compilation. (ii) The national 2018 opening / 2021 closing extent series is not evidenced in the primary MMAF and BPS documents held in this project; what those documents record is site-level figures, with a two-document discrepancy still open. No national opening or closing extent is asserted in this figure. (iii) The bare word “services” is split throughout, as above. Separately, and for the same reason: no BPS ocean account statistical release exists, there is no work-programme entry and no revision policy. This figure makes no positive claim about where the Indonesian programme is institutionalised, because the corpus supports only the negative half of that statement.
