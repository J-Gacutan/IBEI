# **Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI) Overview**

The Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI), developed by the Indonesian Ministry of National Development Planning (Bappenas), is a comprehensive framework used to measure, evaluate, and monitor the development of a sustainable blue economy across Indonesia's provinces. It assesses the balance of marine resource utilization for economic growth, environmental health, and social inclusivity.

## **1\. Methodology**

The index translates raw regional data into a standardized composite score between 0 and 100 through a structured statistical process:

> 1. **Data Standardization:** Because the indicators use varying units of measurement (e.g., percentages, tons, currency), raw data is normalized using Min-Max Normalization or Z-score standardization. This scales all data into uniform values between 0 and 1\.  
> 2. **Dimension Index Calculation:** Standardized indicators within each pillar (Economic, Social, Environmental) are aggregated using an arithmetic average method to generate a sub-index for that specific pillar.  
> 3. **Composite Index Aggregation:** The sub-indices of the three main pillars are synthesized using a geometric mean method to produce the final Blue Economy Index score for the region.  
> 4. **Spatial Mapping and Policy Integration:** The final scores serve as baselines for national and regional policy, helping identify regions needing targeted interventions.

## **2\. Pillars and Indicators**

The framework is structured around three primary pillars, each with specific measurable indicators.

| Pillar | Category | Specific Indicators   |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Environmental Pillar** *Focuses on ecosystem health, conservation, and ecological sustainability.* | Ecosystem Quality | Extent and health of marine ecosystems, including coral reefs, seagrass beds, and mangrove forests. |
|  | Conservation | Total extent of marine conservation areas; land area utilized for sustainable aquaculture. |
|  | Waste Management | Annual waste generation, management capacity (tons), recycling rates, and coastal village access to final waste disposal sites. |
|  | Energy & Infrastructure | Utilization of renewable energy; percentage of coastal households with access to electricity. |
| **Economic Pillar** *Measures the contribution of marine sectors to regional economic growth.* | Fisheries & Aquaculture Output | Total fish production volume (kg/tons) and economic value (IDR) across marine capture, inland capture, and aquaculture. |
|  | Industrial Capacity | Number of operating sea/inland business vessels and micro/small fish processing units. |
|  | Business Demographics | Number of households actively engaged in sea capture fisheries, inland fisheries, and aquaculture. |
|  | Tourism | Number of domestic and international tourist trips to marine and coastal destinations. |
| **Social Pillar** *Evaluates social equity, inclusivity, and welfare of coastal communities.* | Employment | Total number of fishermen/aquaculture workers, open unemployment rates, and labor force participation rates. |
|  | Poverty & Welfare | Percentage of the coastal and regional population living in poverty. |
|  | Basic Needs | Percentage of the population with access to adequate drinking water and standardized educational attainment levels. |
|  | Healthcare Access | Percentage of the population covered by health insurance programs (e.g., BPJS Health). |

## **3\. Enabler Factors**

To ensure the successful implementation of the core pillars, the index also accounts for critical underlying enablers:

> * **Governance:** The existence of integrated policy frameworks, spatial planning alignment, local conservation enforcement capacities, and cross-stakeholder collaboration.  
> * **Technology:** The adoption of digital ecosystems within coastal management, modern maritime infrastructure, and climate-smart technologies (e.g., clean energy hatcheries).
