# Ocean Accounting as a Statistical Backbone for Indonesia's Blue Economy Index: A Concept Note

---

## 1. Framing the Problem

Indonesia's blue economy governance architecture now rests on two parallel measurement initiatives that do not yet speak to each other. The **Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI)** was developed by BAPPENAS with EU support to monitor blue economy progress across all provinces, using economic, social, and environmental pillars, with governance and technology as enablers.[^1] Separately, the **Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (MMAF)** has been developing ocean accounts since 2021 as part of an international collaboration under the Global Ocean Accounts Partnership (GOAP), with the initiative now scaling nationally through ministerial decrees and a multi-ministerial task force.[^2]

The core tension: the IBEI is a composite policy-monitoring index; ocean accounts are structured statistical frameworks rooted in the System of Environmental-Economic Accounting – Ecosystem Accounting (SEEA-EA). Both aim to inform sustainable ocean development, but they operate on different logics, at different spatial scales, and through different institutional channels (BAPPENAS vs. MMAF-BPS).

This concept note argues that connecting them is not merely desirable but **necessary** — to avoid duplicative data architectures and to ground the IBEI's environmental pillar in internationally standardised biophysical evidence.

---

## 2. What the IBEI Currently Measures — and Its Gaps

The IBEI is structured around **over 35 indicators** categorised into three pillars (economy, social, environment), designed as a dashboard tool for setting pillar-specific targets and fostering inter-agency coordination.[^3] It is operationally designed for provincial-level comparison within the Blue Economy Roadmap's phased implementation (2023–2045).

Despite its breadth, the IBEI's **environmental pillar has structural measurement gaps** that ocean accounting is positioned to fill:

- It relies on aggregated outcome indicators (e.g. MPA coverage, marine debris volumes, renewable energy resource quality) but lacks a systematic **stock-flow logic**.
- It does not track **ecosystem extent change over time** as opening and closing stocks.
- It does not distinguish between **ecosystem condition** and the **flow of ecosystem services**.
- It does not link biophysical change to monetary values through the System of National Accounts (SNA).

The consequence: the IBEI can signal that environmental performance is low in a given province but **cannot explain the underlying asset dynamics** driving that signal.

## 3. What Ocean Accounts Provide

### 3.1 The SEEA-EA Architecture in Indonesia

Indonesia's ocean accounting programme applies the SEEA-EA framework through a series of pilot and national-scale efforts. The **Gili Matra pilot** — covering Gili Meno, Air, and Trawangan — compiled ecosystem extent accounts showing that between 2015 and 2021:[^4]

| Ecosystem Type | Change (ha) |
|----------------|-------------|
| Coral reef | −18.16 |
| Mangrove | −7.38 |
| Seagrass | +8.43 |

More recent methodological work in **Saleh Bay** has tested the impact of grid size and designation method for Marine Basic Spatial Units (MBSUs), advancing the spatial granularity of Indonesia's ocean accounting.[^5]

### 3.2 Core Account Outputs

The accounts produce four interconnected components:

1. **Ecosystem extent accounts** — opening and closing stock by ecosystem type
2. **Ecosystem condition accounts** — biophysical indicators of health
3. **Ecosystem service flow accounts** — provisioning, regulating, and cultural services
4. **Supply-use tables** — linking ecosystem services to economic beneficiaries

These are compiled by BPS-Statistics Indonesia, MMAF, BIG-Geospatial Agency, and the Ministry of Finance, with technical support from Rekam Nusantara Foundation, IPB University, and GOAP.

---

## 4. The Integration Argument: Three Connection Points

### 4a. Environmental Pillar Grounding

Ocean accounts can supply the IBEI's environmental indicators with **spatially explicit, time-series data on ecosystem extent and condition**. Rather than relying on point-in-time proxies (e.g. MPA coverage as a share of territorial waters), the IBEI could incorporate extent-change metrics derived from the accounts, enabling provinces to be assessed on whether their **natural capital base is appreciating or depreciating**.

### 4b. Economy–Environment Linkage

The IBEI's economic pillar (fisheries income, aquaculture output, tourism revenue) and environmental pillar are currently **measured in isolation**. Ocean accounts' supply-use tables trace the flow of ecosystem services to economic sectors, making it possible to quantify the degree to which provincial economic performance depends on ecosystem service flows.

This would allow the IBEI to flag provinces where **high economic index scores are being achieved at the cost of natural capital depletion** — a sustainability red flag invisible under the current framework.

### 4c. Governance Account Integration

GOAP's emerging **governance accounts framework** provides structured records of the rules, institutions, and management arrangements governing ocean spaces. This maps onto the IBEI's governance enabler dimension and could provide a standardised method for assessing governance quality beyond simple policy-count metrics.

---

## 5. Institutional Alignment and Feasibility

The institutional landscape is partially aligned. GOAP is co-chaired by MMAF Indonesia and brings together governments, research institutions, and international organisations.[^1] Indonesia has also partnered with Norway on long-term technical cooperation on ocean accounting, with collaboration running from **2024 to 2028**.[^2]

MMAF leads on ocean accounts; BAPPENAS leads on the IBEI; both sit within the broader architecture of the Blue Economy Roadmap. The missing piece is a **dedicated coordination mechanism**, which could take one of several forms:

- A **joint technical working group** under the National Blue Economy Action Plan (NBAAP)
- A **data-sharing MoU** between BPS and BAPPENAS
- Embedding ocean account outputs into the IBEI's **indicator refresh cycles**

The **Bogor Strategic Action Plan** (SSOA 2026 output) represents a potential multilateral lever, given its focus on scaling ocean accounts across the Global South with Indonesia as a lead country.

---

## 6. What This Is Not

This concept note does **not** propose replacing the IBEI with ocean accounts, nor subordinating one framework to the other.

The IBEI serves a distinct policy function as a composite index for cross-provincial benchmarking within the RPJMN cycle. The argument is that its **analytical power is constrained** without the structured biophysical evidence that ocean accounts are designed to produce.

The relationship is one of **statistical infrastructure feeding a policy instrument**.

---

## 7. Proposed Next Steps

1. **Technical mapping exercise** — pair each IBEI environmental indicator to the corresponding ocean account table (extent, condition, or service flow), identifying data gaps and potential substitutions.

2. **Joint BAPPENAS–MMAF–BPS workshop** — test the integration on one or two pilot provinces where ocean accounts are already compiled (e.g. West Nusa Tenggara, Sumbawa), producing a proof-of-concept dashboard.

3. **Policy brief for Phase II review** — produce a short brief for the Blue Economy Roadmap's Phase II (2025–2029) review cycle, positioning ocean accounts as part of the IBEI's forthcoming indicator refresh and recommending a formal inter-agency data protocol.
