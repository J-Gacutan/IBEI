# Annex A: Mapping Exercise -- Potential for IBEI Indicator Derivation from Ocean Accounts

## Rating Scale

| Rating | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| **Full** | The indicator can be derived directly from a compiled ocean account table with no or negligible additional processing. The ocean account produces the data in a form that is structurally compatible with the IBEI indicator specification. |
| **Partial** | The indicator overlaps substantively with ocean account outputs, but derivation requires additional processing, aggregation, or supplementary data from non-account sources. Ocean accounts provide the primary data input but not the complete indicator. |
| **Minimal** | Ocean accounts produce data that is related to the indicator domain but does not correspond to the indicator as currently specified. Accounts may inform the indicator contextually or provide a partial input among several. |
| **None** | The indicator falls outside the scope of ocean accounting as currently practised. No data transfer pathway exists. |

## Environment Pillar

| IBEI Indicator (Reconstructed) | OA Application | Relevant Ocean Account Table | Rationale |
|-------------------------------|----------------|------------------------------|-----------|
| MPA coverage (% of territorial waters) | Partial | Ecosystem Extent Accounts; Governance Accounts | Extent accounts record ecosystem area within and outside MPA boundaries. Governance accounts capture designation status. However, the IBEI indicator as a simple coverage ratio requires spatial overlay rather than direct table extraction. |
| Blue carbon ecosystem area (mangrove, seagrass) | Full | Ecosystem Extent Accounts | Extent accounts directly compile opening stock, additions, reductions, and closing stock of mangrove and seagrass area by spatial unit, as demonstrated in the Gili Matra and Saleh Bay pilots. |
| Coral reef area / extent | Full | Ecosystem Extent Accounts | Coral reef ecosystem extent is a core account table, compiled as hectares with opening/closing stock structure. |
| Habitat quality / condition index | Full | Ecosystem Condition Accounts | Condition accounts compile biophysical indicators of ecosystem health (e.g. coral cover, seagrass density, mangrove canopy integrity) in a standardised condition index framework. |
| Biodiversity indicators | Partial | Ecosystem Condition Accounts | Condition accounts include species richness and diversity metrics as condition variables. However, the specific biodiversity indicator used by the IBEI may require supplementary data (e.g. IUCN Red List assessments) not compiled within standard accounts. |
| Seawater quality index | Partial | Ecosystem Condition Accounts | Condition accounts compile abiotic condition variables (water temperature, salinity, turbidity, nutrient loading). The specific composite index used by the IBEI may weight or aggregate these differently. |
| Marine debris / waste management | Minimal | Not a core account table | Ocean accounts do not systematically compile waste flow data. Residual flow accounts exist in the broader SEEA framework but are not yet standard in ocean accounting practice. Land-based debris sources fall outside marine ecosystem accounting scope. |
| Marine renewable energy resource quality | None | Outside current scope | Ocean accounts do not assess energy resource potential. This indicator relates to physical resource endowment and technology readiness, not ecosystem assets or services. |

## Economy Pillar

| IBEI Indicator (Reconstructed) | OA Application | Relevant Ocean Account Table | Rationale |
|-------------------------------|----------------|------------------------------|-----------|
| Capture fisheries production value | Full | Ecosystem Service Supply-Use Tables; Ocean Economy Satellite Account | Supply-use tables record provisioning service flows from marine ecosystems to the fisheries sector. Ocean economy satellite accounts (SNA-aligned) compile production values by ocean-dependent industry. BPS compiles both. |
| Aquaculture production value | Partial | Ocean Economy Satellite Account | Aquaculture is recorded in ocean economy satellite accounts as an economic activity. However, aquaculture's relationship to ecosystem service flows is indirect (it depends on, but does not directly extract from, wild ecosystem services in the same way as capture fisheries). |
| Fisheries sector GDP contribution | Full | Ocean Economy Satellite Account | The ocean economy satellite account is designed to isolate ocean-dependent GDP by sector, directly producing this indicator. |
| Coastal tourism revenue | Partial | Ocean Economy Satellite Account; Ecosystem Service Accounts (cultural services) | Tourism revenue is recorded in ocean economy satellite accounts. Cultural ecosystem service accounts can attribute a portion of tourism value to ecosystem assets (e.g. reef-based tourism). However, total tourism revenue includes non-ecosystem components (accommodation, transport) not captured in service accounts. |
| Marine-based manufacturing output | Partial | Ocean Economy Satellite Account | Manufacturing output linked to marine resources (fish processing, seaweed products) is captured in ocean economy satellite accounts. However, the boundary of "marine-based manufacturing" in the IBEI may be broader than what ocean accounts classify. |
| Inter-regional trade in marine products | Minimal | Not a core account table | Ocean accounts do not compile trade flow data between provinces. This requires trade statistics from BPS customs and inter-regional trade surveys, which sit outside the SEEA-EA framework. |
| Marine renewable energy production | None | Outside current scope | Energy production statistics are compiled through energy accounts (SEEA-Energy), not ocean ecosystem accounts. No current integration pathway. |

## Social Pillar

| IBEI Indicator (Reconstructed) | OA Application | Relevant Ocean Account Table | Rationale |
|-------------------------------|----------------|------------------------------|-----------|
| Human Development Index (HDI) | None | Outside scope | HDI is a composite social indicator compiled from health, education, and income data. It is not ocean-specific and is not derived from environmental-economic accounting frameworks. |
| Gender Empowerment Index | None | Outside scope | Gender empowerment metrics are compiled from labour force and political participation data, outside the scope of ocean accounts. |
| Employment in fisheries / aquaculture | Partial | Ocean Economy Satellite Account | Ocean economy satellite accounts record employment by ocean-dependent sector. However, the IBEI may use employment definitions or data sources (e.g. MMAF administrative records) that differ from the satellite account classification. |
| Health indicators (life expectancy) | None | Outside scope | Health outcomes are not compiled within ocean accounting frameworks. |
| Marine education / ocean literacy | Partial | Social Accounts | Social accounts within the Ocean Accounts Framework compile data on ocean-related human capital, including participation in marine education and training programmes and ocean literacy indicators. Methodology is under active development, with pilot applications in the Maldives and other GOAP-supported countries. The specific composite metric used by the IBEI may aggregate education data differently. |
| Coastal community poverty rates | Partial | Social Accounts; Ocean Economy Satellite Account | Social accounts compile socio-economic condition data for ocean-dependent communities, including income distribution and livelihood indicators. Combined with household income data from ocean economy satellite accounts, these provide a substantive input to coastal poverty assessment. The specific poverty line thresholds and composite methodology used by the IBEI may require supplementary data. |

## Enablers

| IBEI Enabler Indicator (Reconstructed) | OA Application | Relevant Ocean Account Table | Rationale |
|----------------------------------------|----------------|------------------------------|-----------|
| Number of blue economy policies enacted | Minimal | Governance Accounts (emerging) | GOAP's governance accounts framework records management arrangements and institutional rules governing ocean spaces. This partially overlaps with policy enumeration but uses a different classification logic (governance effectiveness vs. policy count). Methodology is still under development. |
| Policy implementation coverage | Minimal | Governance Accounts (emerging) | As above. Governance accounts may record implementation status of management plans but do not currently produce a standardised implementation coverage metric. |
| Technology / R&D indicators | None | Outside scope | Technology readiness and R&D investment are outside the scope of environmental-economic accounting. |

## Summary Distribution

| OA Application Rating | Count | Share |
|-----------------------|-------|-------|
| Full | 5 | 22% |
| Partial | 9 | 39% |
| Minimal | 4 | 17% |
| None | 5 | 22% |
| **Total** | **23** | **100%** |

Approximately 61% of reconstructed IBEI indicators have full or partial potential for derivation from ocean account outputs. The strongest alignment is in the environment pillar, where four of eight indicators can be fully derived and a further three are partially derivable (75% full or partial). The economy pillar occupies a similar position, with ocean economy satellite accounts and supply-use tables providing a direct channel for several production and GDP indicators (71% full or partial). The social pillar, previously assumed to fall largely outside scope, benefits from the emerging Social Accounts methodology within the Ocean Accounts Framework, which brings marine education and coastal community poverty indicators into partial alignment (50% full or partial). The enablers dimension remains the weakest area of correspondence, with governance accounts still under development and technology indicators outside scope.

---

*Note: This mapping is based on publicly available descriptions of IBEI indicators (Blue Economy Roadmap, 1st and 2nd editions; ARISE+ Indonesia capacity-building documentation; MFBEI academic literature) and the SEEA-EA / Ocean Accounts Framework as applied in Indonesia's pilot accounts. Exact IBEI indicator specifications may differ from the reconstructed descriptions used here. A definitive mapping would require access to the full IBEI indicator metadata maintained by BAPPENAS.*
