## 1. Objective

Establish what would actually have to happen, institutionally, for ocean account outputs to enter the Indonesia Blue Economy Index — and assess honestly whether the agencies involved have the mandate, the mechanism, the calendar window, and the incentive to do it.

Technical feasibility and institutional feasibility are different questions. This prompt handles the second, including the possibility that the answer is no.

---

## 2. Background

Two Indonesian measurement initiatives sit in different institutional homes:

- **Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI)** — owned by **BAPPENAS** (Ministry of National Development Planning), developed with EU/ARISE+ support, operating within the Blue Economy Roadmap 2023–2045 and the RPJMN planning cycle.
- **Ocean accounts** — led by the **Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (MMAF/KKP)** with **BPS-Statistics Indonesia** as statistical authority, plus **BIG** (geospatial) and the **Ministry of Finance**, under the Global Ocean Accounts Partnership (GOAP), which MMAF co-chairs.

A prior concept note identified the missing element as a coordination mechanism, and floated three options: a joint technical working group under the National Blue Economy Action Plan (NBAAP), a BPS–BAPPENAS data-sharing MoU, and embedding account outputs in the IBEI's indicator refresh cycle. It also flagged the **Bogor Strategic Action Plan** (SSOA 2026) as a possible multilateral lever. These are untested proposals, not findings — assess whether each is real, available, and sufficient.

## 3. Research Questions

### RQ4.1 — Mandate architecture
What are the formal statutory mandates of BAPPENAS, MMAF, BPS, and BIG over, respectively: national development indicators, marine data, official statistics, and geospatial information? Where do these mandates overlap or conflict on marine environmental-economic data? Specifically: under Indonesian statistics law, does BPS hold exclusive authority over official statistics in a way that constrains how BAPPENAS may construct an index from ministry-supplied data, and does MMAF-compiled account data require BPS certification to be usable in official reporting?

### RQ4.2 — Existing coordination mechanisms
What already exists that could carry this transfer without anything new being created?
- **Satu Data Indonesia** (One Data Indonesia, Perpres 39/2019): what does it actually oblige agencies to do, what is the *Forum Satu Data* structure, what are data standard and metadata requirements, and would ocean accounts qualify as shareable data under it?
- Existing MoUs or data-sharing agreements among BAPPENAS, MMAF, BPS, BIG
- The multi-ministerial ocean accounts task force: its mandate, membership, and whether BAPPENAS sits on it
- NBAAP governance structures and their working groups
- Any existing blue economy inter-agency coordination body

### RQ4.3 — Policy calendar and entry points
When are the actual decision windows?
- Blue Economy Roadmap Phase II (2025–2029) review: is a review scheduled, who governs it, what is its timeline?
- RPJMN 2025–2029 indicator revision cycles and the annual RKP process
- Any scheduled IBEI indicator refresh
- BPS statistical work programme planning cycle
- MMAF strategic plan (*Renstra*) cycle

For each, identify the deadline by which a proposal would need to land to affect that cycle.

### RQ4.4 — Precedent for cross-agency indicator sourcing in Indonesia
Has this kind of transfer been done before domestically? Candidates: the **Indeks Kualitas Lingkungan Hidup (IKLH)** and its data supply arrangements; **IKLH** or SDG indicator reporting drawing on multi-agency sources; the Low Carbon Development Initiative; forest and land accounting feeding planning indicators; SEEA-based *neraca sumber daya alam* work and whether its outputs ever entered a planning index. What made those work or fail? Domestic precedent is more persuasive to Indonesian agencies than international precedent.

### RQ4.5 — Frictions and incentives
Assess honestly:
- Mandate protection — does either agency have an interest in retaining exclusive control of its framework?
- Budget — who would pay for expanded account compilation, and does either agency's budget line currently permit it?
- Capacity — do provincial statistical offices and marine agencies have the capability to compile or use account data?
- Attribution and credit — does an integrated product create a visibility problem for either owner?
- Donor dependency — the IBEI is EU/ARISE+ supported and accounts are Norway-supported; do differing donor cycles, reporting requirements, or preferences pull the two apart? What happens to each when its donor support ends?
- Political salience — how much political attention does the blue economy agenda currently hold, and has the 2024 administration transition changed institutional priorities, ministry structures, or the standing of the Blue Economy Roadmap?

### RQ4.6 — The multilateral lever
What is the actual status and content of the **Bogor Strategic Action Plan** and the SSOA 2026 process? Is Indonesia formally a lead country for scaling ocean accounts in the Global South, and does that role create any reporting commitment that an IBEI linkage would help satisfy? Does GOAP membership or MMAF's co-chair position create leverage, obligation, or merely visibility? Are there GOAP or UN reporting requirements that already require something close to this integration?

### RQ4.7 — Minimum viable pathway
What is the smallest institutional step that would produce a real result? Assess candidates such as: a single-province proof of concept using existing compiled accounts; a technical annex to the IBEI reporting account-derived indicators alongside rather than inside the index; a shared metadata registration under Satu Data Indonesia; a joint workshop producing an agreed indicator correspondence. For each, name the owner, the authority required, the cost order of magnitude, and what it would demonstrate.

### RQ4.8 — Failure modes
What are the realistic ways this fails after being agreed? Signed-MoU-then-nothing, pilot that never scales, integration dependent on donor funding that ends, indicator adopted then dropped at the next refresh, account data rejected by BPS on quality grounds. Which is most likely here, and what would mitigate it?

## 5. Method Requirements

1. **Cite the instrument.** Every claim about mandate or obligation must name the legal instrument or agreement, with its number and date.
2. **Existing before new.** Exhaust what current mechanisms already permit before assessing anything that would need to be created. New institutions are a last resort.
3. **Distinguish signed from operating.** An MoU that exists on paper is not a functioning data flow; report which is which.
4. **Name owners.** Every proposed step names the agency and, where discoverable, the unit or directorate that would own it.
5. **State frictions plainly.** Institutional self-interest is a legitimate finding, reported factually and without insinuation about individuals.
6. **Absence of evidence is a finding.** If no coordination mechanism can be found, that is the answer, not a gap to fill with plausible-sounding structures.

---

## 6. Required Outputs

1. **Mandate map** — agency × domain × legal basis × overlap or conflict with the others.
2. **Existing mechanisms inventory** — each candidate mechanism, what it obliges, whether it is operating, and whether it could carry ocean account data to the IBEI without amendment.
3. **Policy calendar** — decision windows through 2029 with the deadline for influencing each.
4. **Domestic precedent cases** — what was attempted, mechanism used, outcome, transferable lesson.
5. **Friction and incentive assessment** — per RQ4.5, each with evidence and an assessed severity.
6. **Multilateral lever assessment** — Bogor Strategic Action Plan and GOAP status, and what leverage or obligation each genuinely creates.
7. **Pathway options table** — option × owner × authority required × cost order of magnitude × entry point in the calendar × principal risk, ranked by feasibility rather than ambition.
8. **Minimum viable step recommendation** — one specific step, with why it is the smallest thing that produces a real result.
9. **Failure mode analysis** with mitigations.
10. **Source list** with access dates, language, and reliability note.

---

## 7. Constraints

- Work with existing mandates. Do not propose new institutions unless the research establishes that no existing mechanism could serve.
- Indonesian sovereignty over its statistical system is assumed throughout. Donor leverage is a factor to describe, not a mechanism to recommend.
- Do not assume the integration is desirable and reason backwards to a pathway. If the institutional evidence says the window is closed or the incentives are absent, report that.
- Keep political-economy analysis factual and institutional. No speculation about individual officials' motives.
