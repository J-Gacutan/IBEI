## 1. Objective

Two jobs, deliberately paired because each disciplines the other:

1. **Precedent** — find every case where a country or institution has linked natural capital, ocean, or environmental-economic accounts to a composite index or a policy monitoring instrument, and extract what worked, what failed, and what transfers to Indonesia.
2. **Counter-case** — build the strongest honest argument that Indonesia should *not* link ocean accounts to the Blue Economy Index, then assess whether it holds.

Pairing them matters: the precedent search will surface failures, and those failures are the raw material for the counter-case. A precedent review that only reports successes has not been done properly.

---

## 2. Background

Indonesia operates two parallel measurement instruments: the **Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI)**, a BAPPENAS-owned composite provincial index with 35+ indicators across economy, social, and environment pillars plus governance and technology enablers; and **ocean accounts** compiled by MMAF and BPS-Statistics Indonesia under SEEA–EA and the GOAP Ocean Accounts Framework, currently at pilot scale (Gili Matra, Saleh Bay) with national scaling underway.

A prior concept note argues the IBEI's environmental pillar is analytically weak without account-derived stock–flow data, and that accounts should serve as statistical infrastructure feeding the index. That argument has not been stress-tested against international experience or against a serious objection.

## 3. Part A — International Precedent

### RQ5.1 — Accounts feeding composite indices
Which countries or institutions have used environmental-economic, natural capital, or ocean account outputs as inputs to a composite index or scorecard? For each case: what accounts existed, which index, which specific indicators were transferred, what mechanism carried the transfer, whether it survived, and what was learned.

Candidates to check (not exhaustive — search beyond this list):
- **GOAP member countries**: Maldives, Samoa, Fiji, Tonga, Vietnam, Thailand, Philippines, Kenya, Mozambique, Canada, Australia, Portugal, Ireland
- **EU Blue Economy Report** — how are its indicators sourced, and does it draw on SEEA or marine accounts?
- **UN SEEA global assessment** and country implementation reporting — has any country documented an accounts-to-index pathway?
- **Ocean Health Index** — a globally-applied composite; what data structures does it use, and has any country substituted national account data into it?
- **Natural capital accounting to national planning**: UK (ONS natural capital accounts into policy), Netherlands, Australia (national accounts of ecosystems), Costa Rica, Rwanda, Botswana (WAVES/GPS programme countries) — did accounts change any headline indicator or index?
- **Inclusive Wealth Index**, **Adjusted Net Savings**, **Gross Ecosystem Product** (China) — cases where accounts feed a headline aggregate rather than a dashboard index
- **Blue Economy Development Index** or similar regional constructs (AU/AIMS, IOC, SPC/Pacific)

### RQ5.2 — Mechanism typology
Across the cases found, what mechanisms were used? Direct indicator substitution, satellite/annex reporting alongside the index, accounts informing indicator *selection* rather than supplying values, dashboard integration, a shared data platform, statistical office certification of ministry data. Which mechanisms proved durable and which decayed?

### RQ5.3 — Failure cases (required, not optional)
Which attempts stalled, were abandoned, or produced an integration that was quietly dropped? Why — coverage gaps, compilation lag, cost, institutional resistance, loss of index interpretability, donor withdrawal, methodological objection from the statistical office? Search deliberately for these; they are underreported relative to launches, so look in evaluations, mid-term reviews, and academic critique rather than programme communications.

### RQ5.4 — Sub-national comparability precedent
The IBEI's distinguishing feature is provincial comparison. Which cases involved **sub-national** account-derived indicators used comparatively across regions? How did they handle uneven account coverage between regions — the problem Indonesia faces directly? Did any country score some regions on account data and others on proxies, and what did that do to the index's credibility?

### RQ5.5 — Transferability to Indonesia
For each case, assess transferability against Indonesia's actual conditions: an archipelagic state with 38 provinces, extensive marine area, pilot-scale account coverage, a decentralised administration, a strong central statistical office, and donor-supported programmes on both sides. Rank cases by relevance and state what specifically transfers — mechanism, sequencing, institutional arrangement, or only cautionary lesson.

### RQ5.6 — Methodological guidance
What do the authoritative methodological sources say about this class of integration? The OECD/JRC composite indicator handbook and successors on input data requirements; SEEA guidance on the use of accounts for indicators; UN Statistical Commission positions on accounts-derived indicators in policy reporting; any GOAP guidance on connecting accounts to policy instruments. Is there established good practice, or is this genuinely unmapped territory?

## 4. Part B — The Counter-Case

### RQ5.7 — Build the strongest objection
Construct the most serious available argument *against* integrating ocean accounts into the IBEI. Develop each of these to its strongest form, with evidence, rather than listing and dismissing them:

- **Coverage insufficiency** — accounts exist at pilot scale; an index requiring all 38 provinces cannot use data covering a handful of sites, and partial adoption creates incomparability worse than the imperfect proxies it replaces
- **Lag incompatibility** — account compilation lag may exceed the index's reporting cycle, embedding stale indicators in a current-year product
- **Cost–benefit failure** — the compilation cost of national account coverage may exceed the analytical value added to a monitoring index, with better uses for the same statistical budget
- **Instrument mismatch** — a composite index exists to simplify for political communication; accounts exist to preserve analytical structure. Compressing accounts into index scores may destroy exactly what makes them valuable, serving neither purpose. The right home for account outputs may be a satellite table or dashboard, not an index
- **Compensatory aggregation defeats the purpose** — the concept note's headline promise (flagging economic gains achieved through natural capital depletion) may be structurally impossible inside an additive composite that lets pillars offset each other
- **Interpretability loss** — signed change variables, mixed stock and flow measures, and mixed data vintages may make the index harder to read and easier to game, not more informative
- **Institutional cost** — coordination effort may consume political capital better spent on either framework's own development
- **Non-overlapping decision needs** — the IBEI serves provincial benchmarking within the RPJMN cycle; accounts serve asset-management and valuation questions. These may simply be different decisions with different audiences, and forcing a linkage may be a category error
- **Sequencing objection** — even if integration is right eventually, doing it before national account coverage exists may discredit the idea through a visibly weak first attempt

### RQ5.8 — Assess whether the objection holds
Having built it honestly, evaluate it. Which strands are decisive, which are surmountable, and which are arguments about *timing* rather than *merit*? What specific evidence would settle each? Where the counter-case wins, state what should be done instead — including the possibility that account outputs belong in a companion product rather than inside the index.

### RQ5.9 — The null and alternative options
Assess two alternatives to integration on the same terms:
- **Null option** — both frameworks develop separately with no formal linkage. What is actually lost? Is it as much as the concept note implies?
- **Loose-coupling option** — accounts and index remain separate products but share metadata standards, spatial units, and a published correspondence table, without indicator substitution. Does this capture most of the benefit at a fraction of the cost and risk?

## 5. Method Requirements

1. **Failure cases are mandatory.** A precedent review returning only successes is incomplete. Search evaluations, mid-term reviews, and critical literature specifically.
2. **The counter-case must be built in good faith.** Steel-man it before assessing it. A strawman counter-case is worse than none, because it manufactures false confidence.
3. **Cite per case.** Every precedent carries a source; distinguish programme self-reporting from independent evaluation.
4. **Separate merit from timing.** Objections about sequencing are different from objections about the idea, and conflating them produces bad recommendations.
5. **No fabricated cases.** If no country has done this, that is a significant finding — report it rather than assembling loosely-analogous examples into an apparent precedent.
6. **Search multiple languages** where cases warrant it (Portuguese for Portugal/Mozambique, French for francophone GOAP members, Bahasa Indonesia for domestic commentary).

## 6. Required Outputs

1. **Precedent table** — country/institution × accounts held × index or instrument × indicators transferred × mechanism × outcome × durability × transferability rating to Indonesia × citation.
2. **Mechanism typology** — the mechanisms observed, with durability evidence for each.
3. **Failure case analysis** — what stalled, why, and the warning it carries for Indonesia.
4. **Sub-national comparability findings** — how uneven regional account coverage was handled elsewhere, and whether any approach is defensible.
5. **Methodological guidance summary** — what authoritative sources say, and where guidance is absent.
6. **The counter-case** — the strongest objection, fully developed with evidence, presented as a standalone argument a critic could sign.
7. **Assessment of the counter-case** — which strands hold, which are surmountable, which concern timing; and the evidence that would settle each.
8. **Alternatives assessment** — null option and loose-coupling option evaluated on cost, risk, and benefit against full integration.
9. **Verdict** — on the balance of precedent and objection, is this worth doing, worth doing later, worth doing differently, or not worth doing? State it plainly with the reasoning visible.
10. **Source list** with access dates, language, and a note distinguishing self-reported from independently evaluated.

## 7. Constraints

- Do not reason backwards from the concept note's conclusion. The verdict in output 9 must follow the evidence, including a verdict against integration if that is where it lands.
- Do not propose replacing the IBEI with ocean accounts or subordinating either framework — that framing boundary holds even in the counter-case.
- "No precedent found" is a legitimate and important answer. Do not manufacture analogies to fill the table.
- Keep the counter-case substantive rather than rhetorical: each objection needs evidence, not just plausibility.
