# Deep Research Prompt: The Intersection of Ocean Accounts and the Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI)

*Prepared for use with a deep-research agent (web-enabled, multi-source, citation-required). Copy Sections 1–8 into the research tool as the task brief.*

---

## 1. Research Objective

Establish, with verified primary sources, whether and how the **Indonesia Blue Economy Index (IBEI)** — developed by BAPPENAS with EU/ARISE+ support — can be structurally grounded in **ocean accounts** compiled under the SEEA–EA and Global Ocean Accounts Partnership (GOAP) Ocean Accounts Framework by MMAF, BPS-Statistics Indonesia, and partners.

The output must move the question from *plausible in principle* to *specified in detail*: which exact IBEI indicators can be sourced from which exact account tables, at what spatial and temporal resolution, through which institutional pathway, and at what cost.

**Primary deliverable:** a verified, source-cited indicator-level mapping and feasibility assessment that supersedes a provisional mapping built from reconstructed (not official) IBEI indicator definitions.

---

## 2. Essential Background (do not re-derive; verify and extend)

Two parallel Indonesian measurement initiatives currently do not interoperate:

- **IBEI** — a composite provincial-level policy-monitoring index with ~35+ indicators across economy, social, and environment pillars, plus governance and technology enablers. Operates within the Blue Economy Roadmap (2023–2045) and RPJMN cycles. Institutional owner: BAPPENAS.
- **Ocean accounts** — structured statistical frameworks (ecosystem extent, condition, service flows, supply–use tables; plus emerging ocean economy satellite, social, and governance accounts). Piloted at Gili Matra (2015–2021 extent change) and Saleh Bay (MBSU grid-size methodology). Institutional owners: MMAF and BPS, with BIG, Ministry of Finance, Rekam Nusantara Foundation, IPB University, and GOAP.

A provisional mapping of 23 reconstructed IBEI indicators rated derivability from ocean accounts as ~22% Full, ~39% Partial, ~17% Minimal, ~22% None. **That mapping's central caveat is the research target: the exact IBEI indicator metadata held by BAPPENAS was not available.** Every rating in it should be treated as a hypothesis to confirm, revise, or refute.

## 3. Research Questions

### RQ1 — IBEI specification (highest priority)
What is the **authoritative, current IBEI indicator list** with, for each indicator: precise definition, unit, formula, normalisation method, weight, data source, custodian agency, update frequency, spatial resolution, and available time series? Has the indicator set changed between the 1st and 2nd editions of the Blue Economy Roadmap, or in any post-2024 revision? Is a metadata handbook, technical annex, or dashboard published?

### RQ2 — Ocean accounts state of play
What ocean account tables have actually been **compiled and published** for Indonesia (not merely piloted or planned), for which spatial units, which years, and by which agency? What is the status of the national scaling effort — ministerial decrees, the multi-ministerial task force, the Indonesia–Norway cooperation (2024–2028), and any BPS statistical production schedule? Which accounts are experimental versus official statistics?

### RQ3 — Verified indicator-level mapping
For each confirmed IBEI indicator, what is the derivability rating (Full / Partial / Minimal / None) against actual compiled account tables? Where a rating is Partial, specify exactly what additional processing or supplementary dataset closes the gap. Explicitly test the provisional mapping's most consequential claims:
- Blue carbon extent, coral reef extent, and habitat condition rated **Full** — do compiled condition accounts exist at provincial coverage, or only for pilot sites?
- Fisheries GDP and production value rated **Full** via an ocean economy satellite account — has BPS actually published such an account, and at what disaggregation?
- Marine debris rated **Minimal** — do SEEA residual-flow or waste accounts in Indonesia change this?
- Social accounts (ocean literacy, coastal poverty) rated **Partial** — how mature is this methodology in GOAP practice, and does any Indonesian pilot exist?
- Governance enablers rated **Minimal** — what is the current published status of GOAP governance accounts methodology?

### RQ4 — Scale and comparability mismatch
Ocean accounts are compiled on **Marine Basic Spatial Units (MBSUs)**; the IBEI reports by **province**. What aggregation rules, boundary conventions, and error properties govern MBSU-to-province aggregation? How does the Saleh Bay grid-size finding affect indicator stability and cross-provincial comparability? Which provinces have sufficient account coverage to be scored on account-derived indicators without introducing incomparability between covered and uncovered provinces?

### RQ5 — Methodological compatibility
Composite indices normalise, weight, and aggregate; accounts preserve stock–flow identities and monetary–biophysical distinctions. What is lost or distorted when account outputs are compressed into index scores? Does substituting extent-*change* metrics for point-in-time coverage proxies change provincial rankings — and is there published evidence either way? What does the composite-indicator methodology literature (e.g. OECD/JRC handbook practice) say about embedding accounting-derived variables in indices?

### RQ6 — Institutional feasibility and political economy
What is the actual coordination state between BAPPENAS, MMAF, BPS, and BIG on this? Are there existing MoUs, data-sharing protocols, joint working groups, or *Satu Data Indonesia* provisions that already permit the transfer? What are the mandate, budget, and incentive frictions — including any institutional interest in keeping the frameworks separate? What is the realistic entry point in the Blue Economy Roadmap Phase II (2025–2029) review and RPJMN 2025–2029 indicator cycle? What is the status and content of the Bogor Strategic Action Plan (SSOA 2026) as a multilateral lever?

### RQ7 — International precedent
Which other countries have linked ocean or natural-capital accounts to a composite blue-economy or sustainability index? Candidates to check: Maldives, Samoa/Pacific GOAP members, Vietnam, Thailand, Portugal, Canada, Australia, Kenya, plus EU Blue Economy Report indicators and the UN SEEA global assessment. For each: what was linked, what mechanism was used, what failed, and what is transferable to Indonesia?

### RQ8 — Critical counter-case
Build the strongest available argument that the integration is **not** worth doing — e.g. account coverage too sparse, compilation lag too long for an annual index, index audience needs simplicity, cost exceeds analytical gain, or the two instruments serve non-overlapping decision needs. Then assess whether that argument holds.

- UN SEEA / seea.un.org: Indonesia country pages, Gili Matra ocean accounts report, global assessments

- *Frontiers in Marine Science* (2024) on blue economy governance and the IBEI

- Composite indicator construction and natural capital accounting integration literature

## 5. Method Requirements

1. **Verify before asserting.** Every indicator definition, account table, date, and institutional arrangement must carry a citation to a locatable source. Where a source could not be found, say so explicitly rather than inferring.
2. **Distinguish clearly** between: (a) published official statistics, (b) experimental/pilot compilations, (c) methodology under development, (d) planned or announced but not started. Tag every account table with one of these four states.
3. **Flag reconstruction.** Where an IBEI indicator definition is inferred rather than sourced from official metadata, label it explicitly and state the confidence level.
4. **Preserve disagreement.** Where sources conflict (e.g. on indicator counts, pilot extents, or agency mandates), present the conflict rather than resolving it silently.
5. **Quantify coverage.** Wherever possible, report how many of Indonesia's 38 provinces have account data available for a given table and year.
6. **No fabricated specificity.** Do not invent indicator formulas, weights, hectare figures, or decree numbers. Absence of evidence is a finding.

---

## 6. Required Outputs

1. **Executive summary** (≤800 words) — the verified answer to "can ocean accounts feed the IBEI, and how far?", with the three most consequential findings and the three largest remaining unknowns.
2. **Verified IBEI indicator register** — a table of confirmed indicators: pillar, definition, unit, source, custodian, frequency, resolution, and a sourced/reconstructed flag.
3. **Ocean accounts inventory** — a table of Indonesian account tables: type, spatial unit, coverage, years, compiling agency, publication status, and the four-state maturity tag from §5.2.
4. **Revised mapping matrix** — indicator-by-indicator derivability rating with rationale and citation, plus an explicit change log against the provisional mapping (which ratings changed, and why).
5. **Gap and dependency analysis** — what must be built for each Partial rating to become Full; sequenced by effort and by dependency.
6. **Scale reconciliation note** — MBSU-to-province aggregation rules, error properties, and comparability implications.
7. **Institutional pathway assessment** — realistic options, each with owner, entry point in the policy calendar, precedent, and principal risk.
8. **International precedent table** — country, mechanism, outcome, transferability to Indonesia.
9. **Counter-case and rebuttal** (§RQ8), stated fairly.
10. **Prioritised research agenda** — what could not be resolved from public sources and would require BAPPENAS/BPS/MMAF interviews or data access requests, ranked by decision-relevance.
11. **Full source list** with access dates, language, and a reliability note per source.

---

## 7. Constraints and Framing Boundaries

- The framing is **statistical infrastructure feeding a policy instrument**. Do not propose replacing the IBEI with ocean accounts, or subordinating either framework to the other.
- Treat the existing concept note and Annex A mapping as prior work to be **tested**, not as authority to be cited back.
- Indonesian sovereignty over its statistical system is assumed throughout; recommendations should work with existing mandates rather than propose new institutions unless no alternative exists.
- Where evidence is thin, a well-specified unknown is a more valuable output than a confident guess.

---

## 8. Success Criteria

The research succeeds if a BAPPENAS or MMAF technical officer could read the output and (a) recognise their own indicator set accurately described, (b) identify precisely which of their indicators could be sourced from an account table next cycle, and (c) see what would have to change institutionally for that to happen. It fails if it restates the in-principle case with better prose.
