## 1. Objective

Establish what ocean accounting Indonesia has **actually compiled** — table by table, spatial unit by spatial unit, year by year — as distinct from what has been piloted, planned, announced, or described as methodologically possible.

The distinction matters because downstream integration work rated several IBEI indicators as fully derivable from ocean accounts on the strength of framework capability rather than confirmed compiled output. This prompt tests whether the data exists.

---

## 2. Background

Indonesia's **Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries (MMAF/KKP)** has been developing ocean accounts since 2021 under the **Global Ocean Accounts Partnership (GOAP)**, which MMAF co-chairs. Compilation involves **BPS-Statistics Indonesia**, **BIG** (Geospatial Information Agency), and the **Ministry of Finance**, with technical support from **Rekam Nusantara Foundation**, **IPB University**, and GOAP. The framework applied is **SEEA–EA** (System of Environmental-Economic Accounting – Ecosystem Accounting), extended by the GOAP Ocean Accounts Framework.

Known reference points to verify and extend:
- **Gili Matra pilot** (Gili Meno, Air, Trawangan) — ecosystem extent accounts reporting 2015–2021 change: coral reef −18.16 ha, mangrove −7.38 ha, seagrass +8.43 ha
- **Saleh Bay** — methodological work on grid size and designation method for **Marine Basic Spatial Units (MBSUs)**
- National scaling via ministerial decrees and a multi-ministerial task force
- **Indonesia–Norway** technical cooperation, reported as running 2024–2028
- **Bogor Strategic Action Plan** as a 2026 SSOA output, positioning Indonesia as a lead country for scaling accounts across the Global South

Treat all of the above as claims to verify, including the figures.

## 3. Research Questions

### RQ2.1 — Compiled accounts inventory (core deliverable)
For every ocean or marine account compilation in Indonesia, record: account type (ecosystem extent, ecosystem condition, ecosystem service flow, supply–use / monetary, ocean economy satellite, social, governance, other), spatial unit and its definition, geographic coverage, reference years, compiling agency, whether published and where, and language.

Tag each with a maturity state:
- **(a) published official statistics** — released by BPS or a mandated agency as official output
- **(b) experimental / pilot compilation** — compiled and documented but not official statistics
- **(c) methodology under development** — framework or guidance exists, no compiled data
- **(d) announced but not started**

### RQ2.2 — Ecosystem extent accounts
Which ecosystem types are covered (coral reef, mangrove, seagrass, others)? What spatial units and classification systems? What is the underlying data source (satellite imagery, field survey, existing national mapping such as the *One Map Policy* mangrove maps)? What accuracy, minimum mapping unit, and revision practice apply? Are opening/closing stock and the additions/reductions structure fully populated, or only extent snapshots?

### RQ2.3 — Ecosystem condition accounts
Have condition accounts been compiled for Indonesia, or only specified? Which condition variables, which reference conditions, which normalisation into indices? For which sites and years? **This question is decisive for downstream mapping** — a "Full" derivability rating for habitat condition depends entirely on the answer.

### RQ2.4 — Service flow and monetary accounts
Which ecosystem services have been quantified in physical terms, and which valued monetarily? What valuation methods were used? Are supply–use tables populated with identified economic beneficiaries by sector? Are values SNA-consistent?

### RQ2.5 — Ocean economy satellite account
Has BPS compiled and published an ocean or marine economy satellite account? If so: reference years, sector classification and its correspondence to KBLI, whether provincial disaggregation exists, and whether GDP contribution by ocean-dependent sector is published. If not, what is the nearest existing output (e.g. marine and coastal statistics publications, fisheries GDP within standard national accounts)?

### RQ2.6 — Social and governance accounts
What is the published status of the GOAP **social accounts** and **governance accounts** methodologies — guidance documents, pilot applications, and any Indonesian application? Which countries have piloted them (the Maldives is a reported case — verify)? What variables do they actually compile?

### RQ2.7 — MBSU methodology
How are Marine Basic Spatial Units defined and designated in Indonesian practice? What did the Saleh Bay grid-size work conclude, and what grid resolution has been adopted or recommended? Do MBSU boundaries nest cleanly within administrative boundaries (province, district) and within marine spatial planning zones (RZWP3K / RTRWL)?

### RQ2.8 — Institutionalisation and production schedule
What ministerial decrees, regulations (Permen KP, Perpres), or task force mandates govern ocean accounts? Is ocean accounting incorporated into BPS's official statistical work programme, and if so at what periodicity? What is the content and status of the Indonesia–Norway cooperation and the Bogor Strategic Action Plan? What is the stated national scaling target and timeline?

### RQ2.9 — Compilation lag and cost
How long from reference year to published account? What are the documented resource requirements — staff, budget, satellite data, field survey — per site or per spatial unit? This determines whether accounts can feed an annually-reported index.

### RQ2.10 — Provincial coverage
Of Indonesia's 38 provinces, how many have any compiled ocean account data, for which table types and years? Produce this as an explicit coverage count, not a qualitative characterisation.

## 5. Method Requirements

1. **Maturity tag is mandatory** on every account entry. An untagged claim of existence is not a finding.
2. **Compiled ≠ compilable.** Do not record what the framework could produce. Record what has been produced, with the publication that proves it.
3. **Verify the reported figures** — including the Gili Matra hectare changes. If a figure cannot be traced to the primary report, say so.
4. **Distinguish pilot from national.** A pilot at three islands does not establish provincial coverage; state the spatial extent explicitly in every case.
5. **Quantify coverage** (RQ2.10) as a count of provinces, not a qualitative summary.
6. **Report the search.** List Tier 1 documents sought and not obtained, with what each would resolve.

---

## 6. Required Outputs

1. **Ocean accounts inventory table** — one row per compiled account, per RQ2.1, with maturity tag and citation.
2. **Account-type maturity summary** — for each of the seven account types, the current Indonesian state in one line with its evidence.
3. **Provincial coverage matrix** — province × account type × years, with a coverage count and an explicit list of provinces with no data.
4. **MBSU methodology note** — definition, designation method, adopted resolution, nesting behaviour within administrative and spatial-planning boundaries.
5. **Institutionalisation timeline** — decrees, mandates, cooperation agreements, and production-schedule commitments, dated and sourced.
6. **Lag and cost assessment** — reference-year-to-publication lag and documented resource requirements per unit compiled.
7. **Figure verification note** — which reported statistics were confirmed against primary sources, which could not be.
8. **Unobtained-documents list**, ranked by what it would resolve.
9. **Source list** with access dates, language, and reliability note.

---

## 7. Constraints

- Do **not** map to IBEI indicators here — that is Prompt 03.
- Do **not** assess whether the accounts are good accounts. Document what exists.
- Resist the pull of the framework literature. The GOAP and SEEA guidance describes an extensive account architecture; the question is which parts of it Indonesia has populated with data.

---

## 8. Success Criteria

Someone can read the inventory and state, for any given account table and province, whether Indonesian data exists — and if it does, its year, spatial unit, publication status, and maturity state. A reader should finish knowing precisely how far from national coverage the programme currently is.
